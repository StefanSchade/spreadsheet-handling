== Policy-coherence assessment

The four artifacts read as one policy system. On each cross-cutting axis they
agree, in each artifact's own voice, without contradiction, silent widening, or
silent narrowing:

* *Threat-model boundary.* The core sentence -- domain boundaries robustly
  handle malformed/unusual values reasonably implied by their documented
  pandas/NumPy/configuration/metadata/artifact contracts, and do not promise
  containment of arbitrary hostile executable Python already in-process --
  appears once in the FTR (intent), once in the ADR Decision (decision voice),
  and once in the concept lead (normative voice). This is single-sentence
  anchoring, not paragraph duplication.
* *Framework vs workflow-builder responsibility.* Stated as three balancing
  qualifications in the ADR Decision, as a dedicated "Framework and
  workflow-builder responsibility" section plus an `[IMPORTANT]` guardrail in
  the concept, and as the operational reviewer rule in the review instructions.
  Consistent across all three.
* *Validation robustness vs actual isolation.* "Validation robustness is not
  process isolation" (ADR) and "Actual isolation, not local validation" (concept
  level 4) and "do not require containment of arbitrary hostile in-process
  Python" (review instructions) are mutually consistent.
* *Cheap/local hardening.* ADR qualification 1 and the concept principles
  ("Validate at ingress", "Normalize supported carrier types") keep cheap
  simplifying hardening in scope and encouraged; the boundary is explicitly not
  a licence to under-harden.
* *Readability limit.* ADR rationale ("Why readability limits hardening"),
  concept principle ("Readability is a contract quality"), and the Developer
  Guide leading `[NOTE]` all say the same thing: hardening stays behind a small,
  well-named boundary helper and must not obscure the domain flow.
* *Semantic DRY.* Concept "Semantic DRY" criteria, Developer Guide pattern 6,
  and the review-instructions bullet ("Do not demand shared helpers without
  shared semantics") agree.
* *Classification of adversarial findings.* The concept "How reviews classify
  findings" list (immutability, deterministic ordering, exact identity,
  deliberate diagnostics, failure atomicity, no mutation of the documented
  caller graph) is identical to the FTR review policy and to the
  review-instructions "Report concrete contract violations even when found
  adversarially" bullet.

Normative operational rules live in the concept (the designated owner), not only
in explanatory artifacts. The review instructions carry the concise reviewer
*procedure* and point back to the ADR and concept; this is appropriate
reviewer-facing ownership, not harmful duplication of the normative rules.

== Threat-model and responsibility assessment

*Threat-model precision.* "Reasonably implied by the documented contract" is
inherently a judgement phrase, but it is operationalized well enough to guide
future acceptance decisions: the four-level boundary taxonomy fixes what the
framework may assume (level 1) versus what it must not pretend to contain (level
4); the normative principles convert the phrase into concrete obligations
(validate at ingress, normalize carriers, establish ownership, safe diagnostics,
scope atomicity); and the classification list names the explicit guarantees that
must survive adversarial probing. The policy:

* requires robust handling of malformed/unusual values covered by a capability
  contract (level 2 "must validate");
* preserves the explicit guarantees -- immutability, deterministic ordering,
  exact identity, deliberate diagnostics, failure atomicity, and caller-graph
  non-mutation -- as reportable correctness properties even under adversarial
  probes;
* does not require containment of arbitrary hostile in-process Python (level 4,
  explicit non-goal; ADR non-goals);
* does not become a licence to dismiss cheap simplifying hardening (ADR
  qualification 1; concept principles).

The residual judgement in "reasonably implied" is adequately bounded by the
taxonomy and the guarantee list; the ambiguity that remains would not materially
change a future acceptance decision, so it is not recorded as an important
finding.

*Responsibility balance.* Workflow-builder responsibility cannot excuse
ambiguous behaviour, silent coercion, hidden mutation, weak diagnostics,
undocumented assumptions, or inconsistent contract interpretation: this is stated
directly in ADR qualification 2 and reinforced by the concept's `[IMPORTANT]`
admonition. Symmetrically, framework responsibility does not expand into
application-specific trust decisions or process isolation: ADR qualification 3,
concept level 3 ("trust, bootstrap, and promotion are application-level
decisions the workflow builder owns"), and the ADR non-goals. Neither side
excuses the other.

*Faithfulness to the motivating evidence.* Axis Mapping acceptance review 002
rejected its slice by escalating to metaclass-controlled `__name__` diagnostics,
an incomplete exact-NumPy-integer vocabulary, and a retained caller-owned tuple
subclass; review 003 accepted and explicitly deferred the repo-wide question to
"the planned repository-wide Domain Boundary Robustness clarification". The new
policy targets exactly that escalation pattern. Crucially, review 002 also
produced *genuine* correctness findings via adversarial probes (an
effective-immutability break through a retained mutable-hash tuple; a real
incomplete-vocabulary defect). The new policy preserves these as reportable --
immutability, exact identity, and deliberate diagnostics are all in the "still
reportable" list -- so it cannot be read as suppressing the very findings that
motivated it.

== Pattern-catalogue assessment

All six patterns were inspected. They are valid, idiomatic, and consistent with
the normative concept:

. *Exact built-in value boundary* -- correct; short-circuits before calling
  `.strip()` on a non-str; the leading `[NOTE]` makes clear this is exceptional,
  not a default Python rule.
. *Normalize at ingress* -- correct behaviour; see MIN-1 (a redundant `bool`
  guard).
. *Defensive ownership copy* -- `tuple(labels)` produces an exact built-in tuple
  the caller cannot later mutate or subclass. The comment does not overpromise:
  it claims container-ownership safety only, not immunity to hostile iterators,
  consistent with the concept's explicitly scoped atomicity promise. The review
  question about `frozen=True` protecting a mutable member is apt.
. *Safe diagnostics* -- interpolates only trusted `field`/`row`, never the
  rejected value's `repr`/type; remains actionable.
. *Validate then commit* -- correctly scopes atomicity to build+validate before
  commit; the review question asks the author to document the atomicity scope.
. *Narrow semantic helper* -- correctly keeps physical-label validity and
  configured-field-reference identity as separate contracts even though both
  touch strings; matches the real `domain/tabular/physical_labels.py` split and
  the "no second consumer" conclusion (`_SUPPORTED_EXACT_NUMPY_INTEGER_TYPES`
  appears only in the resolver).

Exact-type checks are clearly framed as exceptional (the leading `[NOTE]` and
each pattern's "Do not use when"). The examples do not create broader repository
contracts. A competent developer without metaclass expertise can follow the
material; hostile behaviour is described by category, not by enumerating special
methods.

=== Minor

MIN-1 -- redundant `bool` guard in the "Normalize at ingress" example::
`docs/developer_guide/ch06_code_conventions/02_domain_boundary_patterns.adoc`
pattern 2 writes `if type(value) is int and type(value) is not bool:`. Because
the check is exact identity (`type(value) is int`) and `type(True)` is `bool`,
not `int`, a `bool` can never satisfy the first clause; the `and type(value) is
not bool` term is always true when reached and therefore dead. *Risk:* a novice
may infer that `type(x) is int` can match a `bool` (it cannot) and carry the
redundant idiom elsewhere; the guard is only needed with `isinstance`. *Evidence:*
`type(True) is int` evaluates to `False`. *Recommended action (non-blocking):*
drop the `bool` clause, or add a one-line comment noting the guard is retained
only to contrast with the `isinstance` idiom. *Disposition:* record; harmless to
behaviour; safe to defer.
