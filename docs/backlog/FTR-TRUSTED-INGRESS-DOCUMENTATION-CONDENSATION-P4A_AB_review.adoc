== 2. Semantic Model review

=== A1--A8

*A1--A8 are materially represented and correctly owned.* The promoted
subsection states the positive six-category ordinary Scalar language
(String, Boolean, Number, Missing, Date, DateTime), zero ordinary composites
today, and the explicit-only extension rule. It distinguishes semantic
category from carrier representation, preserves Missing as recognition and
the visible spreadsheet string substrate, and keeps framework-authored
`FormulaSpec`/Intent outside the ordinary Scalar grammar.

The downstream assumption is useful but bounded: successful ingress lets
ordinary Domain consumers rely on admitted categories, while the adjacent
necessary-not-sufficient statement preserves family/business/local
preconditions. A7 uses *enables* and *provides a basis for* and expressly
disclaims proof; it claims neither formal algebraic closure nor
transformation correctness.

=== Semantic authority boundary

The entry-point exception names only chapter 06's "Domain scalar language"
subsection as accepted current authority. It expressly leaves the remainder
of chapter 06 and the other Semantic Model scaffold in draft status.

The primary-owner split is coherent: the Semantic Model owns category meaning
and the positive Domain language; the Internal Scalar Value Model owns
carrier variants and classifier mechanics. There is no competing normative
owner for category meaning. A stale normalization-ownership passage remains
inside the latter document, but it does not create a competing owner for the
six categories; it is addressed as Important I-4.

== 3. Ingress, binding, and controlled-state review

=== B1--B6

B1--B3, B5, and B6 are accurate and intelligible. A reader can locate
initial establishment at framework-managed entry points, understand complete
ordinary-cell admission and bounded diagnostics, distinguish `run_pipeline`
as a caller-precondition surface, and see why ingress conformance does not
replace local semantic validity. Re-establishment is explicitly about the
returned candidate; the prose correctly says it proves nothing about side
effects already performed by an uncertified callable.

B4 needs correction. The promoted text correctly makes `_meta` an owned,
bounded, acyclic substrate and correctly says authoring delegates are closed
and statically governed. It then overclaims that every new metadata root
requires a reviewed delegate before ingress admits it. Current and accepted
E3 authority admits the ordinary exact-`dict`/exact-`list`/Scalar substrate
under exact-string keys; a delegate is required for a root-specific
*authoring normalization/exception*, not for every otherwise-conforming new
root. See Important I-1.

=== C1--C11 and executable binding

C1--C10 are materially present in the permanent Technical Model. It explains
that certification/classification and execution derive from one authoritative
deeply immutable effective binding snapshot, enforced at the sole ordinary
construction path, so caller-held container aliases cannot alter certified
execution semantics. It also correctly makes visible config, callable/class
identity, registry membership, framework authorship, provenance, and a
certificate-looking object insufficient by themselves. The unsupported term
`Certified Invocation Stability` was not promoted.

The current semantic role set is counted correctly as exactly three:
`LookupFormulaSpec`, `GroupedMatrix`, and
`ArtifactManifestSourceFrames`. The implementation's
`GroupedMatrixFormulaRole` descriptor is correctly treated as the one
admitted nested composition, not a fourth semantic role. Ordinary state,
controlled state, exact introduction, disjoint locality/non-interference,
the sole nested composition, and the absence of a general role algebra are
all clear. The uncertified-return rule correctly ends prior role authority by
rule, rejects identity/provenance preservation, performs ordinary
re-establishment in managed flow, and disclaims validation or rollback of
prior side effects.

C11 is not accurate enough to serve as durable authority. The `GroupedMatrix`
exit bullet uses the exact term "consumed" for selection and transparent
cleanup, then says `expand_grouped_xref` terminates the role without
distinguishing retain mode from drop mode. Accepted authority instead has
carry/transparent transitions, projection consumption, retain-mode expansion,
and drop-mode termination as distinct outcomes. The prose also describes only
the *nested* `LookupFormulaSpec` relocation/adapter exit and omits the
standalone helper-role carry/cleanup/adapter path. The adjacent pending-cleanup
rule is correct, but does not repair those omissions. See Important I-2.

The corrected `bound_configuration.py` docstring removes the superseded
shallow-freeze/nested-mutation claim and correctly names `__post_init__` as
the mandatory deep-freeze hook. Two current-mechanism sentences nevertheless
remain inaccurate: classifiers do not read the same mapping object passed to
the target--`__call__` derives a fresh invocation-local thaw from the frozen
snapshot--and arbitrary non-`dict`/`list`/`tuple` objects are retained by
reference, although E4 classifiers correctly reject such shapes. The
certificate invariant is sound; its source explanation needs the bounded
qualification in Important I-5.

== 4. Description, resource, and layering review

=== D1--D6

*D1--D6 are materially represented and accurate.* The prose distinguishes
managed and caller-precondition surfaces; makes authorization positive,
absence-denying, and fail-closed for an unclassified future registry entry;
and places authorization before import, acquisition, factory resolution,
binding, and resource activation. It distinguishes registered short-name
authority from trusted internal wiring and requires exact canonical plugin
identity.

An authorized plugin is correctly described as trusted same-process Python
under its complete current argument contract, not a Python sandbox. Direct
Python callable targets and direct `BoundStep` composition remain trusted
programmatic composition. This agrees with the accepted Dotted-Extension
assessment/follow-up and E6/E7 surface authority rather than relying on the
obsolete concept sketch.

=== E-1--E-4

E-1, E-2, and E-4 are accurate. The resource-sensitive census is exactly
`apply_overrides`, `write_structured_yaml`,
`write_key_value_resources`, and `write_artifact_manifest`, and is explicitly
a current reviewed census rather than an eternal enumeration. Registry
membership grants no resource authority; external `overrides_path` is
forbidden before activation; checksum reads require the conjunctive
`allow_checksum_reads` permission; and the prose disclaims a generic
filesystem sandbox and hostile concurrent-symlink-race guarantee.

E-3 is incomplete. The exact-root paragraph names only
`write_structured_yaml` and `write_key_value_resources`. Current accepted
authority applies `_authorize_output_root` to `write_artifact_manifest` output
mode too (with an optional relative/contained manifest path), before its
separate checksum-read subpermission. Calling that permission "on top of
manifest write authority" does not state what the write authority is. A
reader must inspect source or history to recover the missing manifest exact-
root contract. See Important I-3.

=== F1--F3

*F1--F3 are materially represented and accurate.* The builder owns
authorization/resolution/binding; the orchestrator executes already-bound
steps and did not become a description interpreter or workflow engine;
`run_app` applies description authorization only to `AppConfig.pipeline`;
and `AppConfig.io` remains trusted-operator-owned outside E7.

== 5. Threat model and explanatory mappings

=== G1--G4

*G1--G4 are materially represented and do not widen accepted guarantees.*
The trusted operator/runtime precondition remains explicit; payload and
description trust are independent; sink capability remains separately owned;
and payload provenance never selects description authority. The section
expressly disclaims a Python sandbox, hostile-interpreter model, generic
network/subprocess policy, generic filesystem sandbox, and general hostile
symlink-race guarantee.

=== G5

*G5 ACCEPTABLE.* It clearly treats authorized arbitrary same-process Python
as inside the process-level security trust base and disclaims process
isolation. It separately says security trust is not a correctness or
bug-freedom assumption. Its three examples are supported: ordinary
re-establishment checks an uncertified return, immutable certificate-relevant
binding state prevents accidental alias mutation, and ingress classification
rejects malformed output. The qualification "where technically enforceable"
and the explicit exclusions for `os.remove`, network/filesystem activity,
monkeypatching, `ctypes`, interpreter compromise, and arbitrary hostile side
effects prevent those robustness checks from becoming a containment claim.

=== Parse-Don't-Validate and LangSec

The Parse-Don't-Validate passage is explicitly an analogy, not formal type
parsing, proof, or required repository terminology. LangSec is explicitly
non-normative and external; Shotgun Parsing is limited to centralized
classification/admission; Overloaded Fields is absent; and no grammar-class,
DCF, or context-free result is claimed.

The `Differing interpretations` row remains a weak and misleading mapping.
Fixed scalar classification order does not decide whether the same object is
role-authorized--that depends intentionally on the current exact transition
state--and registered short-name authority is deliberately distinct from the
plugin canonical-target identity rule. Because the table is non-normative and
the normative sections are correct, this is Minor M-1; deleting the row is
preferable to defending a broader analogy.

== 8. Findings

=== Important I-1 -- `_meta` delegate governance is overstated

`architectural_layers.adoc` says a new metadata root requires its own
reviewed delegate before ingress admits it. Accepted/current E3 admits
ordinary exact-string-keyed substrate nodes generically; only a root-specific
authoring normalization/exception requires a closed reviewed delegate.
Correct that one sentence without weakening the common recursive post-check.

=== Important I-2 -- C11 collapses distinct `GroupedMatrix` exits and omits the standalone formula exit

The controlled-state exit summary uses consume/transparent/terminate
semantics interchangeably and presents `expand_grouped_xref` as termination
without the retain/drop distinction. It also gives only the nested
`LookupFormulaSpec` relocation path, not the standalone helper-role
carry/cleanup/adapter path. Replace the bullet with the exact bounded exit
cases; keep the already-correct pending-cleanup and generic-sink rules.

=== Important I-3 -- Manifest exact-root write authority is absent

The resource section limits its exact-root statement to the two ordinary
writers, although accepted/current E7 also applies exact-root authority to
`write_artifact_manifest` output mode before its separate checksum-read
permission. Add the manifest output-mode rule (including the optional
relative/contained manifest path) without claiming a generic filesystem
sandbox.

=== Important I-4 -- False current Phase-E statements remain in permanent/source prose

Correct the remaining current-looking claims in:

* `internal_value_model.adoc` (general normalization/type resolution still
  assigned to Phase E and future work still called Phase-E-scoped);
* the ch04 historical concept (notably the later-update/adoption/open-question
  passages that still call ingress enforcement/mechanism future or pending);
  and
* `core/scalar_values.py` (ingress policy and especially general normalization
  still described as a future Phase-E-owned stage).

Preserve the historical reasoning and prominent banner; qualify the stale
statements in place. No runtime change is implied.

=== Important I-5 -- The binding source docstring misstates how the frozen snapshot executes

`bound_configuration.py` says classifiers read the same mapping passed to the
target, but `BoundFramesTargetCall.__call__` materializes fresh invocation-
local dict/list containers from the authoritative frozen snapshot. It also
states alias immutability without noting that arbitrary other objects are
retained by reference and become `Uncertified` when E4-relevant. Describe
classification and execution as consuming the same authoritative snapshot,
and bound the no-alias claim to certificate-relevant closed shapes.

=== Minor M-1 -- The LangSec `Differing interpretations` row maps unlike mechanisms

The row incorrectly attributes role authorization to scalar classification
order and blurs registered short-name authorization with canonical plugin
identity. It is non-normative and does not override the correct normative
sections. Delete it, or narrow it to one exact mechanism that genuinely has
the claimed interpretation property.
