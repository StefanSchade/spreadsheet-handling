== 1. Purpose and method

A subsequent architecture discussion raised a potentially load-bearing omission
in the prior encapsulation assessment: that it evaluated `contract_xref`,
`join_frames`, `project_by_role`, and `expand_xref` mainly as independent
primitives and may not have evaluated the *complete forward/inverse view
projection as one semantic unit* -- in particular that the outbound display-helper
*addition* and the inbound display-helper *removal* are owned by different steps,
which was hypothesised to violate a framework projection pattern in which the
complete semantic forward/inverse pair owns reconstruction of the canonical
payload even when its elementary steps are not individually invertible.

This review independently re-derives the evidence, tests the hypothesis against
the repository's own documented ontology, and decides whether Confirmation 001
is upheld, requires correction, or must be superseded.

The bar applied is the one the correction task itself sets: *"requires
correction" only for a load-bearing contract or responsibility error, not for an
aesthetically possible alternative design.*

== 2. Governing ontology actually documented in the repository

The framework already documents the "semantic operation vs. elementary technical
transformation" distinction the discussion reaches for; it does not need new
terminology.

* `docs/technical_model/ch08_architecture_details/pipeline_semantics.adoc` --
  *Step Categories*: `primitive` / `configuration` / `composite` / `plugin` /
  `infrastructure`; a `composite` is "a convenience wrapper over a documented
  sequence of lower-level operations" that "must not become a hidden second
  implementation" and "must not absorb consumer-specific business rules".
* Same document -- *Equality and Bijectivity*: the registry classifies each
  step's inverse behaviour as `bijective` / `conditionally_bijective` /
  `relation_preserving` / `projection_only` / `validation_only` / `extraction` /
  `unknown`. This is the framework's own statement that "a transformation can be
  relation-preserving even when the concrete representation changes" and that
  reconstruction ownership is expressed through the inverse-kind field, *not*
  through step adjacency.

The relevant registry facts (`registries/pipeline_step_registry.json`) read
directly:

[cols="2,1,2,4"]
|===
| Step | Category | inverse.kind / inverse_step | Meaning for this question

| `contract_xref` / `expand_xref`
| primitive
| `conditionally_bijective` / each other
| *This* complete forward/inverse pair owns reconstruction of the canonical relation.

| `expand_compact_multiaxis`
| composite
| `conditionally_bijective` / `contract_compact_multiaxis`
| Precedent composite that owns a roundtrip across non-invertible internals (wraps `expand_xref` + codec).

| `join_frames`
| primitive
| `projection_only` / `None`
| Registry reason: "Join views are materialized projections for display or filtering and do not own canonical recomposition semantics."

| `project_by_role`
| primitive
| `conditionally_bijective` / `project_by_role` (self-inverse)
| Registry reason: "Outbound followed by inbound restores the retained column set but not the original column order; inbound drops display_helper by design."

| `add_fk_helpers`
| primitive
| `projection_only` / `remove_fk_helpers`
| The framework's own read-only display-helper family splits *creation* and *removal* across two different registered steps -- and blesses that split through the inverse ontology.
|===

This table is the crux. Two consequences follow immediately and are developed
below:

. Reconstruction of the *canonical payload* is already owned by a complete
  forward/inverse pair (`contract_xref` / `expand_xref`, and the
  `expand_compact_multiaxis` composite for the one compact surface).
. "Display-helper addition and removal owned by different steps" is the framework
  *norm*, not a violation: `add_fk_helpers` (create) and `remove_fk_helpers`
  (remove) are exactly such a split, registered as an inverse pair.

== 3. Historical decision context (Phase 1) -- MIXED, and the load-bearing fact

Sources: `docs/cold_storage/backlog/ftrs_done/FTR-DYNAMIC-FRAME-PROJECTION-P5_decision.adoc`,
`FTR-DYNAMIC-FRAME-PROJECTION-IMPL-P5*.adoc`,
`FTR-PROJECTED-FRAME-COLUMN-SEMANTICS-P5.adoc`, and the retained pre-migration
plugin `worldbuilding/plugins/story_cast_views.py`.

* The decision deliberately chose *Option C* (consume the foundation role
  taxonomy) plus a *minimal Option-B* surface (one step `project_by_role` with
  `direction`), and honoured the foundation FTR's explicit *Future Design
  Pressure*: *"Composed, not centralized -- `project_by_role` is one consumer; it
  does not take over role authority from `contract_xref`,
  `configure_workbook_view`, or FK-helper configuration."* That is a deliberate,
  recorded preference for composition over a centralising composite in exactly
  this area.
* *Load-bearing historical fact:* the pre-migration plugins were already pure
  reorder/drop. `build_story_cast_matrix_view` reorders
  `[helpers, keys, matrix]` and sorts; `build_story_cast_matrix_payload` drops
  helpers and keeps `[keys, matrix]`. *Neither owned the `title` join* -- each
  took a `source` frame in which `title` was already present. The `title` join
  was a *separate* step before migration and remained a separate `join_frames`
  after migration. The migration was therefore a faithful 1:1 replacement of two
  reorder/drop plugins by one core step; *it did not split a previously unified
  semantic unit.*
* What the historical record *did not* evaluate: whether the *complete*
  forward/inverse view projection (helper materialisation + reorder / helper
  removal + relational inverse) should be one owned composite. That boundary was
  inherited from the plugin composition and never questioned.

*Classification: MIXED.* The role-resolution + reorder/drop step boundary was
deliberate and remains sound. The complete-view-projection/helper-lifecycle
boundary was an unexamined inheritance -- which is the analytical gap the
correction discussion correctly identifies. Supplying that missing analysis is
the substance of §§4-8; it *confirms* rather than overturns Option A.

== 4. Complete semantic forward/inverse traces (Phase 2)

Reconstructed from the ten actual call sites
(`worldbuilding/pipelines/dino_insel/json_to_spreadsheet.yaml`,
`spreadsheet_to_json.yaml`). `title` is `stories.title` joined on
`story_id -> stories.id`.

.Diagram 1 -- ordinary XRef surface (4 cast surfaces: role / notes / character_development / crystal)
[source,text]
----
FORWARD (json -> spreadsheet)
  story_cast_<f>_named            [story_id, character_name, value]        (canonical relation)
    -> contract_xref             [story_id, <character names...>]         (canonical matrix)   <-- owns forward payload
    -> join_frames (+ title)     [story_id, <names...>, title]            (view-enriched; projection_only)
    -> project_by_role outbound  [title, story_id, <names...>]            (workbook matrix view; reorder only)

INVERSE (spreadsheet -> json)
  <sheet> -> story_cast_<f>_matrix_view  [title, story_id, <names...>]
    -> project_by_role inbound   [story_id, <names...>]                   (drops display_helper title; reorder)
    -> expand_xref               [story_id, character_name, value]        (canonical relation)  <-- owns inverse payload
----

.Diagram 2 -- compact-multiaxis surface (1 group surface: story_group)
[source,text]
----
FORWARD
  story_group_named              [story_id, group_name, code]             (canonical relation)
    -> contract_xref             [story_id, <group names...>]             (canonical matrix)
    -> join_frames (+ title)     [story_id, <groups...>, title]
    -> project_by_role outbound  [title, story_id, <groups...>]           (workbook matrix view)

INVERSE
  <sheet> -> story_group_matrix_view  [title, story_id, <groups...>]
    -> project_by_role inbound   [story_id, <groups...>]                  (drops title)
    -> expand_compact_multiaxis  [story_id, group_name, code]             (composite: expand_xref + cell codec)
----

.Diagram 3 -- the common matrix-view sub-operation (the shape shared by all ten sites)
[source,text]
----
FORWARD view adaptation:   canonical matrix  --(add read-only display helper)-->  --(order as workbook view)-->  workbook matrix view
INVERSE view adaptation:   workbook matrix view  --(remove display helper)-->  --(order as canonical matrix)-->  canonical matrix
----

Diagram 3 is real -- a common sub-operation *does* exist. The decisive question
(§6) is whether it is worth minting as an owned composite, given that its
*forward* leg (the `title` join) is consumer-specific configuration and its
canonical reconstruction is already owned elsewhere.

=== Projection-contract answers

. *Is the canonical payload reconstructed exactly?* Yes -- and it is owned by a
  complete forward/inverse pair already: `contract_xref` / `expand_xref`
  (`conditionally_bijective`), or `expand_compact_multiaxis` for the group
  surface. Empirically confirmed by the prior `make diff-reimport` smoke result
  ("Reimport matches canonical data after normalization").
. *Helper authority:* `title` is *non-authoritative, read-only display*. Its
  authoritative source is `stories.title` (canonical in the `stories` frame,
  never lost). It is re-derived on every outbound run and never read back inbound.
. *Edited helper values:* silently *ignored by removal*. `project_by_role`
  inbound drops the whole `display_helper` role before the relational inverse, so
  an edited `title` cell is discarded, not validated and not melted. There is no
  writeback and no drift check today.
. *Which operation removes them:* `project_by_role(direction=inbound)`
  (`_inbound_columns` computes `drop = set(roles.display_helper)`).
. *Is helper removal explicitly connected to helper creation?* Not within a
  single owner -- creation is `join_frames`, removal is `project_by_role`. This
  is the split the discussion flags, and §5 shows it matches the framework's
  established read-only-helper pattern.
. *Does the API make the relationship visible?* Only through `role`/`direction`
  semantics and the registry's `project_by_role` self-inverse reason, not through
  a single call. The linkage is required pipeline-author knowledge.
. *Is the complete flow semantically a projection pair despite non-invertible
  elementary steps?* Yes -- but the pair that carries the *canonical* guarantee
  is `contract_xref`/`expand_xref` (+ the compact composite). The `title`
  decoration is orthogonal to that pair and is correctly modelled as
  `projection_only`. There is no canonical payload that *spans* `title`.
. *Where is the reconstruction guarantee tested?* Core
  `tests/integration/pipeline/test_project_by_role_roundtrip.py` and
  `test_project_by_role_migration_parity.py`, plus the worldbuilding
  `make diff-reimport` path. *Gap:* the Core roundtrip test exercises only
  `project_by_role` outbound->inbound (reorder + drop); no single Core test
  exercises the *complete* `join -> project(outbound) ... project(inbound) ->
  expand` chain or asserts melt-avoidance of the helper. Recorded as a watch-item
  (§8), not a contract defect.

== 5. Helper relationship (Phase 3): `title` vs. the FK-helper family

`title` resembles an FK/display helper in shape: a local key (`story_id`), a
target frame (`stories`), a materialised target field (`title`), a
non-authoritative repeated display value removed on inbound. It differs
materially: it is *not declared as an FK relation*, carries *no `derived`
provenance* (it is added by generic `join_frames`, whose provenance is `None`),
and is not restricted to an identification-helper allowlist.

The framework *does* have a provenance-writing read-only-helper primitive --
`enrich_lookup` (registry `add_lookup_helpers`) writes
`_meta.derived.sheets.<out>.enrich_lookup`, and `add_fk_helpers` writes
`_meta.derived.sheets.<frame>.helper_columns`, which the shared resolver
auto-detects (`_fk_helper_provenance_columns`). Worldbuilding deliberately uses
*generic* `join_frames` for `title` instead. That single choice explains every
"friction" symptom: because `join_frames` writes no provenance, the resolver
cannot auto-classify `title`, so each site passes explicit `helper_columns:
[title]`, and no derived-column policy owns removal, so `project_by_role(inbound)`
does the drop. This is a *worldbuilding pipeline-authoring choice*, explicitly
out of scope for this correction (no worldbuilding runtime change), not a Core
architecture defect.

*Future editable-helper boundary (recorded as non-goal).* The preferred
conceptual separation holds and the current architecture supports it: an *FK/read-only
display helper* (identification assistance, target-metadata-restricted eligible
fields, read-only lifecycle, inbound drop/validate) is a distinct feature family
from an *editable lookup/reference projection* (arbitrary editable field,
conflict detection, controlled writeback). Nothing here should introduce
`writeback: true` on FK helpers, a universal derived-column policy, editable-helper
reconciliation, or cross-view conflict resolution. `title` is firmly in the
read-only family.

== 6. Decomposition, generic-reorder, and wrapper re-evaluation (Phases 4-6)

*Decomposition of `project_by_role`.* Three separable responsibilities, at their
correct abstraction levels:

* *Shared role resolution* -- `domain/column_roles/resolver.py`. Confirmed second
  consumer: `domain/validations/validate_columns.py` (validation role targeting),
  guarded by `tests/architecture/dependency_guards/test_validation_role_targeting_uses_shared_resolver.py`.
  Must remain shared. Not XRef-specific.
* *Direction-specific role ordering + inbound helper removal* -- currently the
  body of `project_by_role` (`_outbound_columns` / `_inbound_columns`). This is
  workbook-view adaptation over a matrix, not canonical relation logic.
* *Mechanical reorder* -- `_reorder_dataframe` = `df.loc[:, ordered].copy()`; a
  strict permutation/selection of an already-computed column list.

*Generic reorder operation (Phase 5): none warranted.* The mechanical step is a
one-line private helper with a single consumer. R2 (a public registered
`reorder_columns` step) fails the repository's own evidence threshold -- "a public
elementary step requires concrete independent consumers"; there is one. R3 (reuse
`extract_frame`) is wrong: `extract_frame` is `extraction`
(`inverse_step: None`, "intentionally discards information"), a different
semantic than an order-preserving permutation. The smallest evidence-supported
form is the status quo (R4/R1): keep the direct `df.loc[:, ordered].copy()` inside
the role helper. If it were ever extracted for clarity it should stay a *private*
helper with a strict contract (every current column exactly once; no unknown /
duplicate / dropped / added column; no sort; no coercion; no metadata mutation;
copy semantics) so a reorder never silently becomes a select/extract.

*Wrapper/composite options (Phase 6).* Re-evaluated with the full
forward/inverse view in hand:

* *Option A (retain current public composition) -- reaffirmed.* Each step names a
  real, correctly classified operation; the canonical-vs-view boundary stays
  visible; the canonical guarantee is owned by `contract_xref`/`expand_xref`.
* *Options B/C/D (normal-XRef composite, matrix-view composite pair, or two thin
  wrappers)* still fail on load-bearing grounds, now stated in ontology terms.
  A composite's forward leg would have to own the `title` join, which is
  *consumer-specific configuration* (`stories`, `story_id`, `title`).
  `pipeline_semantics.adoc` forbids exactly this: a composite "must not absorb
  consumer-specific business rules" and genericisation must be "driven by
  recurring plugin patterns, not by theoretical purity." There is one consumer
  and no second reproduction of the pattern. A composite that instead *required*
  the caller to configure the join reduces no consumer knowledge (the prior
  Option-C rejection, still valid). And no single symmetric pair spans all five
  surfaces -- four invert via `expand_xref`, one via `expand_compact_multiaxis`.
* *Option E (compatibility wrapper) / F (split helper lifecycle from ordering)*
  are solutions to a defect that does not exist; they add indirection to a
  ten-site declarative pattern without a contract driver.

== 7. Correction verdict on the three prior artifacts

*Findings that remain valid* (verified against source this pass):

* Outbound `project_by_role` is separated from `contract_xref` by a `join_frames`
  that adds `title`; the projection adapts a view-enriched matrix. (True.)
* The canonical XRef model has no display-helper slot: `contract_xref`
  `drop_source` rejects any field beyond `row_keys + column_key + value`;
  `expand_xref` melts every non-key column by default. (True; verified in
  `operation.py`.)
* `expand_xref` is a protected foundational primitive wrapped by
  `expand_compact_multiaxis` and grouped-XRef. (True.)
* No single symmetric composite fits all five surfaces (4x `expand_xref`, 1x
  `expand_compact_multiaxis`). (True.)
* The shared resolver has a second live consumer (validation role targeting), so
  it stays shared regardless. (True; guarded by an architecture test.)

*Conclusions that were incomplete* (analysis gap, not error):

* The assessment and confirmation evaluated the four steps largely as independent
  primitives and did not explicitly ask *who owns the display-helper create/remove
  contract across the complete forward/inverse view projection*, nor cite the
  registry inverse ontology, the `add_fk_helpers`/`remove_fk_helpers` split
  precedent, or the `enrich_lookup` provenance path. Confirmation-review 001's
  "explicit composition has value" section touched the semantic-pair question but
  did not settle it in ontology terms. This review supplies that analysis.
* Neither artifact recorded the complete-roundtrip test-coverage gap (§4.8).

*Conclusions that are wrong:* none. No prior finding is factually incorrect.

*Did the prior confirmation fail to evaluate the semantic projection boundary?*
Partially -- it evaluated it implicitly (canonical-vs-view visibility) but did not
name the create/remove ownership contract or measure it against the framework's
inverse ontology. Doing so now *confirms* the boundary is correctly placed.

*Does the terminal "NOT READY FOR IMPLEMENTATION" verdict remain valid?* Yes.

== 8. The two explanations required of an upheld outcome

*Why the distributed helper addition/removal is not a missing semantic-projection
contract.* The canonical payload -- the relation `[story_id, column_key, value]`
(or the compact `[story_id, group_name, code]`) -- is fully reconstructed by a
*single complete forward/inverse pair*: `contract_xref`/`expand_xref`, or the
`expand_compact_multiaxis` composite. The `title` helper is *not part of that
payload*; it is read-only display re-derived from the canonical `stories` frame on
every outbound and dropped before every inverse, so no canonical value ever
depends on it round-tripping. Modelling its creation as `join_frames`
(`projection_only`) and its removal inside `project_by_role` (self-inverse,
"inbound drops display_helper by design") is exactly the framework's established
read-only-helper shape -- identical in structure to `add_fk_helpers`
(`projection_only`) paired with `remove_fk_helpers`. A split of create and remove
across two registered steps is the documented *norm*, not a broken contract. The
only residual is a *general* property of `expand_xref` (it melts every non-key
column, so any un-stripped display column corrupts any XRef inverse); that is a
known primitive contract handled by stripping before expansion, not a defect
specific to this feature.

*Why the elementary-operation distinction does not change public API ownership.*
The repository already expresses the "semantic vs. elementary" distinction through
registry `category` + `inverse.kind`. Under that model the mechanical reorder is a
private one-line helper with a single consumer, below the evidence threshold for a
public step; the XRef-specific ordering policy is legitimately owned by
`project_by_role` composing *around* the canonical primitives; and canonical
reconstruction is owned by the XRef pair. Recognising that the reorder is
"elementary" and the relation inverse is "semantic" changes none of these
placements -- it *explains* the current split rather than contradicting it. There
is therefore no responsibility that must move to a new or different public
surface.

== 9. Risks actively investigated (Phase 8)

* *Provenance on inbound:* `join_frames` records no helper provenance, so inbound
  removal relies on the explicit `helper_columns: [title]` override at each site,
  not on discovery. Deterministic today; the coupling is author knowledge.
* *Cardinality / missing keys:* the `title` join is `many-to-one`
  (`story_id -> stories.id`, unique id). A missing/duplicate lookup key would
  surface through `join_frames`' own contract, not through `project_by_role`.
* *Helper edits:* currently *ignored by removal* (dropped inbound), never
  validated or melted. Validating read-only drift would be scope expansion.
* *Melt safety:* guaranteed only by the inbound `project_by_role` running before
  `expand_*`; verified present at all five inbound sites.
* *Test-coverage gap:* no single Core test spans the complete view-projection
  roundtrip or asserts helper melt-avoidance (§4.8). *Watch-item.* If any
  bounded work is ever opened here, the highest-value increment is a Core
  integration test of the full `join -> project(outbound) -> project(inbound) ->
  expand` chain over a synthetic (non-worldbuilding) fixture -- valuable even
  though the current design is sound.
* *Grouped-XRef:* not the owner and unaffected. The group surface uses *normal*
  `contract_xref` forward and the `expand_compact_multiaxis` composite inverse;
  grouped-XRef composes unchanged `contract_xref` and is a separate
  specialisation. Guarded suite (`-k grouped_xref`) green.
* *External consumers / removal:* no reason to remove or demote
  `project_by_role`; ten live public sites depend on it and it is the settled
  consumer contract.
