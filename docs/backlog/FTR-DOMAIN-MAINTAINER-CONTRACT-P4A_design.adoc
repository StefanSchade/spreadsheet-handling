[[status-authority]]
== 1. Status, authority, and purpose

The active sequencing authority remains
`docs/backlog/roadmap_domain_hardening_sequence.adoc`. Phase E and its
Trusted-Ingress documentation closure are complete. Establishing the DMC is the
sole next authorized global stage; Phase F / the first bounded pilot follows it,
and D-T3 remains a later candidate. This design does not renumber or start any
of those stages.

The DMC answers one practical maintainer question:

[quote]
____
I am about to harden this bounded Domain area. What must I understand, decide,
preserve, simplify, test, document, and review before I call it done?
____

Its job is to prevent two opposite failures:

* preserving sediment as architecture merely because behavior, naming,
  metadata, configuration, or a public-looking import already exists; and
* destroying a real capability, authority boundary, or cross-owner handoff
  merely because a local source-tree cleanup looks simpler.

This is a *broad-but-bounded* contract. It covers semantics, authority,
compatibility, state/provenance, material interactions, consumers, tests,
documentation, and structural locality, but applies only to the selected
bounded Domain change. The roadmap's DMC dimensions A-D remain the structural,
naming, readability, and objective-guard subset of this contract. The roadmap's
statement that it does not write the final detailed DMC means that the roadmap
is sequencing authority and the detailed contract is separate focused work; it
does not require the procedure to ignore the Cycle 0-7 decisions needed to make
a bounded hardening safe. No maintained-authority contradiction requiring a
roadmap edit was found.

*Revision provenance.* This design was materially rewritten after the
consolidated semantic-ownership/topology assessment, its independent Review
001, the historical-review signal inventory, and maintainer consolidation of
those findings:

* `docs/warm_storage/global_reviews/domain_semantic_ownership_topology_assessment_2026-08-19.adoc`;
* `docs/warm_storage/global_reviews/domain_semantic_ownership_topology_assessment_2026-08-19_review_001.adoc`;
* `docs/warm_storage/global_reviews/domain_hardening_historical_review_signal_inventory_2026-08-20.adoc`.

Those artifacts and Git history preserve the detailed evidence. This design is
not a review log.

[[bounded-scope]]
== 2. Bounded scope and non-goals

The subject of a DMC cycle is a *bounded Domain scope*. Depending on the actual
change, that may be:

* a coherent family;
* a capability or public composite;
* a shared primitive whose stewardship changes;
* a cross-cutting Domain service or protocol;
* a boundary capability;
* a material interaction/handoff; or
* another bounded Domain change surface.

Use existing repository vocabulary where it fits. In particular, retain
*family* for a useful family-shaped scope. Do not invent a universal replacement
owner kind, and do not force Trusted Ingress, schema maintenance, final cleanup,
Workbook View, shared primitives, or public composites into a fake family shape.

The DMC does *not* require or authorize:

* a whole-Domain taxonomy, capability/module census, ownership registry, or
  interaction-contract registry;
* complete population or ratification of
  `registries/domain_contracts/canonical/`;
* a universal metadata/state classification, test-ownership marker, package
  skeleton, or one reference document per scope;
* global Domain/Core placement decisions or a Core rename/redefinition;
* redesign of Trusted Ingress, Egress design, output-integrity signatures, or
  sink implementation;
* revalidation of every historical finding or promotion of `HRS-*` identifiers
  into permanent vocabulary;
* implementation plans for unrelated families/capabilities; or
* subjective readability preferences becoming hard CI gates.

A two-line helper does not need a ceremonial inventory covering every question
in this document. Applicability and risk determine depth. Conversely, a locally
small edit that changes persisted authority or a material handoff is not small
architecturally.

[[working-model]]
== 3. Working model and decision axes

=== 3.1 Family as locality of reasoning

Where a family is the useful ownership model, its primary purpose is pragmatic
divide-and-conquer: it is a semantic ownership and comprehension boundary in
which a maintainer can understand and change a capability mostly locally
because its members share meaningful semantic and/or metadata/lifecycle
coupling, not merely co-location or common use (see <<ownership-evidence>>).

[quote]
____
Optimize family boundaries for semantic and metadata locality, not theoretical
taxonomy. A maintainer should be able to understand and change a family mostly
from within its package or bounded scope; cross-family contracts are expected,
but should be narrow, explicit, and few.
____

This is a judgment principle, not a numerical metric. Cross-family dependency
is expected; zero coupling is not the goal. Metadata/state locality, especially
around `_meta`, matters because distant implicit state communication is costly
to understand and change safely. Uncontrolled cross-family `_meta` coupling has
historically been one of the strongest signals of a poor boundary; genuinely
cross-family metadata communication should be comparatively small, explicit,
and contract-backed. Section <<state-lifecycle>> owns the detailed lifecycle
questions; `_meta` location is not itself a family classification.

A family decomposition need not be mathematically unique. Two reasonable
architects may draw a boundary differently. The chosen model may be contingent;
after hardening, semantic stewardship must nevertheless be clear. State, policy,
type, or change ownership must not remain “sort of A and sort of B.”

=== 3.2 Owner, role, relation, property, and layer are separate

Do not collapse these axes:

* *Family / owner:* who owns the semantic capability, state, policy, or change
  surface?
* *Kind / role:* what does an element do? Examples include projection,
  reconstruction, validation, configuration, extraction/derivation, helper,
  model, policy, and composition. This list is open; roles may coexist and may
  be family-owned.
* *Relationship:* how do scopes collaborate? Examples include composes,
  consumes, provides a primitive, produces/consumes metadata, validates,
  reconstructs, requests or executes cleanup, updates references, renders, and
  publishes.
* *Layer / placement:* where can the implementation live while respecting
  authority and dependency direction?

Properties such as composite, shared primitive, public/internal, cross-layer,
confidence, or historical sediment are not competing owner categories.
Validation, configuration, or reconstruction appearing in non-family scopes
does not imply those roles sit outside families; they are often family-owned.

[[ownership-evidence]]
=== 3.3 Evidence is not proof of ownership

None of the following alone proves same-family ownership: shared
implementation, inverse, helper, primitive, metadata namespace, output shape,
package, documentation chapter, common use, or composition. “Independently
useful” is likewise weak evidence for separation.

The stronger question is whether a split or merge improves semantic and
metadata locality while keeping external contracts narrow and understandable.
`_meta.derived` is a channel/location, not proof that all producers using it
share one owner. Common shape does not imply common semantics.

=== 3.4 Working transformation and Projection concepts

For DMC reasoning, a Domain transformation may be approximated as:

[source,text]
----
Frames -> Frames
----

`Frames` includes controlled Domain state and metadata. This is deliberately
broad and does not globally rename repository terminology. Helpers, primitives,
types, policies, services, and boundaries are not necessarily transformations.

The following is clarified *working vocabulary*, not a promotion into the
canonical Functional or Semantic Model:

[quote]
____
A Projection is a Domain transformation that derives a fachlich suitable or
user-oriented editing/presentation surface from canonical state and defines
the authority, precedence, conflict, and reconstruction semantics by which
edits are mapped back toward canonical state.
____

Projection does not imply mathematical bijectivity. A projection may introduce
redundancy, reconstruct lossily, deliberately overwrite redundant competing
state, use explicit precedence or configurable conflict policy, and require
controlled metadata.

=== 3.5 Domain validation and editing constraints

Keep these responsibilities distinct:

* *Domain validation* asks whether existing Domain state satisfies an invariant.
* A spreadsheet editing/input constraint describes which values a user may or
  is encouraged to enter on an editing surface. Excel calls this “Data
  Validation,” but architecturally it is declarative editing/presentation
  intent.

“Workbook Editing Constraint” is useful descriptive wording in this design,
not a global terminology decision. A family may own Domain validation semantics
while reusing shared mechanics. Legend Blocks may contribute values to editing
constraints without becoming Cell-Codec-owned.

[[cycle-zero]]
== 4. Bounded Cycle 0: establish scope and authority

Before topology or public-surface changes, create a temporary working inventory
of only the selected scope and the material edges required to change it safely.
Do not first classify the whole Domain.

As applicable, identify:

* the semantic concept(s), current implementation locations, and present
  ownership/authority claims;
* registered/YAML, documented user, and supported programmatic surfaces,
  distinguishing *importable* from *supported API*;
* configuration and its authoring/resolution authority;
* canonical, projected/derived, transient, and persisted state actually touched;
* metadata/provenance producers and consumers;
* projection, reconstruction, cleanup, and replay behavior;
* material handoffs and adjacent owners that must coordinate;
* actual maintained consumers and credible external consumers when relevant;
* tests protecting semantics, boundaries, interactions, and compatibility;
* historical/version/name sediment and compatibility modes;
* current related docs, contracts, and registry declarations;
* relevant rows/themes from the historical-review signal inventory; and
* local Domain/Core or package-placement questions raised by the change.

Use “none” or “not applicable” when a material question was considered and
genuinely does not apply. Do not turn this list into a mandatory form for every
minor implementation detail. The inventory is complete when the supported
behavior, authority, public/compatibility surface, affected state, and material
handoffs are clear enough to separate the proposed change from unrelated
behavior.

=== 4.1 Evidence and authority discipline

Current runtime behavior, maintained architectural/model documents, executable
tests, supported consumer surfaces, and checked declarations provide different
kinds of evidence. Existing behavior is not self-authorizing, and an intended
registry row is not proof of observed runtime exposure.

The Domain Contracts Registry already exists. It contains explicit family/type
taxonomies and useful checked contract surfaces, but its README states that it
is deliberately small seed infrastructure rather than full transformation
coverage. Much of its content is candidate/inferred. Treat it as a relevant
existing model to reconcile when touched, not as absent, a complete ownership
map, or automatic authority over maintained runtime semantics. Do not create a
parallel ontology.

Historical `HRS-*` rows are navigation evidence. Consult only the rows relevant
to the bounded scope, follow their original sources when the rationale matters,
and verify current truth locally. Do not revalidate the entire inventory or use
its IDs as normative identifiers.

=== 4.2 Contract-proliferation check

Hardening does not automatically mean adding contracts or registry rows. Where
several surfaces appear to declare the same fact, ask:

* Which existing source owns the fact?
* Is another surface a derived projection or a join, or is it a mirror?
* Does a machine-readable declaration provide concrete value and a safe control
  loop?
* Can redundant, obsolete, or untrustworthy authority be removed?
* Is the existing registry complete and current for this specific fact?

The durable Review 005 rule is: one fact, one authoritative owner; join, do not
mirror; grow registries only from bounded proven needs. Full Domain Contracts
Registry population is not a DMC-acceptance or pilot prerequisite.

[[semantic-surface-decisions]]
== 5. Semantic, public-surface, and configuration decisions

Start from desired Domain semantics, not the current function graph or package
layout. For the selected scope, answer as applicable:

* What user/domain problem and transition does the scope own?
* Which invariants, policy, state, and lifecycle belong to it?
* Which adjacent responsibilities and states remain owned elsewhere?
* Which current operations are distinct Domain transitions, and which are
  historical alternatives for one transition?
* What is the supported registered/YAML surface? What Python surface is
  intentionally supported rather than merely reachable?
* Which configuration dimensions express semantic choices, and which are
  transport syntax, inference, or historical implementation accidents?
* Which names express current domain responsibility rather than mechanism,
  retired ontology, carrier shape, or version history?

“One concept -> one preferred surface” is an anti-sediment heuristic, not a
mathematical law. Multiple public operations are legitimate when they represent
genuinely different transitions or user contracts. Minimizing function count is
not a goal and must not hide real input, lifecycle, authority, or capability.

Configuration should make real semantic choices explicit, use bounded
vocabulary where the semantic set is closed, avoid payload-shape interpretation
heuristics unless an accepted producer owns them, expose clear defaults, and
avoid duplicate authoring paths for the same fact. YAML/CLI keys and parameter
shapes are gated surfaces under `docs/ai_info/interfaces_and_gates.adoc`; their
carrier syntax does not decide semantic ownership.

=== 5.1 Composite role contract

This section describes composition-specific facts owned by a bounded scope when
it plays a composite role; it does not introduce a new owner category.

Preserve this invariant:

[quote]
____
Composite owns composition; dependencies retain primitive semantics.
____

A composite may own operation order, dependency configuration, temporary
artifacts, trace/diagnostic policy, public composite identity, cross-operation
failure behavior, and reconstruction coordination. It must not duplicate the
primitive semantics or validation owned by its dependencies. Persisted
composite state should be minimal truthful intent or explicitly
non-authoritative diagnostics, never leaked temporary implementation state
presented as inverse authority.

[[interaction-state]]
== 6. Interaction, state, projection, and provenance contracts

=== 6.1 Material handoffs are first-class

A handoff is *material* when a plausible single-sided change on either side --
for example to authority, ordering, mutation, representation/format, conflict
behavior, failure/retry/replay behavior, or lifecycle timing -- could silently
break the other side's current behavior, invariant, or compatibility surface
unless both sides are considered together. This is a judgment test, not a
metric or registry.

For each material handoff the bounded change touches, establish as applicable:

* producer;
* consumer/executor;
* authoritative state and permitted mutation/copy ownership;
* ordering and consumption/termination behavior;
* failure behavior and failure atomicity/partial-publication guarantee;
* conflict detection and conflict-resolution ownership where multiple
  producers or alternative paths converge on one consumer/executor;
* compatibility expectations; and
* responsibility for coordinating a change across both sides.

Typical seams include feature -> final cleanup, composite -> primitive,
feature-owned metadata -> schema maintenance, projected state -> reconstruction
-> canonical state, Domain intent -> render/adapter, publisher report ->
Artifact Manifest, and family-owned state -> Workbook View or editing
constraints. Record relevant handoffs in the Cycle 0 working material or the
existing owning contract; do not create an interaction registry.

Build and validate before publishing where failure could otherwise mutate
caller-owned frames/nested metadata or expose a partial result. A new top-level
mapping is not sufficient if nested state still aliases the caller.

[[state-lifecycle]]
=== 6.2 State, metadata, provenance, cleanup, and replay

Use the vocabulary and lifecycle authority already owned by the Semantic and
Technical Models; do not create a DMC state ontology. For state the change
actually touches, answer:

* What is authoritative? What is authored Intent versus recomputable
  Resolution?
* What is canonical, derived/projected, transient, or persisted under the
  current owning model?
* What may be edited, recomputed, overwritten, discarded before persistence,
  or intentionally lost?
* What reconstructs, with which authority and precedence?
* Who produces and consumes provenance? What becomes stale?
* Who requests cleanup, who executes it, when does it run, and what state must
  survive until then?
* Is replay safe/idempotent, including after partial prior lifecycle progress?
* Who owns mutation and copies, and what must remain unchanged on failure?

Preserve “Persist Intent; discard recomputable Resolution” where it applies.
Do not infer lifecycle or ownership from a carrier location alone. A
cross-consumer metadata shape needs explicit common versus consumer-specific
policy when consumers differ. Schema maintenance may update feature-owned
references without owning their meaning.

=== 6.3 Projection-specific questions

When the bounded scope contains a projection, establish:

* what state is canonical and what state is projected;
* which surface is authoritative for edits;
* what reconstruction occurs and who owns it;
* which precedence applies;
* how conflicts are detected and resolved;
* what redundancy or information loss is intentional;
* what metadata/provenance is required and how stale metadata is handled; and
* what cleanup, replay, and roundtrip behavior applies.

A one-way extraction needs no invented inverse. A non-projection needs no fake
projection precedence contract.

[[compatibility]]
== 7. Compatibility, migration, naming, and sediment

The durable compatibility principle is:

[quote]
____
Existing behavior is evidence, not an automatic compatibility obligation.
____

A hardening change may preserve, simplify, migrate, rename, merge, deprecate,
replace, or remove behavior when the architectural benefit justifies the
compatibility cost. Breaking changes may be justified before or after 1.0. The
decision depends on migration friction and compatibility expectation, not a
version-number shortcut.

Consider:

* actual maintained consumers and credible external consumers where
  discoverable/relevant;
* maturity and stability expectations of the affected surface;
* migration effort and guidance;
* whether migration is mechanical, declarative, or AI-friendly; and
* the semantic/architectural simplification gained.

The current project appears to have limited external adoption and does not yet
treat its whole surface as fully stable. That provides broad present freedom
for justified cleanup; it is evidence at this time, not a timeless DMC rule.
Evidence is required before retaining duplicate sediment and before breaking a
real contract.

The following dispositions are useful where they clarify a material surface;
they are not a mandatory registry matrix for every implementation detail:

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Disposition | Decision

| *preserve*
| Retain a distinct valuable behavior or contract with supported-use or
  architectural evidence.

| *simplify / merge*
| Reduce historical alternatives without losing a distinct transition, input,
  lifecycle, or authority.

| *migrate / rename / replace*
| Keep the capability through a clearer surface, with proportionate migration
  guidance and affected maintained consumers moved.

| *deprecate*
| Retain a time-bounded compatibility bridge to a named replacement when
  immediate removal has unjustified friction.

| *remove*
| Retire redundant, dead, misleading, or unjustifiably costly behavior after
  checking relevant consumers and migration expectations.
|===

Give historical modes a local preserve/simplify/replace/remove decision; Cell
Codec's older code-row mode is an example, not a global rule. Remove obsolete
version or slice labels from current symbols/config/prose when they no longer
express a live distinction. Keep historical IDs as optional provenance when the
surrounding meaning stands without them. Apply the release-note workflow when a
user- or caller-visible change warrants it.

[[boundaries]]
== 8. Trusted Ingress, layer placement, and output boundary consequences

=== 8.1 Trusted Ingress

Trusted Ingress is accepted permanent architecture. The DMC references rather
than reimplements it. Current owners are:

* `docs/technical_model/ch06_architectural_layers/architectural_layers.adoc`
  for establishment/re-establishment, executable binding, controlled state,
  description/resource authority, and the layer model;
* `docs/developer_guide/ch06_code_conventions/04_trusted_ingress_extension.adoc`
  for maintainer extension procedure; and
* `docs/ai_info/interfaces_and_gates.adoc` for the caller-facing programmatic
  surface.

Consequences for bounded Domain hardening:

* ordinary Domain code may rely on the established Ingress-Conformant Domain
  Payload language after a framework-managed boundary;
* capability/family semantic preconditions and Domain invariants remain with
  their semantic owner;
* Domain code should not reintroduce arbitrary external-carrier/hostile-object
  defenses already owned by Trusted Ingress;
* every controlled non-ordinary transition touched by the change must have
  explicit accepted authority under the canonical controlled-state contract;
* a refactor must not broaden certification or introduce redundant historical
  authority merely to preserve sediment; and
* caller-precondition surfaces such as direct Domain calls or `run_pipeline`
  do not acquire managed-boundary guarantees through importability.

Do not generalize the current FK controlled-role example into a universal
“exactly one producer/transition per capability” law. The accepted architecture
defines exact authority per controlled role and transition; one capability may
legitimately have more than one reviewed producer or transition where its owning
contract permits that.

=== 8.2 Domain and Core

The maintained Architectural Layers chapter remains authority. The DMC does not
define Core as merely pure/stateless, content-free, cross-layer-used, or a Ports
layer; it does not rename Core.

When a bounded change raises a placement question, reason deliberately about
semantic/feature authority, legal inward dependency direction, cross-layer
carrier or mechanism needs, side effects/runtime ownership, compatibility, and
cycle/duplication risk. Two useful local hypotheses are:

* *Complexity isolation:* a small bounded mechanism may live in Core because it
  keeps low-level mechanics out of Domain algorithms.
* *Cross-layer neutrality:* a mechanism/type may live in Core when several
  layers legitimately need it and it owns no feature policy.

Feature authority, projection/reconstruction semantics, Domain metadata
lifecycle, and feature-specific policy are negative evidence against Core.
These are review aids, not a replacement global rule. `core.fk`,
`core.indexing`, and `core.refs` remain local questions only when touched.

=== 8.3 Output context, not Egress design

Retain only this narrow ownership boundary:

* the feature/family owns semantic projection, reconstruction, provenance,
  authority, and cleanup;
* feature code should not learn adapter identity merely to enforce sink
  compatibility; and
* output/boundary logic may later validate admissible Domain exit state and
  sink capability under separately accepted architecture.

Carrier-oriented names do not decide semantic ownership. Keep open the
distinction:

[source,text]
----
tabular / relational state
  -> hierarchical / object-like semantic structure
  -> YAML / JSON / file publication
----

This DMC neither places those responsibilities nor designs Egress. Checksums,
signatures, and beyond-MVP roundtrip integrity evidence are not requirements.

[[source-topology]]
== 9. Source and package topology

Topology follows semantic decisions and exists to improve comprehension and
locality, not symmetry. For a cohesive family large enough to benefit from
physical structure, prefer:

[source,text]
----
family first
  -> kind / responsibility inside the family
----

For example:

[source,text]
----
domain/fk_helpers/
    projection.py
    reconstruction.py
    validation.py
    model.py
    policy.py
----

This is illustrative, not a required skeleton or a conclusion about the FK
pilot's eventual boundary. Kind may remain encoded in filenames while small. A
role may become a subpackage only when a real semantic seam and size justify it:

[source,text]
----
validation.py
  -> validation/
       dangling_references.py
       relation_shape.py
----

Do not require top-level technical-kind trees that scatter one family's
reasoning. Small capabilities need not become packages. Shared primitives and
cross-cutting services may sit outside a family package when their contract is
genuinely shared. Cross-owner relations must remain explicit rather than being
hidden by nesting.

Preserve deliberate package facades, stable supported entry points, registry
targets, inward dependencies, and private-import discipline. Do not infer a
supported API from importability or create packages merely so a guard/template
can exist. Size and complexity remain review signals, not mechanical split
targets. Family/repository locality does not by itself make the code path
locally reconstructable; apply <<local-human-readability>> as a separate,
complementary concern.

[[local-human-readability]]
=== 9.1 Local Human Readability

Local Human Readability is *bounded reconstruction inside sound architecture*.
For a touched code path, a maintainer should be able to reconstruct its current
semantics, lifecycle, ownership/mutation, validation and side-effect order, and
important non-local reasons without avoidable archaeology through trivial
helpers, callers, consumers, tests, metadata, or history.

Three complementary concerns remain distinct: family/repository locality says
where owned reasoning should live; Local Human Readability says how a touched
algorithm can be reconstructed; Reference Readability says permanent meaning
must not be trapped behind historical identifiers or archaeology. None
substitutes for either of the others.

Prefer top-down readable flow and algorithmic locality when extraction would
merely add navigation to reconstruct one coherent operation. Extract or
abstract when doing so exposes a real semantic boundary, owned value, invariant,
reusable contract, independently understandable mechanism, or other coherent
responsibility. Resolve raw option/state bags at the real boundary when that
reduces reconstruction cost; make ownership and mutation explicit; and use
comments for invariants, authority, boundary reasons, or surprising constraints
rather than Python syntax. Remove downstream defensive introspection when the
established Trusted-Ingress contract already owns the guarantee.

This principle favors neither keeping everything inline nor extracting helpers.
It creates no function/file length, line-count, helper-count, or readability
metric. The detailed judgment-based procedure remains owned by
`docs/developer_guide/ch06_code_conventions/03_human_readability_review.adoc`.
For a small change, its lightweight before/after reader-question test is
sufficient; reserve the full review sequence for changes whose cost is
non-trivial.

[[tests-consumers]]
== 10. Tests and consumer evidence

For a bounded change, identify and preserve or add the tests that protect the
contracts actually affected:

* semantic invariants;
* managed-boundary/controlled-state behavior when applicable;
* projection, reconstruction, roundtrip, cleanup, and replay when applicable;
* material interaction ordering, authority, atomicity, and metadata handoffs;
* supported public/registry/configuration surfaces;
* maintained-consumer behavior where relevant; and
* narrow architecture guards that protect a deliberate dependency or package
  boundary.

Existing FTR markers remain useful because they answer “why does this test
exist?” Do not require a new global family/capability marker or reorganize the
test tree around ownership. Integration and interaction tests may legitimately
protect several owners. Add test-ownership machinery only if repeated pilots
demonstrate a concrete problem.

Consumer evidence is proportional. If a maintained consumer uses a changed
surface, inventory and migrate it as an acceptance probe. If no consumer exists,
do not invent migration work. Use credible external-consumer evidence where
available and relevant; do not assume absence merely because the repository has
no local call site. Spreadsheet export/reimport, adapter parity, semantic diffs,
or managed-pipeline probes are required only when the changed contract reaches
those surfaces.

[[documentation]]
== 11. Canonical documentation and human/LLM reference readiness

A supported public family or capability should be understandable without
architecture archaeology, but the DMC must not create one manually maintained
reference document per scope or another source of truth. Canonical information
may remain distributed by document ownership:

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
| Surface | Owns

| Functional Model
| semantic behavior and the user/problem-space responsibility

| Semantic Model
| vocabulary, identities, meaning, and invariants

| Technical Model
| lifecycle, persistence, boundaries, and mechanisms

| User Guide
| supported usage, configuration, examples, errors, and limitations

| Developer Guide
| maintainer procedure, including the eventual DMC, conventions, and review
  guidance

| `interfaces_and_gates.adoc`
| compact public-surface responsibilities and approval gates

| Registries
| facts that genuinely benefit from structured machine-readable authority and
  have a trustworthy control loop

| A bounded FTR / Cycle 0 working artifact
| temporary design, evidence, decisions, and migration plan
|===

The intended future reference model is:

[source,text]
----
canonical sources
  -> deterministic aggregation / projection
  -> coherent human- and LLM-facing reference
----

A future implementation may use a command, templates, registries, or generated
AsciiDoc/Markdown/`llms.txt`; that work is outside this design. JSON should own
only facts that benefit from structured authority. Do not serialize narrative
architecture merely to generate documentation, and do not let generated output
become another authority.

Completion property:

[quote]
____
The semantics of a supported public family or capability have clear canonical
owners and are structured well enough that a coherent human- and LLM-facing
reference can be assembled without inventing missing semantics or creating
another source of truth.
____

Reference Readability applies: permanent code/docs must state their meaning
without requiring an FTR ID, review finding, obsolete version name, or Git
history lookup. Historical IDs may remain optional provenance; useful history
need not be stripped merely because an ID appears. This documentation/citation
property complements but does not substitute for Local Human Readability of the
touched implementation (<<local-human-readability>>), just as tidy package
topology does not substitute for either.

[[completion]]
== 12. Proportional completion gate

A bounded hardening cycle is complete when the following applicable outcomes
hold. “Not applicable” is legitimate; it must not be used to hide a material
touched contract.

. The semantic scope, owner/stewardship, invariants, and material adjacent
  responsibilities are clear.
. Material handoffs have explicit producer/consumer, authority, order,
  conflict detection/resolution where applicable, failure/atomicity,
  compatibility, and coordinated-change responsibility.
. Supported registered, YAML, user, and Python surfaces are deliberate;
  importability has not been mistaken for support.
. Compatibility and migration decisions are evidence-backed and proportionate;
  affected maintained consumers are migrated where required.
. Touched state, metadata, provenance, projection/reconstruction, cleanup,
  replay, persistence, and copy ownership form a coherent lifecycle.
. Trusted-Ingress and layer consequences are respected without reimplementing
  or broadening their authority; local Domain/Core decisions are explicitly
  reviewed where the change requires them.
. Source/package topology supports semantic and metadata locality without a
  mechanical skeleton or false nesting.
. Touched code paths satisfy Local Human Readability
  (<<local-human-readability>>): current semantics, ownership/mutation,
  validation/side-effect order, and important non-local reasons are
  reconstructable without avoidable archaeology. The guide's lightweight
  before/after reader-question test is sufficient for a small change.
. Relevant semantic, boundary, interaction, roundtrip, consumer, and
  architecture tests are green.
. Historical modes and sediment touched by the change have a deliberate
  preserve/simplify/replace/remove disposition.
. Canonical docs and genuinely authoritative contract surfaces are updated once;
  redundant/mirrored authority is not added, and release-note workflow is
  applied where relevant.
. Permanent prose/code satisfy Reference Readability and allow coherent
  human/LLM reference assembly without inventing semantics.
. A proportionate independent review has accepted the hardened bounded scope
  before it is considered complete.

No inverse, consumer migration, package, projection contract, ingress redesign,
archival action, Project Memory edit, registry row, or documentation surface is
required when the bounded change does not actually have or affect it.

[[first-pilot]]
== 13. First-pilot sequencing

The FK/reference-helper area remains a strong leading pilot candidate because
it has current maintenance pain and representative semantic, metadata,
controlled-state, cleanup, rendering, configuration, and consumer handoffs.
That evidence selects an area to investigate; it does not decide its topology.

The first bounded Cycle 0 must determine whether FK Helpers, Lookup Enrichment,
cleanup, validation, and related handoffs form one family, collaborating
capabilities, or another explicit ownership model that improves semantic and
metadata locality. In particular, it must not assume:

* FK Helpers and Lookup Enrichment are one family;
* derived-column policy is owned by either producer;
* shared `_meta.derived` placement or formula/provenance mechanics prove common
  ownership;
* `grouped_xref` or XRef belongs to a reference-helper family; or
* both historical public routes must survive before compatibility and semantic
  evidence selects the concepts and surfaces worth retaining.

Relevant evidence includes the distinct FK declare/materialize/validate/drop
lifecycle, Lookup Enrichment's explicit source-plus-lookup projection and
policies, and their material cleanup/Workbook View/rendering/consumer handoffs.
The pilot tests the DMC; the DMC must not pre-solve the pilot.

No pilot is started by this recommendation. D-T3 remains a roadmap candidate,
not an automatically started or bypassing slice.

[[review-gate]]
== 15. Fresh independent-review gate

Before permanent promotion and before the first Domain hardening pilot, this
revised design must pass a *fresh independent architecture review*. The reviewer
should challenge at least:

* whether the DMC is bounded and proportional enough to avoid another Phase-E
  census;
* whether family locality is operational without becoming a taxonomy or metric;
* whether interaction/handoff contracts cover authority, ordering, failure
  atomicity, and coordinated change adequately;
* whether projection/reconstruction authority, precedence, conflict, loss, and
  metadata are treated correctly;
* whether compatibility decisions measure migration friction and expectation
  without fossilizing behavior or licensing gratuitous breakage;
* whether Trusted Ingress is referenced rather than reimplemented or broadened;
* whether the DMC creates registry/contract duplication or assumes unchecked
  declarations are authority;
* whether package guidance is useful, family-local where appropriate, and
  non-mechanical;
* whether test, consumer, documentation, and completion requirements are
  proportional;
* whether FK/lookup topology remains genuinely open for Cycle 0;
* whether any new global Domain/Core rule was smuggled in; and
* whether an FK-specific or current controlled-role fact was generalized into
  universal architecture without evidence.

The review uses the project's normal independent-review verdict and finding
severity discipline. Acceptance authorizes only the next explicitly sequenced
DMC promotion work; it does not by itself authorize a Domain refactor, Phase F,
the pilot, D-T3, or any other implementation.
