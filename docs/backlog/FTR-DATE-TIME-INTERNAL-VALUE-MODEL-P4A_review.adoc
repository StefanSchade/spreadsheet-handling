==== Central review question -- is this the smallest coherent extension of Slice 1?

*Finding: yes, for the category-set and carrier-model recommendation itself.*
Every one of the six challenge questions in section 1 of the review task was
independently checked:

* *Is Date semantically distinct from midnight DateTime?* Yes --
  independently reproduced: `datetime.date(2026,8,9) < datetime.datetime(2026,8,9)`
  raises `TypeError` at the Python level; a `date`+`None` DataFrame column
  stays `object` dtype with a plain `None` missing cell, while a
  `datetime`+`None` column is silently promoted by pandas itself to
  `datetime64[us]` with `pandas.NaT` -- the two categories are not
  interchangeable even at the pandas-ergonomics level, not merely the
  conceptual level.
* *Does current maintained data exercise Date? DateTime?* Yes, both,
  independently reproduced: `yaml.safe_load` on an unquoted `2026-08-09`
  scalar produces a real `datetime.date`; on `2026-08-09T10:30:00` produces a
  real `datetime.datetime`; on `2026-08-09T10:30:00+02:00` produces an aware
  `datetime.datetime`. This is PyYAML's own `SafeLoader` resolver, already
  reachable through `yaml_backend.load_yaml_dir`'s unmodified `yaml.safe_load`
  call, not a hypothetical.
* *Would collapsing either direction lose semantics?* Yes, independently
  confirmed both directions are lossy (day-vs-midnight ambiguity one way,
  dropped time-of-day the other), matching the FTR's own section 7/8
  analysis.
* *Does pandas coercion undermine the distinction at carrier level?*
  Partially, and the FTR states this correctly, not overclaiming: a pure
  `date` column stays `object`/`datetime.date` (undisturbed); a `datetime`
  column is promoted to `datetime64`/`Timestamp` (disturbed in carrier shape
  only, not in semantic category -- a promoted `Timestamp` is still
  conceptually DateTime, never reclassified as Date by the FTR's own
  recommendation).

No evidence found to challenge the two-category recommendation itself. The
concerns this review does raise are narrower: two specific claims inside the
FTR's own evidence base, and one slice-sequencing self-consistency question.

==== 1. numpy.datetime64 unit semantics -- independently probed, decision-ready

Ran the task's own representative probes
(`np.datetime64("2026-08-09","D")`, `np.datetime64("2026-08-09T12:34","m")`,
an `"ns"`-unit value) plus one additional probe the FTR itself did not run: a
`Timestamp` known to be a genuine midnight *DateTime* value, cast to `[D]`
unit, compared against a genuine day-only value at the same unit.

Result: `np.datetime64(pd.Timestamp("2026-08-09 00:00:00"), "D") == np.datetime64("2026-08-09", "D")`
is `True` -- the two are *bit-identical* once stored at day precision. Unit
alone cannot distinguish "this was authored as a date" from "this was a
midnight datetime that got truncated/resampled to day granularity," because
no information about intent survives the cast. Separately confirmed: pandas
does not even preserve the unit distinction on ordinary `Series` construction
-- both `datetime64[D]` and `datetime64[m]` source arrays produce a
`datetime64[s]`-dtype `Series` of `Timestamp` scalars in the installed pandas
version, discarding the original unit before any framework code runs.

*Assessment:* this independently reproduces and strengthens, rather than
contradicts, the FTR's own recommendation (section 8, open decision 1 in
section 24) not to infer Date-vs-DateTime from `numpy.datetime64` unit. The
evidence is now stronger than what the FTR itself presented -- the FTR
asserted the unit "is not used" as a matter of policy preference; this review
adds that the unit *could not reliably be used* even if a future slice wanted
to, because day-precision storage is genuinely ambiguous between "authored as
a date" and "datetime truncated to a date." This closes decision-ready
evidence gap 1 in the FTR's own favor: *uniform DateTime classification for
all `numpy.datetime64`, ignoring unit, is the only sound default*; a future
slice would need an out-of-band schema/producer-supplied hint (not carrier
inspection) if day-precision `Date` recognition from raw NumPy is ever
wanted.

==== Findings

===== DTVM-REVIEW-F1 -- section 3's inventory table and section 10 both misstate current `numpy.timedelta64` (non-NaT) classification behavior; it is not currently rejected, it silently misclassifies as `"number"`

*Classification:* Important.

*File/symbol:* FTR section 3 (inventory table, `numpy.timedelta64` (non-`NaT`)
row); FTR section 10 ("`scalar_category` already correctly rejects
`datetime.timedelta`, `pandas.Timedelta`, and non-`NaT` `numpy.timedelta64`
(`UnsupportedScalarError`, confirmed by probe) -- unaffected by this FTR.");
runtime: `src/spreadsheet_handling/core/scalar_values.py:202-208`
(`scalar_category`'s Boolean/Number branches).

*Evidence:* independently reproduced from a fresh process against current
`core` HEAD:

[source,pycon]
----
>>> import numpy as np
>>> from spreadsheet_handling.core.scalar_values import scalar_category
>>> v = np.timedelta64(1, "D")
>>> type(v).__mro__
(<class 'numpy.timedelta64'>, <class 'numpy.signedinteger'>,
 <class 'numpy.integer'>, <class 'numpy.number'>, <class 'numpy.generic'>,
 <class 'object'>)
>>> scalar_category(v)
'number'
----

`numpy.timedelta64` subclasses `numpy.signedinteger`/`numpy.integer` (unlike
`numpy.datetime64`, independently confirmed *not* to subclass `np.integer` or
`np.floating` -- its `__mro__` is only
`(numpy.datetime64, numpy.generic, object)`). `scalar_category`'s Number
branch is `isinstance(value, (int, float, np.integer, np.floating))`
(`scalar_values.py:206`); a non-`NaT` `numpy.timedelta64` satisfies this
before the function ever reaches its trailing `raise UnsupportedScalarError`.
The FTR's own claim that this "already correctly rejects... same path as
`datetime64` above" is false: `datetime64` correctly falls through to the
trailing raise (`np.generic` but not `np.integer`/`np.floating`); `timedelta64`
does not (it *is* `np.integer`), and the two carriers reach materially
different, not "same-path," outcomes. `tests/unit/core/test_scalar_values.py`
independently grepped: contains zero `timedelta64` test cases (neither NaT
nor non-NaT), so nothing in the existing suite would have caught this.

*Why it matters:* this is not a Date/Time-specific defect -- it predates this
FTR and is inherited unchanged from Slice 1 -- but it is exactly the "carrier
silently becoming domain semantics" defect class the entire value-model
program exists to close, and it is currently live in the accepted,
independently-reviewed `scalar_values.py` module today. A `numpy.timedelta64`
value (e.g. the natural result of subtracting two `datetime64` columns, a
completely ordinary pandas operation) reaching `scalar_category` today is
silently accepted as an ordinary `Number` -- its integer nanosecond/day count,
stripped of unit -- rather than rejected as an unsupported Duration carrier.
This directly undermines section 10's own reasoning for deferring Duration
("already correctly rejects... unaffected by this FTR") and weakens section
6's `complex128` analogy: `complex128`'s precedent holds because *every*
`complex128` instance, NaN or not, is uniformly rejected today; `timedelta64`
does not have that property today -- only its `NaT` form is (mis)handled via
Missing; its ordinary form is silently absorbed into Number, a strictly
worse defect (semantic corruption, not merely an inconsistent-Missing
classification) than the one section 6 is built to fix. Per
`ADR-DOMAIN-BOUNDARY-ROBUSTNESS`, this is exactly the class of "cheap,
simplifying, local hardening" the ADR calls desirable -- narrowing the
accepted vocabulary to what the category actually intends, not new defensive
introspection.

*Smallest correction:* (1) correct FTR section 3's inventory-table row and
section 10's claim to state the accurate current behavior (misclassifies as
`"number"`, not rejected); (2) fold the fix into D-T1's own scope -- D-T1
already touches the identical bounded-dispatch mechanism for the NaT case, so
adding an explicit `np.timedelta64` exclusion to (or ordering fix around) the
Number branch is a natural, narrow extension of work D-T1 is already doing,
not a new slice. A minimal fix shape: check for `np.timedelta64` (e.g. via
`isinstance(value, np.timedelta64)`) before the Number branch and route it to
`UnsupportedScalarError` unconditionally (consistent with the FTR's own
Duration-stays-deferred recommendation), mirroring the Boolean-before-Number
subclass-ordering pattern already established in this module.

*Disposition:* non-blocking for accepting this FTR's Date/DateTime category
recommendation (the recommendation does not depend on this claim being
correct). Should be corrected in the FTR text before D-T1 is treated as
implementation-ready, and the underlying runtime misclassification should be
fixed as part of D-T1 (or, if the maintainer prefers not to widen D-T1's
scope, filed as its own narrow BUG referencing this finding) before Phase E
begins relying on `scalar_category`'s Number classification as trustworthy.

===== DTVM-REVIEW-F2 -- D-T2 ("canonical Date/DateTime normalization primitive") contradicts this FTR's own section 12 posture and Slice 1's established Phase-E-owns-normalization precedent, without acknowledging the conflict

*Classification:* Important.

*File/symbol:* FTR section 12 ("Producing an actual canonical value from an
accepted carrier variant remains Phase E's future 'value normalization and
type resolution' stage, per Slice 1's own precedent (IVM-REVIEW-F2's
resolution) -- this FTR does not build that primitive either."); FTR section
20, D-T2 ("Adds the `normalize_scalar()`-shaped primitive Slice 1 and this
FTR both deliberately did not build (section 12) -- narrowing an accepted
carrier variant ... to its native-Python canonical form ... for whichever
future consumer needs one canonical representation rather than mere
classification.").

*Evidence:* `roadmap_domain_hardening_sequence.adoc`'s Phase E boundary shape
explicitly names "value normalization and type resolution" as a Phase E
stage, downstream of Phase D's "establish the internal type model."
`FTR-MINIMAL-INTERNAL-VALUE-MODEL-P4A_review.adoc`'s Independent Review 001
answers its own sub-question 4 ("Does Phase E need a normalization primitive
not provided here?") with "Yes," and its resolution of IVM-REVIEW-F2 states
explicitly: "Phase E's 'value normalization and type resolution' boundary
stage still owns adding a normalization primitive on top of these
classifiers." This FTR's own section 12 restates that precedent correctly and
applies it to Date/Time ("this FTR does not build that primitive either").
Section 20's D-T2 then proposes building exactly that primitive -- narrowing
an accepted carrier variant to a canonical native form -- as a *Phase D*
slice, under a `D-T` label, without flagging that this reassigns ownership
away from the precedent section 12 itself just restated two sections earlier
in the same document.

*Why it matters:* the task that produced this FTR explicitly named this a
"major architecture consistency check," and the FTR's own section 24 open
decision 4 asks the maintainer to "confirm... the D-T1..D-T5 slice sequence
and dependency ordering is the right shape" -- but that framing presents the
sequence as needing generic confirmation, not as containing a specific,
already-detectable self-contradiction against the document's own section 12.
A future implementer skimming section 20 in isolation (exactly the failure
mode Independent Review 001 warned about for `ScalarValue`'s
"Native Python representation" framing, IVM-REVIEW-F2) could reasonably
start building a general `normalize_scalar()` primitive inside Phase D,
re-litigating a boundary the project already spent one full review cycle
establishing. D-T3's own text compounds the ambiguity: it lists "a
`normalize_scalar()` call from D-T2" as one option for closing the YAML
write-side gap, treating D-T2's primitive as available Phase-D-internal
infrastructure rather than flagging that its existence there is itself the
open question.

*Smallest correction:* either (a) reframe D-T2 narrowly -- scope it to only
the local `Timestamp`/`numpy.datetime64` -> native-`date`/`datetime`
coercion D-T3's persistence fix concretely needs (a bounded, D-T3-owned
helper, not a general-purpose Phase-E-shaped `normalize_scalar()` contract),
and rename it to make that scope explicit; or (b) if the maintainer decides
Phase D should own the general primitive after all (a legitimate choice --
building it once, early, and having Phase E reuse it is defensible), state
that decision explicitly as an amendment to Slice 1's established boundary,
with its own rationale, rather than silently proposing it under a `D-T`
label two sections after restating the opposite precedent. Either
correction is a documentation-only change to this FTR; no runtime work is
implied by making the correction itself.

*Disposition:* non-blocking for accepting this FTR's Date/DateTime category
recommendation or D-T1. Must be resolved before D-T2 is treated as
implementation-ready; D-T1 does not depend on D-T2 and may proceed once
DTVM-REVIEW-F1 is addressed, independent of this finding's resolution.

===== DTVM-REVIEW-F3 -- section 11's "no `tzinfo` anywhere in either backend's native date-cell round trip" claim was not independently re-probed by this review beyond the already-established fact that both formats lack a timezone concept by design

*Classification:* Minor.

*File/symbol:* FTR section 11.

*Evidence:* this review independently confirmed XLSX/ODS date cells carry no

not as a gap requiring action now.

==== Human decisions -- decision-readiness assessment

  review's own probe (section 1 above) strengthens the FTR's recommendation:
  treat all `numpy.datetime64` uniformly as DateTime; do not infer Date from
  unit. Evidence now shows this is not merely a stylistic preference but the
  only sound default, since day-precision storage is genuinely ambiguous
  between authored-as-date and truncated-from-datetime. *Recommended

  implementation:* no -- D-T1 can proceed with the FTR's own default; only a
  future narrower Date-from-`[D]`-unit recognition (not currently proposed)

. *Timezone-aware DateTime: accept-as-is vs.\ reject-pending-policy.*
  Genuinely open; both this review's and the FTR's own evidence support
  leaving it open rather than forcing a premature choice. *Recommended
  choice:* none forced by evidence; the FTR's own refusal to choose is
  correct. *Must decide before implementation:* yes, before D-T2 (the
  slice whose own acceptance criteria the FTR assigns this resolution to).
  *Owning slice:* D-T2 (contingent on DTVM-REVIEW-F2's resolution of what
  D-T2 actually is).
. *XLSX/ODS egress parity direction.* Genuinely open; three legitimate
  options with no forcing evidence, correctly not decided here.
  *Recommended choice:* none forced by evidence. *Must decide before
  implementation:* no -- explicitly gated behind D-T5, itself gated behind a
  future ADR-VISIBLE-CELL-TYPING-STRING-SUBSTRATE reopening that has not
  happened and is not proposed by this FTR. *Owning slice:* D-T5, if/when
  authorized.
. *D-T1..D-T5 slice sequence and ordering.* Not yet decision-ready as

  before D-T2 before D-T3, D-T4/D-T5 gated appropriately) is sound; D-T2's

  *Owning slice:* the maintainer, at the point D-T2 is scheduled.
