The promotion is directionally sound and correctly leaves the major Phase-E,

carrier behavior, classification-without-narrowing contract, trust/authority
model, output-sink separation, configuration-step responsibility, and most of

architecture. Four Important findings remain:

* Duration's deferred semantic-category status is blurred with the current
  classifier's carrier rejection;

  existing Technical Model authority actually use it;
* the provisional Trusted Ingress concept still contains current-state claims
  that directly predate and contradict the promoted scalar/trust contracts.

One Minor classification-definition precision issue also remains. These are
documentation corrections only. No runtime, test, ADR, guard, Project Memory,
Phase-E, D-T3, or family-pilot work is required to resolve this review.

=== 3. Independently reconstructed scalar contract

The following current implemented semantic categories are accepted and mature:

[cols="2,3,4",options="header"]
|===
| Category | Accepted carrier behavior | Independent conclusion

| String
| `str`, including `numpy.str_` through subclass behavior; `""` is routed to
  Missing first
| Accurate

| Boolean
| `bool` and explicit `numpy.bool_`; checked before Number because `bool` is
  an `int` subclass
| Accurate

| Number
| `int`, `float`, `numpy.integer`, `numpy.floating`; Integer/Float remains a
  bounded deferral
| Accurate

| Missing
| Recognition over `None`, `""`, Python/NumPy NaN carriers, `pandas.NA`,
  `pandas.NaT`, and `numpy.datetime64('NaT')`; not a sentinel or wrapper
| Accurate

| Date
| `datetime.date`, after DateTime
| Accurate

| DateTime
| `datetime.datetime`, `pandas.Timestamp`, and every non-NaT
  `numpy.datetime64` regardless of unit
| Accurate
|===

`scalar_category` first recognizes Missing, then Boolean, String, the explicit
`numpy.timedelta64` exclusion, Number, DateTime, and Date. That order correctly
prevents Boolean-as-Number, Duration-as-Number, DateTime-as-Date, and NaT-as-
DateTime mistakes.

`pandas.Timestamp` remains a `Timestamp`; `numpy.int64` remains a
`numpy.int64`; classification returns a label and does not return, mutate,
rewrap, or narrow the input. Day-unit `numpy.datetime64[D]` is DateTime, not
Date. Timezone-aware `datetime`/`Timestamp` values are classified as DateTime
without UTC conversion, `tzinfo` stripping, or carrier canonicalization.

`is_missing_carrier` bounds `pandas.isna` to known numeric/NumPy scalar
shapes, recognizes `pandas.NA`/`pandas.NaT` by identity, handles
`numpy.datetime64` through `numpy.isnat`, explicitly excludes every
`numpy.timedelta64`, and does not dispatch arbitrary unknown objects through
their NumPy array protocol. The arbitrary-object safety claim is therefore
supported by source and focused tests.

`""` is both a recognized Missing carrier in scalar classification and an
ordinary legitimate visible string whose higher-level meaning may be resolved
from authoritative context. The promoted Missing section states that
contextual distinction explicitly and does not introduce a sentinel.

No `normalize_scalar()`-, `normalize_value()`-, or
`canonicalize_scalar()`-shaped general primitive exists in maintained source.
The private `_yaml_safe_temporal`, `_json_temporal_default`, and
`_json_temporal_object_hook` functions remain backend-local codec operations.
They are not imported by Domain/Core, not wired into Trusted Ingress, and do
not define a general normalized carrier set.

=== 4. Findings

==== TM-PROMOTION-REVIEW-F1 -- Important -- Duration deferral is blurred with current carrier rejection

*Exact file/section:* `docs/technical_model/ch08_architecture_details/internal_value_model.adoc`,
"Semantic categories versus carrier variants," lines 99-114.

*Claim:* Time and Duration are described as "deferred/rejected,
respectively," even though the next paragraph says none of the deferrals is
a permanent prohibition.

*Primary authority:* FTR-DATE-TIME-INTERNAL-VALUE-MODEL-P4A sections 9-10,
20, and 23; accepted D-T1 review/evidence; current
`core/scalar_values.py`.

*Independent evidence:* The FTR separately says to defer the Time and
Duration semantic categories. It also records the current implementation fact
that `datetime.time`, `datetime.timedelta`, `pandas.Timedelta`, and every
`numpy.timedelta64` carrier raise `UnsupportedScalarError`. It explicitly
leaves a future Duration slice free to decide Duration's own Missing semantics.

*Impact:* The slash wording upgrades a current classifier rejection into what
can be read as the semantic-category disposition. The following non-
prohibition sentence reduces but does not remove the conflict inside a
normative document. This is exactly the distinction future value-model work
must be able to recover without reading FTR history.

*Smallest correction:* State that both Time and Duration semantic categories
are deferred, while their named carriers are currently unsupported/rejected by
`scalar_category`; retain the statement that this is not a permanent
architectural prohibition.

==== TM-PROMOTION-REVIEW-F2 -- Important -- Domain invariant ownership is over-centralized and payload ingress has no current owner

*Exact files/sections:*

* `docs/technical_model/ch08_architecture_details/internal_value_model.adoc`,
  "Structural, ingress, and Domain-invariant validation," lines 184-224;
* `docs/technical_model/ch06_architectural_layers/architectural_layers.adoc`,
  "Trust, Authority, and Validation Responsibility," lines 339-350;
* `docs/technical_model/ch09_glossary/01_terms.adoc`, "Domain invariant
  validation," lines 380-390.

*Claim:* Domain semantic/invariant validity is "cleanly owned" by
`domain/validations/`; `apply_severity_policy` is presented as its sole
control-flow decision point; the ch06 summary calls the three rows "three
different questions with three different current owners."

*Primary authority:* ADR-DOMAIN-OWNS-VALIDATION-WRITERS-STAY-DUMB;
ADR-DOMAIN-BOUNDARY-ROBUSTNESS; the consolidation inventory's own seven-part
validation taxonomy; maintained Domain source.

*Independent evidence:* `domain/validations/` does own reusable Finding-based
checks. It does not own every Domain invariant. Transformation-local and
reconstruction invariants are enforced inline throughout maintained source,
including `enrich_lookup/operation.py::_check_*`,
`xref_crosstable/primitives.py::_ensure_*`,
`grouped_xref/reconstruction.py::_validated_geometry`,
`xref_axis_mapping/resolver.py::_ensure_bijection`, and many family-specific
typed-error/raising paths. The corrected inventory explicitly distinguishes
Category C (reusable Domain invariant validation), Category D (transformation
preconditions), and Category E (reconstruction/roundtrip invariants). Payload
ingress enforcement, meanwhile, has no current runtime owner: only unwired
classification machinery exists and Phase E owns the future mechanism.

*Impact:* The promoted wording erases legitimate distributed invariant
ownership and overstates current implementation by turning a future owner into
a third current owner. A maintainer could wrongly move local preconditions to
`domain/validations/` or assume payload ingress is already owned/enforced.

*Smallest correction:* Limit `domain/validations/` ownership to reusable,
Finding-based cross-data/cross-reference checks; explicitly preserve
transformation-local and reconstruction-local invariant owners. Replace
"three different current owners" with wording that distinguishes two current
ownership shapes from the Phase-E-gated ingress responsibility.

=== 6. Configuration-step assessment

The distinction between configuration/policy production and execution-time
policy resolution is mature enough to name normatively. It already exists in
`pipeline_semantics.adoc` and is evidenced by multiple maintained paths:

* `infer_fk_relations` performs bounded heuristics and writes resolved v2 FK
  relation policy;
* `configure_fk_helpers` validates explicit configuration and writes the same
  resolved relation model;
* `configure_lookup_helpers` resolves `auto`, validates keys/helpers/order,
  and writes lookup-helper policy;
* `configure_workbook_view` validates and normalizes workbook-view intent;
* later FK-helper and lookup operations read/resolve existing policy rather
  than reproducing those heuristics.

The promoted section correctly refuses to derive a file-layout convention.
It requires neither `policy.py` nor `configuration.py` and adopts no universal
package skeleton.

=== 7. Projection, extraction, reconstruction, and bijection assessment

The durable, supported parts are:

* projection does not imply reversibility;
* `expand`/`contract` names paired directions but does not itself prove
  bijection;
* `bijection`/`bijective` is used only where the owning contract makes the
  losslessness claim;
* `pipeline_semantics.adoc` remains authoritative for inverse kinds:
  `bijective`, `conditionally_bijective`, `relation_preserving`,
  `projection_only`, `validation_only`, `extraction`, `unknown`;
* extraction intentionally describes an information-discarding result and
  does not claim an inverse;
* reconstruction currently names the impure externally-parsed
  `ExactTable`-consuming reverse path, while `expand` names ordinary
  in-pipeline reverse direction.

Finding F3 is limited to the exclusive `projection` reservation. It does not
reject the useful preference for `extraction`/`selection`, nor does it reopen
the inverse-kind vocabulary.
