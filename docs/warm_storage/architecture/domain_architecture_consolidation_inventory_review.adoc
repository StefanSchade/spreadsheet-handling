The inventory
(link:domain_architecture_consolidation_inventory.adoc[Domain Architecture
Consolidation Inventory]) is a sound basis for the next architecture step. It
is exceptionally well-sourced: every claim independently spot-checked against
its cited ADR, FTR, review, roadmap, or source file in this review matched the
inventory's characterization. It correctly separates evidence from proposed
synthesis from deferred decision, and every non-goal stated at its head
(no Trusted Ingress, no `D-T3`, no package move, no Pydantic adoption, no
guard) was honored.

Two Important findings require correction before the inventory drives guard
promotion or ADR drafting: a guard-classification inconsistency that would
let one specific, unsettled Pydantic-boundary answer be adopted as an
"objectively enforceable now" guard (DACI-REVIEW-F1), and an undisclosed
selection-bias risk in the five-family structural comparison that supplies
most of the evidence for Section 9's "convergent patterns" (DACI-REVIEW-F2).
Neither finding invalidates the inventory's central conclusions; both are
correctable by re-labeling and caveat, not by re-deriving the underlying
analysis.

Acceptance does not adopt every proposal inside the inventory. It confirms
that the inventory correctly distinguishes evidence, proposed synthesis, and
deferred decisions, and gives a safe basis for Step 1 of its own Section 18
sequence.

== Findings

=== DACI-REVIEW-F1 (Important) -- Pydantic guard candidate is miscategorized as decision-independent

*Section:* 15 (guard candidates, Class A), amplified by Section 17 ("safe to
standardize now" -> "Architecture guard class A candidates ... objectively
checkable today, no design work required").

*Evidence:* Section 15 lists "a guard that `domain/` never imports
`pydantic`" as Class A, alongside two other candidates in the same row: (2)
extending the existing `test_package_public_api_boundaries.py` pattern to
new family packages, and (3) a guard that `core/scalar_values.py` stays
unimported by `io_backends/`. Candidates (2) and (3) were independently
verified as already-true, already-evidence-backed current-state facts --
(3) is directly stated in `scalar_values.py`'s own docstring ("No adapter,
CLI, or domain transformation is wired to call it by this slice") and
confirmed by grep to hold today. The pydantic guard is different in kind:
it operationalizes one specific answer (close to the task framing's
Formulation A, "Pydantic may exist only outside `domain/`") to a question
Section 4 explicitly frames as requiring a future ADR ("a recommendation
for a future ADR to test, not a decision") and Section 13 explicitly warns
against deciding speculatively ("writing it purely speculatively risks
exactly the kind of premature authority-granting `ADR-TRUSTED-RUNTIME-AND-
POLICY-AUTHORITY`'s own non-goals list was careful to avoid"). Section 17
then folds this candidate into "safe to standardize now" without carrying
that caveat forward, via the blanket line "Architecture guard class A
candidates (Section 15) -- objectively checkable today, no design work
required."

*Why it matters:* "Objectively checkable" (a mechanism property -- can a
test assert it today) and "adoptable now" (an authority property -- does an
accepted decision exist for the test to protect) are being treated as one
axis. They are not. Per the task framing's own distinction, the load-
bearing question is not "is Pydantic syntactically outside `domain/`" but
"which semantics may Pydantic own" -- and the codebase does not currently
answer that cleanly in Pydantic's favor being excluded from `domain/` on
principle: `domain/helper_policies.py::configure_lookup_helpers` already
performs "structural" shape validation (e.g. `missing not in
_VALID_MISSING_MODES`) colocated with semantic policy-production logic,
inside `domain/`, for the same locality reasons Section 11/HR-12 endorses
elsewhere. A guard adopted now, under a "no design work required" label,
would freeze Formulation A before the ADR evaluates Formulation B
("Pydantic may represent structurally validated description/control
models, potentially including domain-adjacent models, but must not define
payload scalar semantics or transformation/domain invariants") against it
-- exactly the "guard that ... prevent[s] future authorized work" the task
framing warns a guard must not be.

*Smallest correction:* In Section 15's table, move the `domain/`-pydantic-
import guard out of Class A into its own disposition ("ADR-gated"), and add
that disposition explicitly to the table -- Section 15's current A/B/C
scheme has no slot for "mechanically checkable today, but protects an
undecided question," which is the actual shape of this candidate. In
Section 17, narrow "Architecture guard class A candidates" to (2) and (3)
only, or add one clause excluding the pydantic candidate from the "no
design work required" claim. If a guard is wanted before the ADR, scope it
to the semantic prohibition the evidence does support today (Section 4.C/D:
no library's coercion may redefine `String`/`Boolean`/`Number`/`Date`/
`DateTime` membership or narrow `scalar_category`'s carriers) rather than a
package-import prohibition, and let the ADR decide whether that semantic
prohibition also implies a location restriction.

=== DACI-REVIEW-F2 (Important) -- Undisclosed selection bias in the five-family structural comparison

*Section:* 9 (family/package structural comparison) and, derivatively,
Section 14's "domain-module composite" row and Section 17's "convergent
patterns" bucket.

*Evidence:* The five families compared (`xref_crosstable`, `grouped_xref`,
`enrich_lookup`, `fk_helpers`, `compact_multiaxis`) are stated as "chosen
for variety." Independently checked: three of the five
(`xref_crosstable`, `enrich_lookup`, `fk_helpers`) are exactly the three
families the Human Readability investigation already reviewed together
(Section 11 states this explicitly). The other two (`grouped_xref`,
`compact_multiaxis`) are direct structural siblings of `xref_crosstable`
within `domain/transformations/` -- the inventory's own Section 5/9 text
says `grouped_xref` and `compact_multiaxis` both compose `xref_crosstable`
(or its primitives) directly. None of the five is drawn from
`domain/validations/`, `domain/extractions/`, `domain/column_roles/`, or
`domain/schema_maintenance/` (the last is sampled only as a deliberate
counterexample, correctly labeled as such, not as a comparison-table
member).

*Why it matters:* Section 9's "convergent patterns already emerging" (a
policy-resolution step happens once and early; validation is inline versus
centralized by reuse scope; `__init__.py` states public/private explicitly
in the two most complex families) are inferred entirely from one lineage
cluster that shares authorship, review history, and likely shared
conventions by construction, not by independent convergence. This does not
make the patterns false, but it weakens the specific evidentiary claim that
they are "convergent" across the Domain layer generally, as opposed to
consistent within one already-related family group. The inventory's own
Section 17 already gates most of this material behind "must wait for the
first full family pilot," which limits practical risk -- but Section 9
itself states the patterns as observed fact without flagging the sampling
frame, and Section 14 treats the domain-module "composite" sense as
real-but-family-pilot-gated using the same two-family evidence base without
naming the lineage relationship either.

*Smallest correction:* Add one sentence to Section 9 (or a footnote to its
comparison table) stating the sampling frame explicitly: three of five
families were already jointly reviewed by the Human Readability
investigation, and the other two are structural siblings of one of those
three within `domain/transformations/`; the "convergent patterns" claim
should be read as within-cluster, not repo-wide, until the family pilot
samples outside this cluster (e.g. `domain/validations/`,
`domain/extractions/`, or `domain/column_roles/`).

=== DACI-REVIEW-F5 (Minor) -- Sequence does not name the roadmap's own "Domain Maintainer Contract" stage

*Section:* 18 (recommended sequence, steps 3-6).

*Evidence:* `roadmap_domain_hardening_sequence.adoc` names a distinct
"Domain Maintainer Contract" stage, positioned after Phase E and before
Phase F, whose Dimension A ("domain module responsibility vocabulary") and
Dimension B ("naming conventions") substantively overlap with the
inventory's step 4 (Domain responsibility/taxonomy vocabulary) and step 5
(Developer Guide conventions). The roadmap states "Completing this phase
[Phase E] unblocks two later stages ... and the Domain Maintainer Contract
stage below," positioning it after Phase E; the inventory's steps 4-5 are
sequenced before Phase E design (step 6). This is plausibly fine --
Dimension C of the DMC stage ("removing defensive introspection once
Trusted Ingress owns the guarantee") is the part that clearly depends on
Phase E, and Dimensions A/B do not obviously need to wait -- but the
inventory's Section 18 never names the DMC stage or states that its steps
4-5 are a bounded subset of it, not a substitute for it.

*Why it matters:* Without this cross-reference, a future maintainer could
treat "Developer Guide conventions added" (inventory step 5) as satisfying
the roadmap's own Domain Maintainer Contract exit criteria, when the
roadmap's exit criteria (a settled, bounded, durable contract document
covering all four dimensions, cited rather than re-derived) are broader
than what Section 17's "safe to standardize now" list promotes.

*Smallest correction:* Add one sentence to Section 18 noting that steps
4-5 are a partial, evidence-backed advance on Domain Maintainer Contract
Dimensions A/B specifically, and that the roadmap's DMC stage (all four
dimensions, including the Phase-E-dependent Dimension C) remains a
separate, not-yet-started piece of work.

=== ADR candidate (task Section 13 of the task framing)

The recommendation to defer drafting until a concrete Pydantic use site
exists is the strongest of the three options offered by the task framing.
The narrower principle ("third-party structural validation must not define
Domain payload semantics") does not fully follow from the three existing
ADRs already accepted -- `ADR-DOMAIN-OWNS-VALIDATION-WRITERS-STAY-DUMB`

ROBUSTNESS` settles *how robust*, and `ADR-VISIBLE-CELL-TYPING-STRING-
SUBSTRATE` settles a narrower cell-typing question; none addresses whether
a specific third-party library's coercion semantics may become the
definition of Domain meaning. A new ADR extracting this narrowly, once a
concrete use site exists, remains the right call. See *DACI-REVIEW-F4*
(Minor) for the one bundling issue found in this section's own scope
statement.
