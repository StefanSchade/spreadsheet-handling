== 2. Overall verdict

The guide is *accepted* as a provisional, non-binding, judgment-based review aid.

It preserves the two independent quality dimensions (macroarchitecture and local
Human Readability), keeps *bounded reconstruction inside sound architecture* as
the target, weighs six costs rather than reader cost alone, keeps observations
distinct from findings and findings distinct from tasks, treats `accept` and
`stop here` as normal successful outcomes, transfers gated findings to their
owner without claiming authority over them, and confines AI guidance to reviewer
behaviour with no Sentinel, orchestration, or auto-refactor stage. It reproduces
the accepted Project Memory decision's rejected-mechanical-rules set faithfully.

No finding grants the guide implementation or contract authority, creates a
mandatory workflow or Sentinel by implication, contradicts current architecture
or public/persisted contracts, presents an unsupported hypothesis as established
fact, or makes ordinary application unsafe or systematically misleading.
Acceptance conditions (0 Blocking, 0 Important) are met. Three Minor refinements
are recorded for the guide's continued provisional life; each is a wording or
navigation improvement, not implementation authority.

== 6. Hidden-normativity assessment

The guide lives under Developer Guide → Code Conventions, a location with
normativity pull. The review tested whether location, title, imperative language,
tables, or process steps make it functionally binding despite the disclaimer.

* *Disclaimer visibility.* A prominent `[NOTE]` block (lines 11–16) and §1
  ("Status and purpose") state provisional/practical/non-binding/judgment-based
  status. Both survive into the built Developer Guide (rendered and inspected).
* *Imperative scope.* "should"/"must"/"reject"/"require" appear with appropriate
  scope. "must be *transferred to that owner*" (§6) is protective, not a code
  mandate; "Reject the change if the only improvement is lower LOC …" (§4) is
  addressed to reviewer judgment; §9 "should"/"should not" are scoped to "An AI
  reviewer".
* *Ten-step sequence not mandatory.* §3 says it is "a way of thinking, *not* a
  formal program required for every small edit", and "A one-line rename with no
  gate needs only steps 1, 5, and 10 in your head." §10 repeats the lightweight
  framing. This is sufficient to keep the process from becoming a ceremony.
* *No new acceptance gate for ordinary refactors.* The guide explicitly routes
  only gate-crossing findings to owners and leaves gate-free renames to the
  before/after test; it does not manufacture sign-off for local edits.

The guide does not accidentally revoke or weaken a higher authority: §1 ranks it
below architecture, public-API, persisted, lifecycle, ownership, and
compatibility contracts.

*Result: functionally non-binding; status unambiguous in the built guide.* The
one adversarial hook (line 42 vs executable source-style guards) is recorded as
GR1-MIN-002, not as hidden normativity in the guide itself.

== 8. Operational-usability assessment

From the *future human maintainer* perspective the guide is usable without first
reconstructing the whole investigation: the `[NOTE]`, §1, and §2 give the frame
in under a page; §3 gives a bounded sequence with an explicit lightweight path;
§4 gives eight bounded question groups; the before/after test in §4 is concrete
and actionable; §6 identifies the right escalation; §7 produces a clear outcome.

From the *adversarial AI agent* perspective the guide resists the enumerated
failure modes: mechanical cleanup (§5 counterexamples + §4 "Reject the change if
the only improvement is …"); every-observation-a-finding (§7); invented
governance (bounded by §6, subject to GR1-MIN-003); authority over
architecture/contracts (§1 subordination); examples-as-universal-rules (§10 "use
its examples as calibration, not as a backlog"); mandatory ceremony (§3/§10
lightweight path); proxy optimisation (before/after reader-question requirement).

Repetition across §4 questions, §5 trade-off table, §8 positive patterns, §9
AI do/don't, and §10 application serves *different reader tasks* (ask vs weigh vs
preserve vs run-as-agent vs apply-day-to-day) rather than creating avoidable
navigation cost. Length is proportionate; no finding is inferred from length.

*Result: usable for both intended audiences; repetition is task-differentiated.*

== 9. Central acceptance-claim assessment

Claim (line 184): "A real improvement removes a reader question or a traversal."

This is a useful operational compression and correctly rejects proxy "wins"
(lower LOC, more types/helpers/comments, uniformity, a better static score,
stylistic taste). The word "traversal" already captures the common
narrowing/locality case: turning a question that requires traversing several
files into one answerable locally *is* removing a traversal, which §4's "what
question is now answerable locally" makes explicit.

The residual concern is only the absoluteness of "removes … a reader question":
some legitimate improvements make an existing question *safer, narrower, more
reliable, or more locally answerable* without eliminating it. Because §4 and the
§5 six-cost model already carry that breadth, qualification is *not required for
acceptance*; it is the Minor wording refinement GR1-MIN-001 (e.g. "removes a
reader question or a traversal, or makes an unavoidable question reliably
answerable locally").

*Result: sound compression; Minor refinement only.*

== 10. Gate-table and governance assessment

The §6 table maps each boundary to a conceptual authority and a required
characterization. It does the *opposite* of granting authority: it states the
finding "does not authorize the change" and must be "transferred to that owner
before any change". The characterization column matches the evidence reviews'
gate inventories (public/interface FTR, persisted-contract FTR,
architecture/lifecycle decision, round-trip and read-old/write-new
characterization for persisted metadata, behavioural characterization for carrier
behaviour). It does not prescribe approvals absent from the current workflow —
those FTR/interface gates already exist (`interfaces_and_gates.adoc`,
`review_instructions.adoc`).

The only issue is presentational (GR1-MIN-003): the "Who must authorize" role
names can read as formal organisational positions in a single-maintainer repo.
The recommended refinement is to frame them as "the authority owning this
contract (via the appropriate FTR/interface gate)", consistent with the already
hedged lifecycle row. The table does not understate characterization for
persisted or carrier behaviour, nor overstate Human Readability authority.

*Result: faithful authority boundaries; Minor framing refinement (GR1-MIN-003).*

== 12. AI-assisted-review and static-analysis boundary

*AI boundary (§9).* The guide defines reviewer behaviour only. The `should` list
(begin from a concrete reader question; distinguish idiom from misleading
construct; bound the search; report positives; label evidence/inference/
hypothesis; allow no-change; transfer architecture findings; avoid generating work
from every observation) and the `should not` list (auto-refactor; infer
unreadability from length; ban an idiom; demand total typing; turn preference into
severity; claim measured benefit; create a mandatory stage) keep it from becoming
a Sentinel implementation, orchestration design, mandatory model role, auto-
refactor stage, autonomous issue generator, or a replacement for architecture/
independent review. Evidence, inference, and hypothesis stay separated; uncertainty
is allowed; context loading is bounded by a concrete reader question; §10 forbids
repository-wide archaeology for every rename.

*Static-analysis boundary (§9 "Relationship to static analysis").* Static tools
supply "signals and guards, not readability verdicts": they may contribute
evidence on cycles, dependency direction, surface drift, duplication, complexity,
and type coverage, but cannot decide coherence, extraction value, vocabulary
truthfulness, ceremonial vs clarifying types, or long-operation locality. Accepted
contextual decisions "may later be frozen as a narrow guard … but only after a
human establishes it" — neither dismissing tools nor implying every contextual
judgment should become a static check.

*Result: boundary correct in both directions; no Sentinel created.*
