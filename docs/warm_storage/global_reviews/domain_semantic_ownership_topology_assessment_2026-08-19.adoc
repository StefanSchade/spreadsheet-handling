== 1. Executive conclusion

There is no canonical Domain family taxonomy today.

The repository has several partially overlapping classifications:

* pipeline-step categories such as `primitive`, `configuration`, `composite`,
  and `infrastructure`;
* current packages and modules;
* metadata producer/consumer groupings;
* documentation clusters such as "Compact Transforms";
* historical consolidation inventories;
* public operation pairs and inverse relationships.

None is an authoritative semantic ownership map for the whole Domain.

The central finding is that *transformation family is too narrow to be the
universal unit of Domain maintenance*. The Domain also contains:

* cross-cutting lifecycle and boundary services;
* independently meaningful lossy or generic operations;
* public composites;
* shared semantic primitives;
* capability-local and cross-capability vocabulary;
* validation capabilities with different owners;
* mixed Domain/output responsibilities;
* historical helpers whose ownership is unclear.

The DMC already has strong general rules: semantic-first analysis, explicit
state and lifecycle, evidence-based compatibility, bounded inventories,
proportional tests, and no mandatory package template. Those rules generalize.
Its governing unit does not. The current family definition cannot cleanly
classify Trusted Ingress, schema maintenance, final cleanup, workbook-view
mapping, output writers, generic frame operations, or shared vocabulary.

The recommended umbrella term for DMC purposes is *durable semantic ownership
unit*. A transformation family is one possible shape of such a unit, not the
universal shape.

Confidence is high on the absence of a canonical taxonomy and the need to
broaden the DMC. Confidence is medium on several individual boundaries,
especially Grouped XRef and the FK/lookup-helper cluster.

== 3. Proposed classification vocabulary

These categories are supported by current evidence. They are provisional
assessment vocabulary, not accepted architecture.

[cols="2,4,4,5",options="header"]
|===
| Category | Inclusion criterion | Exclusion criterion | Examples and ambiguity

| Durable semantic ownership unit
| Owns a coherent user/domain problem, vocabulary, invariants, compatibility
  decisions, or lifecycle.
| Mere package adjacency or helper reuse.
| XRef, Cell Codec, schema maintenance, Trusted Ingress. This is an umbrella
  term, not necessarily a package.

| Transformation family
| Several operations jointly express one semantic capability and normally
  share configuration, invariants, state identity, and lifecycle.
| Shared inverse/helper alone; a composite merely calling two capabilities.
| XRef expand/contract, discriminator split/merge, sparse collapse/expand.

| Capability variant or subcapability
| Shares the same core semantic identity and lifecycle while adding a bounded
  mode or projection.
| Independently usable semantics or independently owned state.
| One possible interpretation of Grouped XRef, not yet established.

| Composite capability
| Public, user-meaningful orchestration that owns composition order,
  temporary-state rules, trace policy, or a composed carrier.
| A convenience alias with no irreducible contract. Its dependencies do not
  become one family.
| Compact Multiaxis, Grouped XRef, flat XRef axis projection.

| Shared semantic primitive
| A narrow Domain mechanism reused by multiple owners for the same semantic
  reason.
| A public workflow, generic technical helper, or merely similar code.
| Physical-label contracts, XRef axis mapping, bounded where predicates,
  shared cell equality.

| Generic Domain operation
| An independently meaningful user-visible tabular operation with Domain-level
  selection or derivation semantics, often lossy and with little lifecycle.
| Pure representation mechanics or an operation already owned by a family.
| Extract, join, pivot. This is a catalog class, not necessarily one family.

| Cross-cutting Domain service or protocol
| Establishes or maintains a Domain-wide boundary, lifecycle transition, or
  metadata protocol across several capabilities.
| A normal forward/reverse transformation.
| Trusted Ingress, schema maintenance, final cleanup, workbook-view identity.

| Domain vocabulary/value surface
| Types or identifiers with stable semantic meaning across one or more
  capabilities, classified as local, cluster-shared, or Domain-wide.
| A central ontology created only to normalize the tree.
| Scalar categories, physical labels, findings, `GroupedMatrix`, column roles.

| Boundary-adjacent mixed responsibility
| A current module combines Domain semantics with filesystem, carrier, or sink
  behavior.
| A proven final ownership category.
| Structured YAML, key/value writer, artifact manifest. This label identifies
  a later review need; it does not design an egress layer.

| Core substrate
| Policy-free representation, carrier, grammar, or mechanism genuinely reused
  across architectural layers.
| Feature-specific semantics merely because implementation is pure.
| `ExactTable`, `HeaderGrid`, formula intent, generic paths/flatten mechanics.

| Historical sediment or ambiguous
| Code whose semantic owner cannot be recovered confidently from current use
  and contracts.
| Anything with a demonstrated coherent owner.
| Parts of `transformations.helpers`; possibly `core.refs`.

| Capability cluster
| A non-owning navigation or planning umbrella for adjacent capabilities.
| Must not own compatibility, provenance, or lifecycle.
| "XRef cluster" and "reference-helper cluster". "Compact Transforms" is at
  most historical/navigation vocabulary.
|===

The repository's semantic model correctly treats semantic sets as overlapping.
The categories above classify ownership role, not every property. A capability
may simultaneously be public, composite, persistent-state-consuming, and
render-adjacent.

== 6. Shared inverse and shared primitive rules

=== Evidence for same-family ownership

Several of these should normally be present together:

. The operations solve the same user/domain problem.
. They share one semantic identity, not merely a data shape.
. Their configuration fields express the same decisions and conflict rules.
. They enforce the same core invariants.
. They create and consume the same owned state or provenance.
. They jointly form a forward/reverse, create/consume, or
  produce/cleanup lifecycle.
. A compatibility change in one normally requires coordinated decisions in
  the others.
. A compact user-facing reference can explain them as one capability without
  first explaining another independent capability.

=== Evidence that is insufficient by itself

The following do not establish one family:

* same package or directory;
* same registry subtype;
* same documentation chapter;
* same helper or Core function;
* same result shape;
* one operation calling another;
* one inverse being capable of processing outputs from several producers;
* a common metadata container such as `_meta.derived`;
* purity, statelessness, or absence of metadata.

=== Shared inverse

A shared inverse supports one family when it reconstructs state created
specifically under one owned identity and provenance contract, and all forward
variants intentionally feed that same lifecycle.

It does not establish one family when the inverse is generic, different
producers retain distinct configuration/provenance, reconstruction uses a live
external mapping rather than producer-owned state, or a composite merely
delegates reconstruction to another owner.

=== Shared primitive

A primitive can be shared without collapsing consumers when it has a narrow,
stable semantic contract; identical accepted/rejected vocabulary for each
consumer; no ownership of the consumers' public lifecycle; and no need to know
which composite or adapter eventually uses the result.

XRef axis mapping and physical-label validation meet this test. The bounded
`_where_predicates` engine meets it within a smaller derivation cluster.

=== Shared provenance and configuration

Shared provenance is stronger evidence than shared code, but namespace sharing
is insufficient. Common-family ownership requires common producer identity,
staleness rules, reconstruction authority, cleanup behavior, and compatibility
decisions.

`_meta.derived` is a protocol/channel. FK `helper_columns` and lookup
`enrich_lookup` entries under it do not automatically become one family.

=== Composite ownership

A composite owns only irreducible composition semantics:

* order;
* dependency configuration;
* temporary artifacts;
* trace/provenance suppression or preservation;
* public output identity;
* cross-operation failure and reconstruction behavior.

A composite depending on A and B never, by that fact alone, owns A or B.

== 8. Domain versus Core

A more usable criterion than "Core is pure/stateless" is:

[quote]
____
Core owns context-free representation, carrier, grammar, and mechanism
contracts that are stable across several architectural layers. Domain owns
spreadsheet-handling meaning: user-visible semantic choices, feature
configuration, invariants, authority, provenance, reconstruction, and
lifecycle.
____

Consequences:

* Purity and statelessness are common Core qualities, not sufficient placement
  criteria.
* Cross-layer use is stronger evidence for Core than reuse among two related
  Domain capabilities.
* A semantic value type may physically live in Core when it is a stable
  inter-layer carrier while still participating in the Domain language.

[cols="3,8",options="header"]
|===
| Module | Assessment

| `core.exact_table`
| Appropriate Core substrate: backend-neutral exact carrier used across
  rendering and Domain reconstruction.

| `core.header_grid`
| Appropriate Core substrate: format-neutral structural carrier used by
  grouped reconstruction and rendering.

| `core.formulas`
| Appropriate cross-layer declarative Intent contract, provided it remains
  framework-authored Intent rather than ordinary payload.

| `core.scalar_values`
| Technical implementation of the Domain-wide scalar grammar. Physical Core
  placement is defensible because ingress and other layers share it; semantic
  ownership remains part of the Domain language.

| `core.df_build`, `paths`, `flatten`, `unflatten`
| Mostly generic representation/serialization mechanisms. Their behavior
  should still be reviewed when used to implement public Domain operations.

| `core.fk`
| Strong placement-review candidate. `FKDef`, FK patterns, relation maps, and
  helper application are feature-specific and consumed predominantly by the FK
  capability. Purity does not make them semantically weak.

| `core.indexing`
| Borderline. The mechanism is generic, but usage is concentrated around
  FK/MultiIndex behavior. Evidence does not yet justify movement.

| `core.refs`
| Unclear/historical. Few or no current source consumers were found;
  responsibility should be re-established before treating it as durable Core
  architecture.

| Domain cell/where/physical-label primitives
| Should not move to Core merely for reuse or purity; their contracts encode
  Domain decisions.

| Domain writers
| Their concern is not Core substrate. The question is separation between
  Domain projection and an output/persistence boundary, not movement into Core.
|===

== 9. Package-topology implications

A future package tree can make semantic ownership more visible, but only after
ownership is established.

Useful principles:

* Give stable, independently meaningful ownership units a discoverable path.
* Keep capability-local vocabulary, policy, invariants, provenance, and
  reconstruction near the owner.
* Put composites under their own public owner or an explicitly named
  composition area; do not bury them inside one dependency.
* Keep shared primitives narrow and visibly scoped. "Shared" should name the
  semantic contract, not become a dumping ground.
* Allow cross-cutting services such as ingress and schema maintenance to use
  non-transformation package shapes.
* Allow small capabilities to remain one module.
* Do not require every owner to contain `model.py`, `validation.py`,
  `projection.py`, and `operations.py`.
* Do not make `domain/validations` the permanent home for every function that
  checks something.
* Do not make `domain/transformations` the mandatory home for every
  user-facing Domain capability.

Where paths cannot express everything, explicit dependency/ownership records
should carry composite dependencies, shared vocabulary relationships,
cross-cutting consumers, and metadata producer/consumer relationships.

== 10. Test-ownership implications

A durable capability-ownership dimension would be useful in addition to FTR
markers.

FTR markers answer historical traceability: why and when a test was introduced.
Capability ownership answers who is responsible for the invariant now.

The mapping must not be strictly one-test-to-one-family:

* semantic unit tests normally have one primary owner;
* composite tests have a composite owner and named dependency coverage;
* integration tests may verify boundaries among several owners;
* roundtrip tests often protect cross-cutting lifecycle invariants;
* ingress tests own external-value admission, while family tests retain
  business validity;
* schema-maintenance tests own safe metadata-reference transitions, not every
  referenced feature's meaning.

A future convention should allow a primary owner plus collaborating
capabilities, or an equivalent many-to-many representation. A Compact test
exercising XRef and Cell Codec must not become evidence that all three form one
family.

No marker or directory redesign is needed before the DMC. The immediate need is
for the DMC to distinguish historical traceability from durable semantic
ownership.

== 11. User and LLM reference implications

The proposed boundaries pass the compact-reference test better than a universal
family model.

A stabilized primitive/family reference can describe:

* purpose;
* public operations;
* inverse or counter-operation;
* configuration;
* invariants;
* owned metadata/provenance;
* pipeline placement;
* output limitations.

A composite reference additionally needs composed capabilities, order,
internal versus public frames, trace policy, and a statement of which semantics
remain owned by its dependencies.

A generic operation such as extract or join does not need invented lifecycle or
inverse sections.

A cross-cutting service such as ingress, schema maintenance, or cleanup needs a
lifecycle seam, producer/consumer roles, admitted or updated state, failure and
atomicity behavior, and explicitly excluded responsibilities.

A shared primitive normally needs maintainer documentation, not a public
pipeline reference.

== 12. DMC gap analysis

=== MUST address before DMC acceptance

. Replace "family" as the universal Domain unit with a broader ownership-unit
  concept, while retaining family for coherent transformation lifecycles.
. Define family, composite, shared semantic primitive, generic Domain
  operation, cross-cutting service, vocabulary surface, and Core substrate
  sufficiently to prevent mechanical package-based classification.
. Add explicit rules for shared inverse, shared provenance, shared
  configuration, and shared implementation. None alone establishes common
  ownership.
. Require each Domain production module to have a provisional semantic owner
  or explicit `ambiguous/sediment` disposition.
. State that metadata/lifecycle rules apply to all ownership units, not only
  transformation families.
. Do not pre-decide that FK helpers and lookup enrichment are one family. Their
  exact relationship belongs to a bounded pilot Cycle 0.
. Recognize composites as independent public capabilities without allowing them
  to absorb their dependencies.
. Recognize ingress, schema maintenance, cleanup, workbook-view identity, and
  boundary-adjacent output responsibilities as DMC-relevant topology.
. Establish a semantic Domain/Core criterion and reject purity/statelessness as
  sufficient evidence.

=== Should be incorporated but can remain lightweight

* A single provisional Domain capability census before the first pilot.
* Confidence and alternative-interpretation fields for disputed boundaries.
* Test ownership distinct from FTR traceability.
* Reference-document variants for families, composites, generic operations,
  and cross-cutting services.
* Registry guidance stating that step category and semantic owner are different
  axes.
* Package-path guidance favoring visible ownership without a universal package
  template.
* An explicit statement that a capability cluster is non-owning.

=== Should remain local to later Cycle 0 work

* Whether Grouped XRef is a subfamily or sibling composite.
* Whether Legend Blocks is independent vocabulary, Cell-Codec-owned vocabulary,
  or a shared metadata service.
* Whether FK helpers and lookup enrichment share a higher-level owner.
* Detailed public-surface consolidation.
* Exact metadata precedence and compatibility decisions.
* Exact package moves.
* Whether derived-column cleanup becomes its own protocol package.
* Historical modes such as Cell Codec's code-row behavior.

=== Explicitly out of DMC scope

* redesigning Trusted Ingress;
* designing an Egress subsystem;
* moving Domain or Core modules;
* refactoring a family;
* creating a universal metadata ontology;
* making registries a runtime rule engine;
* redesigning the full test suite;
* consumer migrations;
* resolving every ambiguous module before the first pilot.

=== Need for a global census

A global census should precede the first individual family cycle, but it must be
deliberately smaller than the Phase-E census machinery.

The needed census is one static orientation table containing only:

* current module or public operation;
* provisional semantic owner;
* ownership-unit kind;
* important dependency/composite edges;
* metadata root, if any;
* confidence;
* unresolved alternative.

It should not contain full behavior, type, consumer, test, or migration matrices
for every capability. Those remain local to Cycle 0.

== 13. Counterexamples and unresolved questions

=== Counterexamples to a neat family taxonomy

* Schema maintenance is independently meaningful and lifecycle-rich but has no
  inverse transformation-family shape.
* Trusted Ingress is a Domain capability implemented as an automatic macro
  boundary, not a public pipeline transformation.
* Final cleanup has several feature producers and one cross-cutting consumer;
  assigning it to one producer family would be wrong.
* Extract, join, and pivot are legitimate Domain operations despite being lossy
  and mostly stateless.
* `validate_columns` sits under validations but writes workbook constraint
  intent rather than validating canonical data.
* Cell Codec and XRef share Compact as a consumer but remain independently
  usable and semantically distinct.
* Sparse Defaults reads XRef metadata without becoming part of XRef.
* Axis Mapping has no public workflow but is a real Domain semantic primitive.
* `core.fk` is pure and reusable at function level but semantically
  feature-specific.
* `GroupedMatrix` is a controlled carrier owned by a composite capability; not
  every carrier belongs in Core.
* Shared `_meta.derived` placement does not make FK and lookup helper production
  one family.
* `transformations.helpers` appears to contain multiple responsibilities; a
  current module cannot safely be assumed to be one owner.

=== Unresolved questions

. Is Grouped XRef best governed as an XRef subfamily or as an independent
  composite with XRef as a dependency?
. Is Legend Blocks an independent vocabulary capability, Cell-Codec-owned
  vocabulary, or a shared metadata service?
. Does the FK/lookup cluster have a useful common public abstraction beyond
  "derived display helper," or would that obscure different relation and
  lookup semantics?
. Should derived-column policy own a general derived-column lifecycle protocol,
  or remain a coordinated cleanup consumer?
. Are `core.indexing` and `core.refs` genuinely cross-layer substrate or
  historical placement?
. Where should the semantic projection portion of structured output end and
  sink publication begin?
. Is graph validation a specialization of reference validation or an
  independent validation owner sharing findings?
. How much current column-role vocabulary remains durable after role-driven
  projection was removed?
. Should XRef axis projection be called a composite or a variant/subfamily?
  Both remain plausible because it has a public inverse pair but delegates base
  matrix semantics.
. Which direct Domain functions are intentionally supported Python APIs rather
  than merely importable implementation surfaces?

== 14. Recommended next lifecycle step

The smallest next step is a *bounded DMC pre-acceptance topology ratification
slice*.

It should do only three things:

. adopt or revise the ownership-unit vocabulary;
. record the lightweight whole-Domain census described above;
. select the first pilot subject without pre-deciding its internal boundary,
  especially whether FK helpers and lookup enrichment are one family or
  collaborating capabilities.

The chosen pilot's Cycle 0 can then perform the detailed behavioral, consumer,
metadata, and test inventory.

This ordering gives the DMC enough global orientation to classify the whole
Domain while avoiding another large census and without beginning a refactor.
