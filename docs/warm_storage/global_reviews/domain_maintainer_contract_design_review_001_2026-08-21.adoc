== Overall verdict

*MATERIAL REVISION REQUIRED.*

The revised DMC is a substantial, well-evidenced correction of the prior
design. It correctly absorbs almost every blocking and important finding from
the independent topology-assessment review (`..._review_001.adoc`): it
acknowledges the existing Domain Contracts Registry instead of re-declaring a
taxonomy over it, it drops the seven/nine-category ownership ontology, it
removes the whole-module census as an acceptance prerequisite, it treats
"composite," "shared primitive," and "layer" as properties rather than
competing owner kinds, it restates the Domain/Core criterion as a hypothesis
rather than a replacement rule, and it keeps the FK/lookup pilot topology
genuinely open. These are durable, load-bearing corrections and this review's
first job is to protect them.

One finding is severe enough by itself to block promotion as written:
`DMC-REV-001`, the near-total absence of *local/algorithmic Human
Readability* (bounded reconstruction cost, per
`03_human_readability_review.adoc`) from the DMC's actual completion
procedure, despite the DMC's own section 1 explicitly claiming to carry the
roadmap's readability dimension. A handful of further IMPORTANT findings
(materiality left undefined for scope-narrowing, general-handoff conflict
ownership, family-locality's "strong internal coupling" criterion diluted
relative to its evidence base, and pilot/current-state content mixed into
what is meant to become a permanent contract) do not individually block
acceptance but should be corrected in the same pass, because each is a
concrete, cheap, DMC-local fix and together they materially improve
executable usability.

Findings by severity: 1 BLOCKER, 4 IMPORTANT, 3 MINOR, 2 NOTE.

== Findings

=== DMC-REV-001 -- Local/algorithmic Human Readability is missing from the executable procedure

*Severity:* BLOCKER.

*DMC section(s):* Section 1 (`status-authority`, the dimensions-A-D claim);
Section 9 (`source-topology`); Section 11 (`documentation`); Section 12
(`completion`, all 12 items); Section 2's non-goal list (line ~100).

*Claim challenged:* That this DMC design carries the roadmap's readability
dimension (Dimension C) and that its completion procedure is sufficient to
protect Local Human Readability during a bounded hardening cycle.

*Evidence:* The roadmap's Domain Maintainer Contract section names four
dimensions the DMC stage must settle, dimension C being: "Human-readability
rules, evidence-derived from the three-family investigation and
`03_human_readability_review.adoc`, not invented fresh: top-down readable
algorithm flow; resolving raw option/state bags early; explicit
ownership/mutation; semantic rather than textual abstraction; removing
defensive introspection once Trusted Ingress owns the guarantee...; comments
that explain invariants and boundaries rather than restating Python syntax."
The DMC design's own section 1 asserts: "The roadmap's DMC dimensions A-D
remain the structural, naming, readability, and objective-guard subset of
this contract" -- a direct claim that dimension C is delivered.

A full-text search of the DMC design for readability content
(`grep -ni "readab"`) returns exactly five hits: the section-1 claim above; a
non-goal ("avoiding subjective readability preferences becoming hard CI
gates"); one mention of "machine-readable" (unrelated); and two mentions of
*Reference Readability* in section 11/item 11 of the completion gate. The
Human Readability Review Guide (`03_human_readability_review.adoc`) is never
cited by title or path anywhere in the DMC. None of the following, all
central to that guide, appear anywhere in the DMC: the before/after
reader-question test; the review sequence (state the reader question,
establish local responsibility, inspect only what the question requires,
record positive structures, compare reconstruction cost against the six
trade-off costs); the contract/ownership gates table; the explicit rejection
list (no mandatory helper extraction, no length/parameter-count limits, no
extraction merely to shorten a function); or the "positive patterns to
preserve" discipline.

The DMC's *completion gate* (section 12, the executable per-cycle procedure)
has 12 items. Item 7 covers source/package topology (family/repository
locality -- level A in this review's framework). Item 11 covers Reference
Readability (documentation/citation hygiene -- level C). No item addresses
level B: whether a reader can reconstruct a touched code path's current
semantics, lifecycle, validation/side-effect order, and non-local reasons
without avoidable archaeology through callers, consumers, tests, metadata,
and history. The only level-B-adjacent content anywhere in the DMC is section
8.1's instruction not to reintroduce Trusted-Ingress-owned defensive checks --
which is exactly one of the roadmap's six dimension-C sub-bullets, not the
whole dimension.

The Human Readability Review Guide itself states the reason this omission
matters: "Macroarchitecture (package boundaries, dependency direction,
ownership, contracts) and local Human Readability are complementary and
neither subsumes the other. Sound architecture can still leave a maintainer
paying an avoidable local reconstruction cost." Section 9's family/package
topology and section 11's Reference Readability are precisely the two
dimensions the guide says do *not* substitute for level B.

*Assessment/inference:* This is not a case of the DMC correctly deferring to
another owning document by reference (the pattern it uses successfully for
Trusted Ingress in section 8.1, and for the Domain Contracts Registry in
section 4.1). It is a genuine content gap: the DMC claims coverage it does
not deliver, and the gap sits exactly where the roadmap put it as a named,
mandatory dimension, established from a completed three-family investigation
whose specific, hard-won lessons (do not shorten functions by mechanical
extraction; preserve validation-order locality; resolve option bags at the
right boundary, not indiscriminately) are the empirical antidote to the most
common failure mode of an AI-assisted structural hardening pass.

*Confidence:* High. The gap is demonstrated by direct text search, not
inference, and the roadmap's dimension-C wording is unambiguous about what
was supposed to land here.

*Concrete failure mode:* A future hardening Cycle 0 (for example the FK/lookup
pilot) proceeds to Family-cycle-4-equivalent structural work, extracts several
small helpers from a cohesive validation-order-sensitive function "to reduce
size," or resolves a raw option bag late instead of at the real boundary. Every
item in the DMC's 12-item completion gate can be satisfied -- scope is clear,
handoffs are recorded, tests are green, docs are updated, Reference
Readability holds -- while the change reproduces exactly the harm
`03_human_readability_review.adoc`'s worked examples (FK's raw-options bag,
XRef's cohesive long operations, Enrich Lookup's resolved policy) were used to
argue against. An independent reviewer who does not personally know to apply
the separate, non-binding guide has no DMC-level prompt to do so.

*Smallest reasonable correction:* Add one completion-gate item, proportionally
scoped like item 11 ("Reference Readability applies..."), of the form:
"Code paths the change touches satisfy Local Human Readability
(`03_human_readability_review.adoc`): a reader can reconstruct current
semantics, validation/side-effect order, and non-local reasons without
avoidable archaeology; apply the guide's lightweight before/after
reader-question test, not a mandatory full review sequence, for small
changes." Cross-reference this new item from section 9 (to make explicit that
topology and local readability are separate dimensions) and from section 11
(to make explicit that Reference Readability does not substitute for it).

*Disposition:* DMC-local (a completion-gate item and two cross-references);
does not require any new document, taxonomy, or mechanism -- the owning guide
already exists and is explicitly designed to be applied proportionally.

=== DMC-REV-002 -- "Material" handoff/scope is asserted but never operationally defined

*Severity:* IMPORTANT.

*DMC section(s):* Section 2 (`bounded-scope`, "a locally small edit that
changes persisted authority or a material handoff is not small
architecturally"); Section 4 (`cycle-zero`, "material edges required to
change it safely"; "material handoffs and adjacent owners that must
coordinate"); Section 6.1 (`interaction-state`, "For each material handoff
the bounded change touches..."); Section 12 item 2.

*Claim challenged:* That the DMC's boundedness is safe against a maintainer
choosing an artificially narrow scope and thereby classifying a genuinely
coupled adjacent capability as out of scope.

*Evidence:* The word "material" gates four separate load-bearing rules
(what belongs in Cycle 0's working inventory, which handoffs get a full
interaction-contract writeup, what disqualifies an edit from being "small",
and what the completion gate checks). The only definitional content offered
is circular: section 2 says a handoff that is "material" is "not small
architecturally" -- material is defined by its consequence, not by any
independent test. Section 6.1 lists *typical* seams (feature -> final
cleanup, composite -> primitive, and so on) as examples, not a test for novel
cases. The independent topology-assessment review (`..._review_001.adoc`,
DSO-REV-009) named exactly this as the missing first-class dimension and
recommended "a scoped pilot requirement to identify material handoffs" --
which the DMC added -- but did not itself supply a materiality test, so the
gap survives into this design.

*Assessment/inference:* Cycle 0's instruction to identify "material handoffs
and adjacent owners that must coordinate" as part of the working inventory
*does* structurally block the most naive version of the evasion (declaring
scope narrowly and then never looking outward at all), because identifying
adjacent material handoffs is required regardless of how narrowly the primary
subject is drawn. The residual risk is narrower but real: without any
operational criterion, a maintainer under time pressure can honestly-but-
wrongly judge a real coupling as "not material," and nothing in the DMC gives
an independent reviewer (completion-gate item 12) a shared vocabulary to
contest that judgment other than re-deriving the whole analysis themselves.

*Confidence:* Medium-high. The failure mode is plausible and recurring (every
Cycle 0 faces this question), but the DMC's design intentionally prefers
judgment over mechanism elsewhere (matching `03_human_readability_review.adoc`'s
own philosophy), so the absence of a formula is partly by design, not pure
oversight.

*Smallest reasonable correction:* Add one sentence near section 6.1's typical-
seams list, of the form: "A handoff is material if a plausible single-sided
change on either side -- change of ordering, authority, mutation, format, or
error/retry behavior -- would silently break the other side's current
behavior or compatibility surface without the other side's owner reviewing
it." This gives the completion-gate reviewer (item 12) a falsifiable question
to ask, without turning it into a checklist or registry.

*Disposition:* DMC-local (one sentence).

=== DMC-REV-003 -- Family locality's positive criterion is weaker than its evidence base; "local comprehension" alone does not exclude a dumping ground

*Severity:* IMPORTANT.

*DMC section(s):* Section 3.1 (`working-model`); cross-reference Section 3.3.

*Claim challenged:* That section 3.1's family-locality principle, on its own
wording, prevents a family from becoming a dumping ground for convenience.

*Evidence:* Section 3.1's positive test is: "A maintainer should be able to
understand and change a family mostly from within its package or bounded
scope." The consolidated topology assessment's corresponding language (its
Executive Conclusion) is stronger and two-sided: a useful boundary has
"strong internal semantic and metadata coupling" *and* "substantial
family-internal state communication that can be understood locally" *and*
"few, narrow, explicit, understandable external relationships." The DMC keeps
the external-narrowness half and the internal-comprehensibility-from-inside
half, but drops the explicit positive requirement that internal members
*share* meaningful semantic/metadata coupling with each other.

*Assessment/inference:* "A maintainer can understand and change the family
from mostly within its bounded scope" is close to tautological for any family
large enough to be self-contained -- merging two weakly related capabilities
into one package trivially satisfies it, because everything needed is now,
by construction, inside the boundary. This is precisely the failure mode
section 13 explicitly guards against for the FK/lookup pilot by name
("derived-column policy is owned by either producer" must not be assumed;
"shared `_meta.derived` placement... prove common ownership" must not be
assumed) and that Review 001 of the topology assessment flagged repeatedly
(DSO-REV-006: "the report... fragments or groups operations according to
whichever property is most visible"). Section 3.3's negative list ("shared
implementation, inverse, helper, primitive, metadata namespace... common
use, or composition" do not alone prove common ownership) and its positive
question ("whether a split or merge improves semantic and metadata
locality") do the real protective work -- but that protection lives in 3.3,
one subsection away from 3.1's own stated purpose, and 3.1 itself does not
carry it.

*Confidence:* Medium-high. This is a textual weakening relative to the
assessment, not a demonstrated runtime harm; its consequence depends on
whether a future maintainer reads 3.1 in isolation or together with 3.3 and
section 13's example.

*Concrete failure mode:* A future Cycle 0 for a different pilot (not FK, where
the DMC already gives explicit per-pilot guardrails in section 13) merges two
capabilities that share only co-location and common consumers, justified as
"a maintainer can understand and change this mostly from within the merged
package" -- true, but only because the merge itself created that locality,
not because the two capabilities share real semantic or metadata coupling.

*Smallest reasonable correction:* Add one clause to 3.1's quoted principle:
"...a semantic ownership and comprehension boundary in which a maintainer
can understand and change a capability mostly locally *because its members
share substantial semantic and metadata coupling, not merely co-location or
common use (see 3.3)*."

*Disposition:* DMC-local (one clause plus an explicit cross-reference to
3.3).

=== DMC-REV-004 -- General material-handoff checklist omits conflict-resolution ownership outside projections

*Severity:* IMPORTANT.

*DMC section(s):* Section 6.1 (`interaction-state`); contrast Section 6.3.

*Claim challenged:* That section 6.1's handoff checklist (producer, consumer/
executor, authoritative state and mutation/copy ownership, ordering and
consumption/termination, failure/atomicity, compatibility, coordinated-change
responsibility) is a sufficient general list for material handoffs.

*Evidence:* Section 6.3 asks, specifically for projections, "how conflicts
are detected and resolved" -- but section 6.1's general list has no
equivalent item. The topology assessment's own boundary table records final
cleanup, a canonical non-projection cross-owner interaction that section 6.1
lists by name as a "typical seam," as explicitly owning "conflict detection,
execution, and consumption" among multiple feature-owned producers (topology
assessment, "Final cleanup" row: "Multiple feature producers emit commands;
one carrier-neutral consumer owns conflict detection, execution, and
consumption before persistence"). HRS-006 (historical signal inventory)
independently preserves the same invariant for handoffs generally, not only
projections.

*Assessment/inference:* Conflict-resolution ownership is a real,
independently-evidenced property of general material handoffs (final
cleanup being the clearest example), not only of projections. Confining it
to section 6.3 risks a Cycle 0 treating "who resolves a conflict between two
producers converging on one consumer" as answered only when the scope
happens to contain a projection, and skipping it for a pure multi-producer/
one-consumer handoff like cleanup.

*Confidence:* High on the omission; medium on how often it would actually
change a Cycle-0 outcome, since an attentive reviewer would likely surface it
under "authoritative state" or "coordinated-change responsibility" anyway.

*Smallest reasonable correction:* Add "conflict detection and resolution
ownership, where more than one producer or path can converge on one
consumer" to section 6.1's bullet list, mirroring 6.3's existing projection-
specific phrasing.

*Disposition:* DMC-local (one bullet).

=== DMC-REV-005 -- Sections 13-14 mix durable procedure with current-cycle-specific content that must not promote verbatim

*Severity:* IMPORTANT.

*DMC section(s):* Section 13 (`first-pilot`); Section 14 (`open-decisions`).

*Claim challenged:* That the DMC design, as currently organized, is ready to
become a permanent Developer Guide contract understandable without reading
this design FTR or the current backlog state.

*Evidence:* Section 13 makes present-tense claims about the current backlog
("The FK/reference-helper area remains a strong leading pilot candidate
because it has current maintenance pain...") and gives FK/lookup-specific
non-assumptions. Section 14 lists six "genuine open decisions," several of
which are backlog-cycle-specific (whether a parent FTR shell is needed; the
exact FK/Lookup boundary "deferred explicitly to pilot Cycle 0"). None of
this is procedure a future maintainer applies to an arbitrary bounded scope;
it is evidence and state specific to *this* moment in the roadmap.

*Assessment/inference:* This is not a defect in the current design artifact
-- the design correctly labels itself provisional and not yet promoted, and
section 14 itself frames these as questions "for the fresh DMC review,
promotion work, or the later bounded pilot," which already anticipates that
this content will not survive verbatim. But because the review gate (section
15) explicitly names "permanent promotion" as one of the two events this
review gates, this review must say plainly: a promotion pass that copies
sections 1-12 and 15 into the permanent Developer Guide location while
dropping sections 13-14 (routing their content to the roadmap and to the
pilot's own Cycle-0 artifact instead) produces the self-contained document
the promotion lifecycle requires. Promoting sections 13-14 unedited would
leave a "permanent" maintainer contract that references one specific pilot
by name and one specific backlog moment, which is exactly what
`interfaces_and_gates.adoc`-style permanent documents in this repository do
not do.

*Confidence:* High that the separation is needed; this is a structural
observation about promotion readiness, not a claim that today's design is
wrong to contain this content now.

*Smallest reasonable correction:* No DMC edit needed now. Record, as
promotion-time guidance, that sections 13-14 route to the roadmap /
pilot-local Cycle-0 artifact at promotion time rather than the permanent
Developer Guide location; sections 1-12 and 15 are the durable-procedure
core.

*Disposition:* Promotion-time / canonical-doc-placement follow-up, not a
correction to this design artifact.

=== DMC-REV-006 -- "Composite contract" heading risks re-reading composite as an owner kind

*Severity:* MINOR.

*DMC section(s):* Section 5.1 (`Composite contract`); contrast Section 3.2.

*Claim challenged:* That section 5.1 is consistent with 3.2's rule that
"composite" is a dependency-relationship property, not a competing owner
category.

*Evidence:* Section 3.2 explicitly states: "Properties such as composite,
shared primitive, public/internal, cross-layer, confidence, or historical
sediment are not competing owner categories." Section 5.1 is titled
"Composite contract" and describes what "a composite" "owns" and "must not"
own, phrased as though the composite itself were the ownership unit.

*Assessment/inference:* Read together with 3.2 and section 6.1's use of
"composite -> primitive" as a relationship-edge label, the intended meaning
is almost certainly "when the scope under review is a family/capability that
plays a composite role, here is what that role owns versus what it must
leave to its dependencies" -- consistent with HRS-007's preserved invariant
and Review 001's DSO-REV item 7 agreement. But the heading and phrasing alone
invite exactly the axis-conflation 3.2 warns against, for a reader who skims
section headings rather than the full text.

*Confidence:* Medium; this is a readability/precision concern about the
DMC's own text, not a demonstrated contradiction.

*Smallest reasonable correction:* Retitle to "Composite role contract" or
add one clarifying sentence: "This section describes the composition-specific
facts a scope owns when it plays a composite role, not a new owner category."

*Disposition:* DMC-local (heading/one sentence).

== Required Human-Readability conclusion

*Does the revised DMC adequately incorporate the durable contract from
`03_human_readability_review.adoc`?* No, not at the level that matters most.

Distinguishing the three levels this review was asked to test:

*A. Family/repository locality* -- where in the source tree a maintainer must
look. Adequately covered by section 9 (source/package topology) and, for
ownership rather than physical placement, by section 3.1/3.3. This level is
in reasonably good shape, subject to DMC-REV-003's narrower wording concern.

*B. Local/algorithmic bounded reconstruction* -- how much avoidable
navigation through helpers, callers, consumers, state bags, tests, metadata,
or history is required to reconstruct what a local code path means and in
what order it acts, per `03_human_readability_review.adoc`'s own definition
("bounded reconstruction... without avoidable archaeology"). *This level is
essentially absent from the DMC's executable procedure.* It is not listed in
the completion gate (section 12), not named as an owning concern anywhere in
section 11's documentation-ownership table, and the guide itself is never
cited. Section 8.1's single point about not reintroducing Trusted-Ingress-
owned defensive checks is real but is only one of the roadmap's six named
dimension-C sub-bullets, not the dimension.

*C. Reference readability* -- whether permanent code/docs can be understood
without resolving FTR IDs, review findings, or Git-history archaeology.
Explicitly and correctly covered: section 11's completion property and
completion-gate item 11 both track the guide's "Reference Readability"
vocabulary closely.

Level A and level C do not substitute for level B, for the reason the guide
itself states: they are complementary, independent dimensions, and "sound
architecture can still leave a maintainer paying an avoidable local
reconstruction cost." Knowing where a capability's package lives (A) does not
tell a reader whether the function they open there preserves validation
order, resolves its option bag at the right boundary, or extracts helpers for
a real semantic seam rather than merely to shorten itself. Knowing that
permanent docs cite no dangling FTR IDs (C) says nothing about the code's own
local reconstructability. The gap is real, it is exactly the level the
roadmap's Dimension C was created to protect, and the correction
(`DMC-REV-001`) is small: one completion-gate item citing the existing,
already-proportional guide, not a new mechanism.
