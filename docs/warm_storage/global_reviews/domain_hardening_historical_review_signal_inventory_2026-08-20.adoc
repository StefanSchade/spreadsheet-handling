== 1. Executive conclusion

Substantial durable structural signal survives. Twenty consolidated signals were retained. Most are not arguments for more work; they are explanations of why already-established boundaries and behaviors must not regress.

The recurring themes are:

* semantic ownership and inward dependency direction;
* explicit producer/consumer handoffs, ordering, authority, and failure atomicity;
* Intent versus Resolution at persistence and reconstruction boundaries;
* thin composites that leave primitive semantics with their dependencies;
* package/API discipline based on real semantic seams and compatibility evidence;
* metadata lifecycle, provenance, and schema-maintenance responsibilities;
* contract/registry duplication and the danger of competing authority;
* consumer-visible configuration synchronization and local explainability.

The old reviews will materially help Domain hardening. They contain both debt signals and positive invariants. Several of the most valuable findings are formally resolved: the Domain-to-rendering import, render-input mutation, FK v1/v2 sediment, and Compact temporary-XRef leak. Their lasting value is the guardrail they established, not their ticket state.

The dominant conclusion is therefore not “remove historical complexity.” It is:

____
Simplify only after identifying the current semantic owner, the authoritative state, and the interaction contract; preserve the tested reasons that made prior simplification safe.
____

There is also strong counter-evidence against turning this inventory into another registry or ownership taxonomy. REVIEW-005 demonstrated the cost of parallel vocabularies and duplicated declarations, and the current topology assessment's adversarial review independently warns against repeating that pattern.

=== HRS-001 -- Preserve inward dependency direction

Disposition:: `PRESERVE-INVARIANT`
Themes:: ownership; dependency direction; Domain/Core; architecture guard
Statement:: Domain and Core must not depend on rendering or I/O backends. Backend-neutral semantic intent belongs inward; adapters translate it into carrier-specific mechanics.
Historical sources:: REVIEW-001 `D-001` + `IR-002` in `review-consolidation.adoc`; REVIEW-002 §1.1 and §2 in `REVIEW-002-FINDINGS.adoc`; REVIEW-007 §§2, 5, and 7.
Original problem:: Domain transformations imported `rendering.formulas` because backend-neutral formula specifications lived in the wrong layer.
Durable insight:: Fixing the import site was insufficient; ownership of the value object had to move to the inward layer, and the dependency direction had to become executable.
Why it matters:: Package moves during hardening can accidentally put shared semantic objects back into a downstream layer or introduce a convenience import across the hexagonal boundary.
Current sanity evidence:: `tests/architecture/dependency_guards/test_spreadsheet_boundary_guards.py` still forbids Domain/Core imports of rendering and I/O backends and keeps Core leaf-only.
Confidence:: High.
Later scope:: Every Domain hardening Cycle 0 that moves a shared specification or helper; Domain/Core placement decisions.
Direction:: `PRESERVE / DO NOT REGRESS`.

Statement:: Schema maintenance may update declared references owned by another capability, but it does not own their meaning. Each supported path needs an explicit maintenance policy; unknown or unmaintained paths must not be guessed from naming conventions.
Historical sources:: REVIEW-005 recommended white-box slice and `P2-5`; closure risks `GAP-META-REF-CONVENTION-ESCAPE`; FK lifecycle/closure; Legend `LB-6`; Compact `CM-3` and its correction review.
Original problem:: `meta_refs.py` used key-name frozensets; `helper_policies.lookup` escaped maintenance; Legend/Compact blocked-root tests used shapes that did not match real producer payloads.
Durable insight:: Runtime feature code remains semantic authority. A behavior registry can declare maintenance treatment and join to the metadata owner; tests should compare declaration with real emitted shapes. Blocking/ignoring must be intentional and transactional.
Why it matters:: Domain hardening can add, rename, or remove metadata references that schema maintenance silently misses or falsely claims to protect.
Current sanity evidence:: `meta_reference_surfaces.json` plus architecture tests cover current declared surfaces and join to `meta_registry.yaml`; broader convention escapes remain explicitly bounded risk.
Confidence:: High for known paths; unknown for future capability roots.
Later scope:: The affected capability's Cycle 0 plus schema-maintenance impact check; never a global root census by default.
Direction:: Revalidate locally; `FIX / HARDEN` only where a real emitted path escapes.

=== HRS-019 -- Reduce duplicated configuration authority without hiding distinct boundaries

Disposition:: `STRUCTURAL-WEAKNESS-CANDIDATE`
Themes:: configuration authority; consumer contract; interaction/handoff; maintainability/readability
Statement:: A model fact should have one maintained authoring source where forward and reverse consumers mean the same thing; distinct render/readback decisions should remain explicit rather than inferred or hidden behind a macro.
Historical sources:: REVIEW-006 `F1`, `F2`, `F3`, and `F6`; Configuration Simplification assessment; REVIEW-001 `IR-012` as the earlier two-dialect example.
Original problem:: Consumers duplicated reference-validation rules two or three times and synchronized XRef/frame-list pairs mentally. At the same time, the healthy baseline was a short explicit built-in pipeline, and one-consumer macro proposals remained unjustified.
Durable insight:: Deduplicate shared schema facts, not distinct boundary choices. Prefer a named shared source or explicit argument over a new DSL/registry; keep short, inspectable pipelines and plugin escape hatches for genuine local policy.
Why it matters:: Public-surface consolidation can either leave silent drift or overcorrect into an opaque configuration framework.
Current sanity evidence:: This pass did not inspect sibling consumers. REVIEW-006 remains the strongest bounded fleet evidence; Project Memory retains the duplicated-rules finding as current.
Confidence:: High historically; current consumer details require local confirmation.
Later scope:: Validation/configuration or FK/reference-helper pilot with maintained-consumer evidence.
Direction:: `FIX / HARDEN` locally; preserve explicit distinct handoffs.

=== HRS-020 -- One shared metadata shape needs explicit cross-consumer policy

Disposition:: `LOCAL-CYCLE-EVIDENCE`
Themes:: ownership; validation; metadata/lifecycle; interaction/handoff; consumer contract
Statement:: When several owners consume one authored metadata shape, shared structure alone does not establish shared semantics; divergent validation, error, and fallback policies must be either deliberately unified or explicitly documented as consumer-specific.
Historical sources:: Legend Blocks assessment `LB-7`, `OB-1`, and human decisions 1-2; Legend Reader Consolidation `LRCR-001/002`; REVIEW-007's triplicated option-key observation.
Original problem:: Cell Codec, Compact, ValidationPass, and the composer gave different answers for missing entries, duplicate tokens, empty labels, and unknown Legend references. The shared reader correctly owned only the token/group schema walk, not the whole root contract.
Durable insight:: Consolidate the common contract at its narrowest true seam; do not make one consumer's strictness silently normative for every other consumer. Conversely, do not let a single authored value be valid for Domain and invalid only when it reaches rendering without an explicit policy.
Why it matters:: Similar cross-consumer shapes occur in validation, provenance, and presentation metadata; package consolidation can obscure rather than solve the policy decision.
Current sanity evidence:: Legend shape normalization and persistence/readback defects have been hardened, but `FTR-LEGEND-BLOCKS-ENTRY-CONTRACT-P5` remains open; Domain consumers raise for unknown references while ValidationPass still warns/no-ops.
Confidence:: High on historical/current divergence; final desired policy is undecided.
Later scope:: Legend Blocks entry-contract Cycle 0; use as an example, not a global validation framework.
Direction:: Revalidate locally before `FIX / HARDEN`; preserve the narrow shared-reader seam.

== 4. Preserve-invariant shortlist

The highest-value rules the Domain refactor must not erase are:

* `PRESERVE:` Domain/Core dependencies point inward; adapters do not own semantic intent.
* `PRESERVE:` Parsers do not infer canonical business truth from formatting; sanctioned reverse writes remain explicit and narrow.
* `PRESERVE:` persistence keeps Intent and drops Resolution; provenance is evaluated separately.
* `PRESERVE:` reconstruction authority, fallback order, conflict policy, and cleanup order are explicit and test-pinned.
* `PRESERVE:` composite owns composition order and temporary lifecycle; dependencies retain primitive semantics and validation.
* `PRESERVE:` persisted composite state names public reality and is either operative intent or explicitly non-authoritative diagnostics.
* `PRESERVE:` package facades remain stable and cross-family code does not import private internals.
* `PRESERVE:` FK helpers remain derived editing projections with durable declarations, transient provenance, and metadata-driven cleanup.
* `PRESERVE:` failures do not partially publish or mutate caller-owned nested state.
* `PRESERVE:` one maintained declaration owns each contract fact; joins do not become mirrors.

== 5. Structural-weakness shortlist

== 10. Implications for the DMC and Domain hardening

=== Insights the DMC should probably make possible to preserve

* semantic scope and current authority are established before topology changes;
* material producer/consumer handoffs can record authority, ordering, atomicity, and compatibility without assigning the relationship to one node;
* compatibility decisions use current consumers and distinct capability evidence;
* pilots can preserve positive invariants and tests from resolved historical findings;
* package shape remains advisory and proportional.

=== Evidence to consume locally in a pilot

* the relevant `HRS-*` rows from the routing table;
* original cited reviews where the reason matters;
* current runtime/registry/tests for the affected capability;
* maintained-consumer evidence where public/config compatibility is at stake.

=== Things that must not become new global DMC requirements

* the `HRS-*` IDs, themes, or routing groups as a new Domain taxonomy;
* a whole-Domain module census or permanent ownership registry;
* a required package template, module-size limit, dataclass/type hierarchy, or universal import spelling;
* mandatory new metadata classification fields merely because one historical review proposed them;
* global registry completion before the first bounded pilot;
* reopening every old finding or proving current truth for every historical path.

This inventory does not edit or redesign the DMC. Its role is to give later pilots evidence that can be consumed without re-running the historical reviews.

== 11. Recommended use of this artifact

At the start of a family/capability hardening Cycle 0:

. select only the routing row(s) relevant to the bounded subject;
. read the concise `HRS-*` statements and preserve/fix direction;
. follow the original historical sources only where rationale or ambiguity matters;
. verify current runtime, contracts, tests, and maintained consumers locally;
. record which historical rule was preserved, hardened, or found superseded in the cycle's temporary evidence;
. do not promote this inventory into runtime, registry, or permanent taxonomy authority.

Original reviews remain historical evidence. Current runtime behavior, maintained contracts, architecture guards, and supported consumer surfaces remain authoritative. This artifact is a refactor navigation aid, not a source of runtime truth and not a backlog.

It may be archived after the Domain hardening wave. Its success criterion is that later refactors use the architectural learning and then move on, rather than maintaining a second permanent history-derived contract system.
