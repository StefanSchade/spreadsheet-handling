[[domain-maintainer-contract]]
=== Domain Maintainer Contract

The Domain Maintainer Contract (DMC) is the reusable procedure for hardening a
bounded Domain area. It answers one practical question:

[quote]
____
I am about to harden this bounded Domain area. What must I understand, decide,
preserve, simplify, test, document, and review before I call it done?
____

The contract prevents two opposite failures:

* preserving sediment as architecture merely because behavior, naming,
  metadata, configuration, or a public-looking import already exists; and
* destroying a real capability, authority boundary, or cross-owner handoff
  merely because a local source-tree cleanup looks simpler.

The DMC is *broad but bounded*. It covers semantics, authority,
compatibility, state and provenance, material interactions, consumers, tests,
documentation, and structural locality, but applies only to the selected
bounded Domain change. Applicability and risk determine the depth of the
work. A small helper does not need a ceremonial inventory covering every
question below; a locally small edit that changes persisted authority or a
<<dmc-material-handoffs,material handoff>> is not small architecturally.

[[dmc-bounded-scope]]
==== 1. Establish a bounded scope

The subject of a DMC cycle is a *bounded Domain scope*. Depending on the
change, that may be:

* a coherent family;
* a capability or public composite;
* a shared primitive whose stewardship changes;
* a cross-cutting Domain service or protocol;
* a boundary capability;
* a material interaction or handoff; or
* another bounded Domain change surface.

Use existing repository vocabulary where it fits. In particular, retain
*family* for a useful family-shaped scope. Do not invent a universal
replacement owner kind, and do not force Trusted Ingress, schema maintenance,
final cleanup, Workbook View, shared primitives, or public composites into a
false family shape.

The DMC does not require:

* a whole-Domain taxonomy, capability or module census, ownership registry,
  or interaction-contract registry;
* complete population or ratification of the Domain Contracts Registry;
* a universal metadata/state classification, test-ownership marker, package
  skeleton, or one reference document per scope;
* global Domain/Core placement decisions or a Core rename or redefinition;
* redesign of Trusted Ingress, an Egress design, integrity signatures, or a
  sink implementation;
* revalidation of every historical finding or promotion of historical review
  identifiers into permanent vocabulary;
* implementation plans for unrelated families or capabilities; or
* subjective readability preferences becoming hard CI gates.

Use `none` or `not applicable` when a material question was considered and
genuinely does not apply. Do not use proportionality to hide a touched
contract.

[[dmc-working-model]]
==== 2. Keep the reasoning axes separate

===== Family as semantic and metadata locality

Where a family is the useful ownership model, its purpose is pragmatic
divide-and-conquer. It is a semantic ownership and comprehension boundary in
which a maintainer can understand and change a capability mostly locally
because its members share meaningful semantic and/or metadata/lifecycle
coupling, not merely co-location or common use.

[quote]
____
Optimize family boundaries for semantic and metadata locality, not theoretical
taxonomy. A maintainer should be able to understand and change a family mostly
from within its package or bounded scope; cross-family contracts are expected,
but should be narrow, explicit, and few.
____

This is a judgment principle, not a numerical metric. Cross-family dependency
is expected; zero coupling is not the goal. Metadata/state locality, especially
around `_meta`, matters because distant implicit state communication is costly
to understand and change safely. Uncontrolled cross-family `_meta` coupling is
one of the strongest signals of a poor boundary; genuinely cross-family
metadata communication should be comparatively small, explicit, and backed by
an owned contract. The state-lifecycle questions in
<<dmc-state-lifecycle>> govern the detail. A metadata location is not itself a
family classification.

A family decomposition need not be mathematically unique. After hardening,
semantic stewardship must nevertheless be clear. State, policy, type, or
change ownership must not remain “sort of A and sort of B.”

===== Family, role, relationship, property, and layer

Do not collapse these axes:

* *Family / owner:* who owns the semantic capability, state, policy, or change
  surface?
* *Kind / role:* what does an element do? Examples include projection,
  reconstruction, validation, configuration, extraction or derivation,
  helper, model, policy, and composition. This list is open; roles may coexist
  and may be family-owned.
* *Relationship:* how do scopes collaborate? Examples include composes,
  consumes, provides a primitive, produces or consumes metadata, validates,
  reconstructs, requests or executes cleanup, updates references, renders,
  and publishes.
* *Layer / placement:* where can the implementation live while respecting
  authority and dependency direction?

Properties such as composite, shared primitive, public or internal,
cross-layer, confidence, or historical sediment are not competing owner
categories. Validation, configuration, or reconstruction appearing in a
non-family scope does not imply that those roles sit outside families; they
are often family-owned.

===== Evidence is not ownership proof

None of the following alone proves same-family ownership: shared
implementation, inverse, helper, primitive, metadata namespace, output shape,
package, documentation chapter, common use, or composition. “Independently
useful” is likewise weak evidence for separation.

Ask whether a split or merge improves semantic and metadata locality while
keeping external contracts narrow and understandable. A common carrier or
shape does not imply common semantics or ownership.

===== Working transformation and projection concepts

For DMC reasoning, a Domain transformation may be approximated as:

[source,text]
----
Frames -> Frames
----

`Frames` includes controlled Domain state and metadata. This approximation
does not rename repository terminology. Helpers, primitives, types, policies,
services, and boundaries are not necessarily transformations.

*Projection* is useful working vocabulary for a transformation that derives a
domain-suitable or user-oriented editing/presentation surface from canonical
state and defines the authority, precedence, conflict, and reconstruction
semantics by which edits are mapped back toward canonical state. The term is
not, through this contract, promoted into framework-wide Functional or
Semantic Model vocabulary.

A projection need not be bijective. It may introduce redundancy, reconstruct
lossily, deliberately overwrite redundant competing state, use explicit
precedence or configurable conflict policy, and require controlled metadata.

Keep Domain validation separate from spreadsheet editing/input constraints.
Domain validation asks whether existing Domain state satisfies an invariant.
An editing/input constraint describes which values a user may or is encouraged
to enter on an editing surface. “Workbook Editing Constraint” may be useful
descriptive wording, but is not globally canonical terminology established by
this contract.

[[dmc-cycle-zero]]
==== 3. Perform bounded Cycle 0

Before topology or public-surface changes, create a temporary working
inventory of only the selected scope and the material edges required to change
it safely. Do not first classify the whole Domain.

As applicable, identify:

* the semantic concepts, current implementation locations, and present
  ownership or authority claims;
* registered/YAML, documented-user, and supported programmatic surfaces,
  distinguishing *importable* from *supported API*;
* configuration and its authoring and resolution authority;
* canonical, projected or derived, transient, and persisted state actually
  touched;
* metadata and provenance producers and consumers;
* projection, reconstruction, cleanup, and replay behavior;
* material handoffs and adjacent owners that must coordinate;
* actual maintained consumers and credible external consumers when relevant;
* tests protecting semantics, boundaries, interactions, and compatibility;
* historical version or name sediment and compatibility modes;
* current related canonical documentation, contracts, and registry
  declarations; and
* local Domain/Core or package-placement questions raised by the change.

The inventory is complete when supported behavior, authority, public and
compatibility surfaces, affected state, and material handoffs are clear enough
to separate the proposed change from unrelated behavior. It is working
material, not a new permanent registry.

===== Evidence and authority discipline

Runtime behavior, maintained architectural and model documents, executable
tests, supported consumer surfaces, and checked declarations provide different
kinds of evidence. Existing behavior is not self-authorizing, and an intended
registry row is not proof of observed runtime exposure.

The
link:../../../registries/domain_contracts/README.adoc[Domain Contracts
Registry] is an existing model to reconcile when touched, not an automatic
authority over maintained runtime semantics or a reason to create a parallel
ontology. Check the registry's current scope and confidence rules rather than
assuming full Domain coverage.

Historical material is optional provenance and navigation evidence. Consult
only history relevant to the bounded scope when current rationale cannot be
understood from maintained authority, then verify current truth locally. Do
not use historical IDs as normative identifiers or make historical archaeology
a prerequisite for applying the contract.

===== Contract and registry proliferation check

Hardening does not automatically mean adding contracts or registry rows. Where
several surfaces appear to declare the same fact, ask:

* Which existing source owns the fact?
* Is another surface a derived projection or a join, or is it a mirror?
* Does a machine-readable declaration provide concrete value and a safe
  control loop?
* Can redundant, obsolete, or untrustworthy authority be removed?
* Is the existing registry complete and current for this specific fact?

Use one authoritative owner for one fact; join rather than mirror; grow
registries only from bounded, proven needs. Full Domain Contracts Registry
population is not a DMC-cycle prerequisite.

[[dmc-semantic-decisions]]
==== 4. Decide semantics, public surfaces, and configuration

Start from desired Domain semantics, not the current function graph or package
layout. For the selected scope, answer as applicable:

* What user or Domain problem and transition does the scope own?
* Which invariants, policy, state, and lifecycle belong to it?
* Which adjacent responsibilities and states remain owned elsewhere?
* Which current operations are distinct Domain transitions, and which are
  historical alternatives for one transition?
* What is the supported registered/YAML surface? What Python surface is
  intentionally supported rather than merely reachable?
* Which configuration dimensions express semantic choices, and which are
  transport syntax, inference, or historical implementation accidents?
* Which names express current Domain responsibility rather than mechanism,
  retired ontology, carrier shape, or version history?

“One concept -> one preferred surface” is an anti-sediment heuristic, not a
mathematical law. Multiple public operations are legitimate when they
represent genuinely different transitions or user contracts. Minimizing
function count is not a goal and must not hide real input, lifecycle,
authority, or capability.

Configuration should make real semantic choices explicit, use bounded
vocabulary where the semantic set is closed, avoid payload-shape
interpretation heuristics unless an accepted producer owns them, expose clear
defaults, and avoid duplicate authoring paths for the same fact. YAML/CLI keys
and parameter shapes are gated surfaces under
link:../../ai_info/interfaces_and_gates.adoc[Interfaces and Gates]; carrier
syntax does not decide semantic ownership.

===== Composite role contract

Composition-specific facts are owned by a bounded scope when it plays a
composite role; composite is not a new owner category.

[quote]
____
Composite owns composition; dependencies retain primitive semantics.
____

A composite may own operation order, dependency configuration, temporary
artifacts, trace or diagnostic policy, public composite identity,
cross-operation failure behavior, and reconstruction coordination. It must
not duplicate primitive semantics or validation owned by its dependencies.
Persisted composite state should be minimal truthful intent or explicitly
non-authoritative diagnostics, never leaked temporary implementation state
presented as inverse authority.

[[dmc-material-handoffs]]
==== 5. Treat material handoffs as first-class contracts

A handoff is *material* when a plausible single-sided change on either side --
for example to authority, ordering, mutation, representation or format,
conflict behavior, failure/retry/replay behavior, or lifecycle timing -- could
silently break the other side's current behavior, invariant, or compatibility
surface unless both sides are considered together. This is a judgment test,
not a metric or registry.

For each material handoff touched by the bounded change, establish as
applicable:

* producer;
* consumer or executor;
* authoritative state and permitted mutation or copy ownership;
* ordering and consumption or termination behavior;
* failure behavior and failure atomicity or partial-publication guarantee;
* conflict detection and conflict-resolution ownership where multiple
  producers or alternative paths converge on one consumer or executor;
* compatibility expectations; and
* responsibility for coordinating a change across both sides.

Typical seams include feature -> final cleanup, composite -> primitive,
feature-owned metadata -> schema maintenance, projected state ->
reconstruction -> canonical state, Domain intent -> renderer or adapter,
publisher report -> Artifact Manifest, and family-owned state -> Workbook View
or editing constraints. Record relevant handoffs in the Cycle 0 working
material or the existing owning contract; do not create an interaction
registry.

Build and validate before publishing where failure could otherwise mutate
caller-owned frames or nested metadata, or expose a partial result. A new
top-level mapping is not sufficient if nested state still aliases the caller.

[[dmc-state-lifecycle]]
==== 6. Make state, provenance, and reconstruction explicit

Use vocabulary and lifecycle authority already owned by the Semantic and
Technical Models; do not create a DMC state ontology. For state the change
actually touches, answer:

* What is authoritative? What is authored Intent versus recomputable
  Resolution?
* What is canonical, derived or projected, transient, or persisted under the
  current owning model?
* What may be edited, recomputed, overwritten, discarded before persistence,
  or intentionally lost?
* What reconstructs, with which authority and precedence?
* Who produces and consumes provenance? What becomes stale?
* Who requests cleanup, who executes it, when does it run, and what state must
  survive until then?
* Is replay safe and idempotent, including after partial prior lifecycle
  progress?
* Who owns mutation and copies, and what must remain unchanged on failure?

Preserve “Persist Intent; discard recomputable Resolution” where it applies.
Do not infer lifecycle or ownership from a carrier location alone. A
cross-consumer metadata shape needs explicit common versus consumer-specific
policy when consumers differ. Schema maintenance may update feature-owned
references without owning their meaning.

When a bounded scope contains a projection in the provisional working sense
described in <<dmc-working-model>>, also establish:

* what state is canonical and what state is projected;
* which surface is authoritative for edits;
* what reconstruction occurs and who owns it;
* which precedence applies;
* how conflicts are detected and resolved;
* what redundancy or information loss is intentional;
* what metadata or provenance is required and how stale metadata is handled;
  and
* what cleanup, replay, and roundtrip behavior applies.

A one-way extraction needs no invented inverse. A non-projection needs no fake
projection precedence contract.

[[dmc-compatibility]]
==== 7. Decide compatibility by migration friction and expectation

[quote]
____
Existing behavior is evidence, not an automatic compatibility obligation.
____

A hardening change may preserve, simplify, migrate, rename, merge, deprecate,
replace, or remove behavior when the architectural benefit justifies the
compatibility cost. Breaking changes may be justified before or after 1.0.
The decision depends on migration friction and compatibility expectation, not
a version-number shortcut.

Consider:

* actual maintained consumers and credible external consumers where
  discoverable and relevant;
* maturity and stability expectations of the affected surface;
* migration effort and guidance;
* whether migration is mechanical, declarative, or AI-friendly; and
* the semantic or architectural simplification gained.

Require evidence before retaining duplicate sediment and before breaking a
real contract. Where a disposition clarifies a material surface, use:

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Disposition | Decision

| *preserve*
| Retain a distinct valuable behavior or contract with supported-use or
  architectural evidence.

| *simplify / merge*
| Reduce historical alternatives without losing a distinct transition, input,
  lifecycle, or authority.

| *migrate / rename / replace*
| Keep the capability through a clearer surface, with proportionate migration
  guidance and affected maintained consumers moved.

| *deprecate*
| Retain a time-bounded compatibility bridge to a named replacement when
  immediate removal has unjustified friction.

| *remove*
| Retire redundant, dead, misleading, or unjustifiably costly behavior after
  checking relevant consumers and migration expectations.
|===

Remove obsolete version or slice labels from current symbols, configuration,
and prose when they no longer express a live distinction. Keep historical IDs
only as optional provenance when the surrounding meaning stands without them.
Apply the
link:../ch09_release_management/02_release_runbook.adoc[release-note workflow]
when a user- or caller-visible change warrants it.

[[dmc-boundaries]]
==== 8. Respect established boundaries and layers

===== Trusted Ingress

Trusted Ingress is accepted permanent architecture. Apply its consequences by
reference rather than reimplementing it. The canonical owners are:

* link:../../technical_model/ch06_architectural_layers/architectural_layers.adoc[Architectural
  Layers] for establishment and re-establishment, executable binding,
  controlled state, description and resource authority, and the layer model;
* link:04_trusted_ingress_extension.adoc[Extending Trusted Ingress] for the
  maintainer extension procedure; and
* link:../../ai_info/interfaces_and_gates.adoc[Interfaces and Gates] for the
  caller-facing programmatic surface.

Consequences for bounded Domain hardening are:

* ordinary Domain code may rely on the established Ingress-Conformant Domain
  Payload language after a framework-managed boundary;
* capability or family semantic preconditions and Domain invariants remain
  with their semantic owner;
* Domain code should not reintroduce arbitrary external-carrier or
  hostile-object defenses already owned by Trusted Ingress;
* every controlled non-ordinary transition touched by the change must have
  explicit accepted authority under the canonical controlled-state contract;
* hardening must not broaden certification or introduce redundant historical
  authority merely to preserve sediment; and
* caller-precondition surfaces such as direct Domain calls or `run_pipeline`
  do not acquire managed-boundary guarantees through importability.

Do not generalize a controlled-role example into a universal “exactly one
producer or transition per capability” law. Accepted architecture defines
exact authority per controlled role and transition; one capability may have
more than one reviewed producer or transition where its owning contract
permits that.

===== Domain and Core

The maintained Architectural Layers chapter remains authority. Do not define
Core as merely pure or stateless, content-free, cross-layer-used, or a Ports
layer. Do not rename Core through a bounded DMC cycle.

When a bounded change raises a placement question, reason locally about
semantic or feature authority, legal inward dependency direction, cross-layer
carrier or mechanism needs, side effects and runtime ownership,
compatibility, and cycle or duplication risk. Two useful local review aids
are:

* *Complexity isolation:* a small bounded mechanism may live in Core because
  it keeps low-level mechanics out of Domain algorithms.
* *Cross-layer neutrality:* a mechanism or type may live in Core when several
  layers legitimately need it and it owns no feature policy.

Feature authority, projection or reconstruction semantics, Domain metadata
lifecycle, and feature-specific policy are negative evidence against Core.
The review aids above are subordinate to Architectural Layers, not new global
placement rules.

===== Output context without Egress design

Preserve only this contextual ownership boundary:

* the semantic owner owns semantic projection, reconstruction, provenance,
  authority, and cleanup;
* family code should not learn adapter identity merely to enforce sink
  compatibility; and
* later output-boundary architecture may validate admissible Domain exit state
  against sink capabilities.

Carrier-oriented names do not decide semantic ownership. This contract does
not establish Egress packages, capability registries, fallback rules,
integrity mechanics, or the placement of output responsibilities.

[[dmc-source-topology]]
==== 9. Make topology support local reasoning

Topology follows semantic decisions and exists to improve comprehension and
locality, not symmetry. For a cohesive family large enough to benefit from
physical structure, prefer:

[source,text]
----
family first
  -> kind / responsibility inside the family
----

For example:

[source,text]
----
domain/<family>/
    projection.py
    reconstruction.py
    validation.py
    model.py
    policy.py
----

This is illustrative, not a required skeleton. Kind may remain encoded in
filenames while small. A role may become a subpackage only when a real
semantic seam and size justify it. Do not require top-level technical-kind
trees that scatter one family's reasoning. Small capabilities need not become
packages. Shared primitives and cross-cutting services may sit outside a
family package when their contract is genuinely shared. Cross-owner relations
must remain explicit rather than hidden by nesting.

Preserve deliberate package facades, stable supported entry points, registry
targets, inward dependencies, and private-import discipline. Do not infer a
supported API from importability or create packages merely so a guard or
template can exist. Size and complexity remain review signals, not mechanical
split targets.

[[dmc-local-human-readability]]
===== Local Human Readability

Local Human Readability is *bounded reconstruction inside sound architecture*.
For a touched code path, a maintainer should be able to reconstruct its current
semantics, lifecycle, ownership and mutation, validation and side-effect order,
and important non-local reasons without avoidable archaeology through trivial
helpers, callers, consumers, tests, metadata, or history.

Keep three complementary concerns distinct:

* *Family/repository locality* says where semantic reasoning lives.
* *Local Human Readability* says whether a touched algorithm can be
  reconstructed without avoidable archaeology.
* *Reference Readability* says permanent meaning is understandable without
  historical identifiers or archaeology.

None substitutes for either of the others.

Prefer top-down readable flow and algorithmic locality when extraction would
merely add navigation to reconstruct one coherent operation. Extract or
abstract when doing so exposes a real semantic boundary, owned value,
invariant, reusable contract, independently understandable mechanism, or
other coherent responsibility. Resolve raw option or state bags at the real
boundary when that reduces reconstruction cost; make ownership and mutation
explicit; and use comments for invariants, authority, boundary reasons, or
surprising constraints rather than syntax. Remove downstream defensive
introspection when the established Trusted-Ingress contract already owns the
guarantee.

This principle favors neither keeping everything inline nor extracting
helpers. It creates no function/file length, line-count, helper-count, or
readability metric. The detailed judgment-based procedure remains owned by
the
link:03_human_readability_review.adoc[Human Readability Review Guide]. For a
small change, its lightweight before/after reader-question test is sufficient;
reserve the full review sequence for changes whose cost is non-trivial.

[[dmc-tests-consumers]]
==== 10. Preserve tests and consumer evidence

For a bounded change, identify and preserve or add the tests that protect the
contracts actually affected:

* semantic invariants;
* managed-boundary or controlled-state behavior when applicable;
* projection, reconstruction, roundtrip, cleanup, and replay when applicable;
* material interaction ordering, authority, atomicity, and metadata handoffs;
* supported public, registry, and configuration surfaces;
* maintained-consumer behavior where relevant; and
* narrow architecture guards that protect a deliberate dependency or package
  boundary.

Existing FTR markers remain useful because they answer “why does this test
exist?” Do not require a new global family or capability marker, or reorganize
the test tree around ownership. Integration and interaction tests may
legitimately protect several owners. Add test-ownership machinery only if
repeated bounded cycles demonstrate a concrete problem.

Consumer evidence is proportional. If a maintained consumer uses a changed
surface, inventory and migrate it as an acceptance probe. If no consumer
exists, do not invent migration work. Use credible external-consumer evidence
where available and relevant; do not assume absence merely because the
repository has no local call site. Spreadsheet export/reimport, adapter parity,
semantic diffs, or managed-pipeline probes are required only when the changed
contract reaches those surfaces.

[[dmc-documentation]]
==== 11. Leave canonical documentation reference-ready

A supported public family or capability should be understandable without
architecture archaeology. Do not create one manually maintained reference
document per scope or another source of truth. Canonical information may remain
distributed by document ownership:

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
| Surface | Owns

| Functional Model
| semantic behavior and the user or problem-space responsibility

| Semantic Model
| vocabulary, identities, meaning, and invariants

| Technical Model
| lifecycle, persistence, boundaries, and mechanisms

| User Guide
| supported usage, configuration, examples, errors, and limitations

| Developer Guide
| maintainer procedure, including this DMC, conventions, and review guidance

| `interfaces_and_gates.adoc`
| compact public-surface responsibilities and approval gates

| Registries
| facts that genuinely benefit from structured machine-readable authority and
  have a trustworthy control loop

| A bounded FTR or Cycle 0 working artifact
| temporary design, evidence, decisions, and migration plan
|===

The reference model is:

[source,text]
----
canonical sources
  -> deterministic aggregation / projection
  -> coherent human- and LLM-facing reference
----

Generated AsciiDoc, Markdown, or machine-readable output must remain a
projection of canonical sources, not another authority. Structured data should
own only facts that benefit from structured authority; do not serialize
narrative architecture merely to generate documentation.

The semantics of a supported public family or capability must have clear
canonical owners and be structured well enough that a coherent human- and
LLM-facing reference can be assembled without inventing missing semantics or
creating another source of truth.

Apply *Reference Readability*: permanent code and documentation must state
their meaning without requiring an FTR ID, review finding, obsolete version
name, or Git history lookup. Historical IDs may remain optional provenance
when the surrounding meaning stands independently. Reference Readability
complements but does not substitute for Local Human Readability
(<<dmc-local-human-readability>>) or family/repository locality.

[[dmc-completion]]
==== 12. Apply the proportional completion gate

A bounded hardening cycle is complete when the following applicable outcomes
hold. “Not applicable” is legitimate; it must not hide a material touched
contract.

. The semantic scope, owner or stewardship, invariants, and material adjacent
  responsibilities are clear.
. Material handoffs have explicit producer and consumer, authority, order,
  conflict detection and resolution where applicable, failure and atomicity,
  compatibility, and coordinated-change responsibility.
. Supported registered, YAML, user, and Python surfaces are deliberate;
  importability has not been mistaken for support.
. Compatibility and migration decisions are evidence-backed and proportionate;
  affected maintained consumers are migrated where required.
. Touched state, metadata, provenance, projection or reconstruction, cleanup,
  replay, persistence, and copy ownership form a coherent lifecycle.
. Trusted-Ingress and layer consequences are respected without reimplementing
  or broadening their authority; local Domain/Core decisions are explicitly
  reviewed where the change requires them.
. Source and package topology supports semantic and metadata locality without a
  mechanical skeleton or false nesting.
. Touched code paths satisfy Local Human Readability
  (<<dmc-local-human-readability>>): current semantics, ownership and mutation,
  validation and side-effect order, and important non-local reasons are
  reconstructable without avoidable archaeology. The lightweight before/after
  reader-question test is sufficient for a small change.
. Relevant semantic, boundary, interaction, roundtrip, consumer, and
  architecture tests are green.
. Historical modes and sediment touched by the change have a deliberate
  preserve, simplify, replace, or remove disposition.
. Canonical documentation and genuinely authoritative contract surfaces are
  updated once; redundant or mirrored authority is not added, and the
  release-note workflow is applied where relevant.
. Permanent prose and code satisfy Reference Readability and allow coherent
  human and LLM reference assembly without inventing semantics.
. A proportionate independent review has accepted the hardened bounded scope
  before that scope is considered complete.

No inverse, consumer migration, package, projection contract, ingress redesign,
archival action, Project Memory edit, registry row, or documentation surface is
required when the bounded change does not actually have or affect it.
