== 1. Problem statement

Two independently accepted, currently published contracts collide on the same
value space, and neither can be silently narrowed to resolve the collision:

*Contract 1 -- XRef physical-label authority [ACCEPTED, published].* XRef
distinguishes *matrix-axis identities* (the persisted roundtrip vocabulary:
unique, non-empty strings) from *physical frame column labels* (the address
of a DataFrame column on a participating relation/matrix/`base_relation`
frame: non-missing, hashable, uniquely and deterministically comparable, and
scalar-addressable -- explicitly *not* required to be a string). This
distinction, and the concrete claim that a non-string physical label is
accepted ("a unique numeric label, for example, is accepted"), is published
in four independent authority layers, not one: the Technical Model
(`docs/technical_model/ch04_concepts/crosstab_binary_xref.adoc`), the
Functional Model
(`docs/functional_model/ch04_use_case_concepts/xref_crosstable.adoc`,
"Axis Identity Versus Physical Column Label"), the User Guide
(`docs/user_guide/ch03_transformation_catalog/02_bijective_transforms.adoc`,
"Carrier-stable column identities"), and the release notes
(`docs/release_notes/release_notes.adoc`), plus `registries/meta_registry.yaml`'s
`xref_crosstable` entry. `row_keys` (both steps) and `dense_axes.rows_from`/
`columns_from.key`/`.keys` select physical fields on the relation/axis-source
frame and are governed by this weaker, explicitly non-string-only contract --
confirmed directly in `src/spreadsheet_handling/domain/transformations/xref_crosstable/operation.py`
and `dense_axes.py` (section 4).

*Contract 2 -- Trusted-Ingress bounded metadata substrate [ACCEPTED, not yet
implemented].* `FTR-TRUSTED-INGRESS-P4A`'s E3 precondition (its own section
11 and 23/E3) requires `_meta` to be an owned, bounded, acyclic dict/list/
Scalar substrate before E3 may enforce it: exact `dict` root and recursive
containers, exact `str` dictionary keys, and E1-admitted Scalar leaves
(`str | bool | int | float | date | datetime`, `core/scalar_values.py`).
Special-value and special-key carrier compatibility is dispositioned per
census sections 17.2/18.6; that disposition is sound and is not reopened
here.

*The collision.* `_meta.xref_crosstable.<entry>.row_keys` is a list whose
*elements are the physical labels themselves*. It is durable, persisted
Intent -- `src/spreadsheet_handling/pipeline/persistence_boundary.py`
preserves `row_keys` and `dense_axes.rows_from`/`columns_from` unchanged and
strips only `column_keys` and `dense_axes.resolved` (section 4). A physical
label admitted by Contract 1 but outside E1 Scalar (`bytes`, `complex`,
`frozenset`, a stable custom hashable/comparable object) can therefore reach
durable Trusted Metadata as an ordinary leaf value the moment it is used as a
`row_keys` or `dense_axes` axis-key selector -- not only as a transient
runtime artifact. `_meta.xref_crosstable.<entry>.dense_axes.resolved.row_identities`
compounds this: it is a run-local Resolution facet (stripped before
persistence) that uses the same physical label as a *dictionary key*, which
would also violate Contract 2's exact-`str`-key rule even for E1-Scalar
labels (`int`, `bool`, `float`, `date`) that satisfy Contract 2's leaf rule
just fine. Section 5 separates these two sub-problems precisely; they do not
have the same shape and do not need the same fix.

Four consecutive census corrections (sections 18-20 of the census) tried to
resolve this inside the census document itself, each independently reviewed
and each found insufficient (`TRUSTED-INGRESS-E3-CENSUS-FOLLOWUP-F1`,
currently open). The last correction (census section 20) proposed narrowing
XRef's own `row_keys`/axis-key contract to exact non-empty `str`. The
independent review of the census (review section 16) rejected that specific
narrowing there as an unauthorized silent contradiction of the four published
authority layers above, because a census-text correction is not the artifact
with authority to revise a published contract.

This FTR exists to give that conflict an explicit, owning architecture
decision instead of a fifth census-text patch. Section 8 (as corrected)
explains why this FTR ultimately reaches the same `str`-only *durable
reference* vocabulary census section 20 proposed, but for a different,
carrier-proof-backed reason, through an artifact with the standing to
deliberately revise the published contract, and with the atomic
documentation-transition obligation (section 10) census section 20 lacked.

== 2. Why this is not a census-text problem

The four failed corrections shared one structural mistake: each treated
"which physical labels does XRef's `row_keys` accept" as a fact to be
established once and then poured directly into the E3 metadata-key/leaf
grammar. But the accepted authority in section 1 already commits to a
specific, *published, four-times-documented* answer to that question --
broader than E1 Scalar -- and Trusted Metadata's grammar is deliberately
*narrower* than that answer by design (bullet 11 of this FTR's originating
task; census sections 17.1/18.1 apply the same principle to values and
keys). Neither side is free to unilaterally win. Resolving it requires an
architecture decision about the *relationship* between the two contracts,
not a rewording of either one. That decision is this FTR's entire scope.

== 3. Non-goals

This FTR is *not*:

* an XRef redesign, a general physical-label redesign, or a change to
  `domain/tabular/physical_labels.py`'s `ensure_unique_physical_column_labels`
  predicate (Contract 1's frame-level authority is unchanged everywhere it
  is not the specific selector role decided in section 7);
* a metadata-schema project, a parser project, or `FTR-META-SCHEMA-VALIDATION-P5`/
  `FTR-META-CONTRACT-VERSIONING-P4A` work;
* `E7` dotted-extension authorization, tuple/multiheader future work
  (`FTR-XREF-AXIS-MAPPINGS-P4A2`'s deferred "future column identity / display
  model"), output-sink safety, or Trusted-Ingress documentation condensation;
* a re-opening of census sections 1-17 (special-value/special-key
  dispositions, ownership/alias/cycle dispositions, Legend Blocks delegate
  treatment) -- all remain accepted and unmodified;
* an implementation. No runtime or test file is touched by this FTR.

== 5. Runtime versus durable analysis -- the two sub-problems are not the same shape

*Sub-problem 1 -- durable leaf-value vocabulary (Class B/D).* `row_keys` and
`dense_axes.rows_from`/`columns_from.key`/`.keys` are lists whose elements
are the physical labels. For `str`/`bool`/`int`/`float`/`date`/`datetime`
labels this is already inside E1 Scalar -- no conflict. For `bytes`,
`complex`, `frozenset`, or a stable custom hashable/comparable label, the
label itself is not E1 Scalar and can never satisfy E3's "ordinary leaves:
E1-admitted Scalar" rule as a durable value, independent of any dict-key
question. This sub-problem exists even if `dense_axes.resolved` did not
exist at all.

*Sub-problem 2 -- runtime dict-key representation (Class E).* Independent of
label type, `dense_axes.resolved.row_identities[i]` uses the physical label
as a Python dictionary *key*. E3's "ordinary dict keys: exact `str`" rule
would reject this for *every* non-`str` label, including `int`/`bool`/
`float`/`date` labels that pass sub-problem 1 cleanly. `persistence_boundary.py`
strips this facet before any carrier write, so it is never a durable-carrier
problem, but the accepted E3 precondition is explicitly scoped to
"external, persisted, *runtime-created*, projected, and *consumed*"
metadata (`FTR-TRUSTED-INGRESS-P4A_E3_metadata_census.adoc` section 1) --
runtime creation and same-run consumption both happen before persistence
projection ever runs (`_stored_row_identities` reads the unstripped runtime
value). Output-side stripping does not retroactively make the runtime
substrate conformant; census section 14.2 and review section 14.2
independently reach the same conclusion.

Conflating these two sub-problems is exactly what made census sections 19-20
oscillate: section 19's list-of-`{"row_key","value"}` migration solves
sub-problem 2 losslessly for *any* label type, but does nothing for
sub-problem 1 (a `frozenset` value under `"row_key"` is still not E1 Scalar
-- moving it from key to value position does not admit it). Section 20 then
tried to solve sub-problem 1 by force (narrow the label vocabulary to `str`),
which also happens to solve sub-problem 2 as a side effect (a `str` key is
already exact-`str`).

As corrected (section 7), this FTR's selected durable-reference vocabulary
is exact `str` as well -- not because `str`-only is convenient, but because
census section 20's E1-Scalar-and-wider premise for sub-problem 1 is itself
disproven (section 8): a physical column *label* is not carrier-neutral
across every maintained frame carrier for `int`/`bool`/`float`/`date`/
`datetime`, so admitting those categories into the durable-reference
vocabulary would not actually close sub-problem 1 for them either. Once the
durable-reference vocabulary is exact `str`, sub-problem 2 dissolves as a
direct consequence rather than needing a separate representation fix: every
key reaching `dense_axes.resolved.row_identities` is already guaranteed
exact `str` by construction, which already satisfies E3's exact-`str`-key
rule with the existing `dict` shape unchanged (section 7.3).

== 6. Evaluated alternatives

=== 6.1 Alternative A -- capability split by metadata durability

*[RECOMMENDATION -- selected, see section 7]* Broad physical labels remain
usable at the DataFrame boundary and via Class A's frame-level predicate
everywhere they are not nominated as a `row_keys`/axis-key selector. Any
XRef mode that writes a selector reference into durable metadata (Class B/D)
must use a carrier-stable, durably-representable label subset.

*[Corrected]* The original selection of this subset -- E1-admitted Scalar --
is disproven by direct evidence (section 8): `admit_ordinary_scalar`
classifies a value's *semantic family* but does not establish that the same
value, used as a physical DataFrame column label, reloads with the same type
and identity from every maintained frame carrier. A real cross-carrier probe
(section 8) shows the physical header itself -- not just the metadata copy --
silently changes type (`int` `7` reloads as column label `"7"`) on the
JSON-directory, XLSX, and ODS paths, breaking later XRef lookup regardless of
what type the metadata entry preserves. E1 Scalar is therefore not a
carrier-neutral durable-reference vocabulary; only its `str` member is,
because a `str` physical label is the one category no maintained carrier
retypes on reload. The durable subset selected in section 7 is accordingly
*exact native, non-empty `str`* -- see section 8 for the full evidence and
for why this is a deliberate, evidenced compatibility revision of the
published contract rather than either the original E1-Scalar choice or a
convenience-only re-adoption of census section 20's proposal.

=== 6.2 Alternative B -- stable label-reference representation

*[Evaluated, rejected as unnecessary/unavailable]* Could XRef preserve
*every* Class A label (including `bytes`/`complex`/`frozenset`/custom
objects) by persisting a separate stable technical reference instead of the
raw label?

No genuine stable identity exists to reference. XRef's own documentation
states physical column *order* is not a reliable identity ("XRef must not
rely on accidental dataframe order",
`docs/functional_model/ch04_use_case_concepts/xref_crosstable.adoc`, "Axis
Scope"), and no separate id system for physical columns exists anywhere in
the repository. The one accepted precedent for a "swap vocabulary without
persisting a hidden identity" pattern is `FTR-XREF-AXIS-MAPPINGS-P4A2`'s
`project_axis_labels`/`restore_axis_keys` and
`contract_grouped_xref`/`expand_grouped_xref`: a *live, non-persisted*
resolver frame consulted fresh on every run, explicitly documented as
persisting nothing. That pattern solves a different problem (canonical
technical key <-> visible display label, both already known values) and
requires a source frame the caller must maintain; it has no answer for "what
stable reference names an opaque `frozenset` or custom object" without
inventing exactly the disallowed escape hatch (`repr`/`str`/`hash`/pickle).
Per this FTR's own governing instruction, that invention is out of bounds.
Section 9's E3 dependency accordingly does not require or introduce any new
stable-id system.

=== 6.3 Alternative C -- controlled runtime role outside `_meta`

*[Evaluated, rejected as unnecessary and out of independent scope]*
Relocating `dense_axes.resolved` (or `row_keys` itself) to an execution-local
state object outside `_meta` was considered for sub-problem 2.

`dense_axes.resolved` is already an accepted `_meta` Resolution facet under
the existing, independently accepted Intent/Resolution split
(`docs/ai_info/agent_rules.adoc` "Persistence boundary for `_meta`": Intent
survives a clean re-run unchanged, Resolution is materialised from current
state and is stripped at the persistence boundary -- `dense_axes.resolved`
is named there explicitly, alongside `legend_blocks[*].resolved`, as the
established example of this pattern). Relocating it outside `_meta` would
reopen that already-accepted architecture for one feature, is not required
to solve either sub-problem (section 6.1's leaf/key fixes solve both without
moving anything out of `_meta`), and would introduce a second controlled-role
inventory entry this FTR has no independent mandate to authorize. `row_keys`
itself cannot be relocated: it is Intent, and XRef's rediscovery model
(`FTR-...02_bijective_transforms.adoc`, "Persisted XRef intent") requires a
later `contract_xref`/`expand_xref` in the same run, or a fresh run, to
rediscover it from `_meta` by frame identity when no explicit config is
supplied -- moving it out of `_meta` would break that rediscovery contract
independent of the metadata-substrate question this FTR answers.

=== 6.4 Alternative D -- bounded extension of the metadata vocabulary

*[Evaluated, rejected]* Could E1 Scalar simply be widened to include
`bytes`/`complex`/`frozenset`/"any label the physical-label predicate
admits"?

Rejected on the originating task's own terms (no arbitrary-object escape
hatch) and independently: the physical-label predicate (non-missing,
hashable, deterministically comparable, scalar-addressable) is not a closed
semantic vocabulary -- it is satisfied by any well-behaved custom class a
caller defines, which is precisely the "arbitrary comparable object" shape
the originating task explicitly forbids admitting into Trusted Metadata.
Importing it into E1 Scalar would also retroactively widen every *other*
ordinary `_meta` leaf in the repository, not just XRef's selector role,
which is a far larger and unrelated compatibility decision this FTR has no
mandate to make.

=== 6.5 Positional representation (task section 12)

*[Evaluated, rejected]* `dense_axes.resolved.row_identities` cannot drop
name-based association for position-only representation: the documented
same-run fallback consumer (`_stored_row_identities`) explicitly supports a
later `contract_xref` supplying `row_keys` in a *different order* (composite
reordering is a pinned, tested behavior -- census section 19.3/15.1
reproduced `region, category` -> `category, region` reassembly). Position
loses that semantic; rejected per the task's own instruction. This does not
revive or require the previously-rejected pair-record migration as a
standalone project -- section 7.3 reuses the already-specified,
name-preserving list-of-records shape from census section 19.8 instead.

== 7. Selected architecture

*[RECOMMENDATION, corrected]* Alternative A (section 6.1): a capability split
by metadata durability. The durable-selector vocabulary is *exact native,
non-empty `str`* (not E1-admitted Scalar, corrected from the original
selection -- section 8). The runtime dense-axis dict-key representation is
*unchanged* (the section 19 list-of-records migration is withdrawn as
unnecessary -- section 7.3).

=== 7.1 The rule, stated for a maintainer without replaying this history

____
Physical column labels stay broad everywhere they address a DataFrame
column as ordinary data (the global physical-label contract, Class A,
unchanged). The moment a physical label is *nominated* as an XRef `row_keys`
or `dense_axes` `key`/`keys` selector -- directly, or inherited through
Compact Multiaxis's forwarding -- it enters durable `_meta` Intent and must
additionally be an exact, non-empty native `str`: `type(value) is str and
value != ""`. This is a deliberate, evidenced narrowing of the previously
published broader selector contract (section 8), not a description of prior
behavior. Everything else about a column -- including a column that is not
nominated as a selector -- keeps full physical-label authority, unchanged.
____

This is one new, narrow, statically-local qualification on one specific role
(selector nomination), not a reopening of Class A's frame-level authority and
not a second grammar exception scattered across paths.

=== 7.2 Sub-problem 1 -- durable leaf-value vocabulary

At the two seams census section 20.4 already identified --
`contract_xref`'s/`expand_xref`'s `row_keys` (`operation.py:199,357,252`)
and `_axis_source_config`'s shared `key`/`keys` handling
(`dense_axes.py:122`, used by both `rows_from` and `columns_from`) -- add an
exact-`str`, non-empty check for each individual label, *after* the existing
Class A physical-label and flat/non-tuple checks (order matters: the
existing diagnostics for missing/ambiguous/unhashable/duplicate/tuple labels
fire first, unchanged).

*Exact predicate:* `type(value) is str and value != ""`.

* `type(value) is str`, not `isinstance(value, str)`: excludes both a `str`
  subclass and `numpy.str_` (confirmed by direct probe:
  `numpy.str_` is a genuine `str` subclass, so `isinstance` would silently
  accept it while `type(...) is str` correctly rejects it -- this is exactly
  the hazard the review found in `_ensure_column_identity_values`, which
  this predicate does not reuse, for the same reason section 7.2's original
  text already gave: it uses `isinstance` and calls `repr()` on a rejected,
  unclassified candidate).
* `value != ""`: excludes the E1 Missing category (`scalar_category("")`
  classifies as `"missing"`) from the selector role. This makes the
  currently-mechanically-accepted empty-string physical label a deliberate
  exclusion for the *selector* role specifically, not an oversight; an
  empty-string column may still exist as ordinary DataFrame data (Class A
  unchanged).
* Whitespace-only strings (e.g. `"   "`) are *not* excluded by this rule.
  `scalar_category("   ")` classifies as ordinary `"string"`, not `"missing"`
  -- confirmed by direct probe. No maintained producer, fixture, or
  documented example uses a whitespace-only physical label in any role, but
  none is currently required to exclude it either: it is a carrier-stable
  `str` value like any other under every probed carrier (section 8), and
  Class C's separate whitespace exclusion in `_ensure_column_identity_values`
  is a distinct contract (matrix-axis spreadsheet-header display hygiene) this
  FTR does not import by default. The implementing slice may choose to align
  the two contracts for consistency, but this FTR does not require it absent
  further evidence.

*Normalization ownership (resolves the review's normalization-ownership
question -- section 5.1 of the review):* no normalization is needed or
introduced. This is Decision A of the review's own two-option framing:
because the selected durable-reference vocabulary is *exact* native `str`,
a value either already is in canonical reference form or it is rejected --
there is no accepted-but-not-canonical carrier variant to convert. The XRef
family check is therefore a *family contract* over an already-canonical
value, not general scalar normalization; it must not call `repr`, `str`,
arbitrary equality, or hashing on a rejected candidate merely for
diagnostics (mirroring `admit_ordinary_scalar`'s own safe-rejection
discipline, without reusing that function -- `admit_ordinary_scalar`
classifies the *E1 semantic family*, which is broader than this role's
`str`-only, carrier-neutrality-driven boundary, and returns the value
unchanged rather than establishing carrier-neutral identity). Phase E's
general scalar-normalization ownership (`core/scalar_values.py`'s own
documented deferral to Phase E) is untouched -- no `XRefScalarNormalizer`,
`str(value)`, or `repr(value)`-based coercion is introduced anywhere in this
role. A tiny XRef-local predicate (the two-line rule above) is clearer than
adapting a broader existing helper whose semantics are wrong for this role.

This keeps `row_keys=["region"]`, `row_keys=["7"]` (the literal string), and
every currently-published documentation example that already uses a string
selector working unchanged. It rejects `int`, `bool`, `float`, `date`,
`datetime`, NumPy/pandas scalar carrier variants, `bytes`, `complex`,
`frozenset`, and custom-object labels *only in the selector-nomination
role* -- they remain valid ordinary DataFrame columns everywhere else (Class
A unchanged). This includes the previously published numeric-label example;
section 8 explains why that specific example is retired rather than
preserved.

*Compact Multiaxis inherits this automatically.* `contract_compact_multiaxis`/
`expand_compact_multiaxis` build `row_key_cols` and call the real
`expand_xref`/`contract_xref` (`compact_multiaxis.py` lines 74 and 111-122;
217 and 239-254) *before* writing their own independent
`_meta.compact_multiaxis.<id>.row_keys` copy at lines 158/254 -- confirmed by
direct read. Because the inner XRef call already applies this section's
check to the same `row_key_cols` object, a non-conforming label raises before
Compact Multiaxis's own metadata write is ever reached; no second validation
gate is required at the Compact Multiaxis seam itself. The implementing slice
must still add a Compact-Multiaxis-specific test proving this inherited
rejection (not merely infer it from reading the composite once), because a

== 8. Why the durable-reference vocabulary is exact `str` -- carrier-neutrality evidence

*[Corrected.]* The original FTR argued *against* census section 20's
`str`-only proposal because it would falsify the published "a unique numeric
label ... is accepted" example. That argument is withdrawn: it is correct
that the *sentence* would become false, but it does not follow that the
*behavior* the sentence describes is preservable. This section replaces that
argument with the actual reason `str`-only is correct: under every currently
maintained carrier, a physical column label is not carrier-neutral for any
type other than `str`, so the published numeric example does not survive a
real round trip today regardless of what this FTR decides about metadata.

=== 8.1 The numeric example fails end to end, independent of any metadata decision

A direct probe (reproducing the review's section 5.3, independently
confirmed for this correction) built the review's exact scenario -- physical
relation label `7`, `row_keys=[7]`, `dense_axes.rows_from.key=7` -- and
persisted the result through the real `contract_xref` and JSON-backend
writer:

....
_meta.yaml (sidecar):        row_keys: [7]      # int, unchanged
values.json (data frame):    [{"7": "r1", ...}]  # JSON object key: string "7"
....

JSON object keys are strings by definition -- this is not a bug in this
project's serializer, it is a property of the JSON data model itself, and it
applies identically to the XLSX/ODS hidden-sheet JSON blob (both composers
call plain `json.dumps` for the data-record payload). Directly reproduced:

[cols="2,2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Carrier |Reloaded physical column label |Reloaded metadata `row_keys` |Later XRef lookup

|JSON directory
|`"7"` (`str`)
|`7` (`int`)
|Fails -- configured row-key column `7` is missing on the reloaded frame

|XLSX
|`"7"` (`str`)
|`7` (`int`)
|Same failure

|ODS
|`"7"` (`str`)
|`7` (`int`)
|Same failure

|YAML records + YAML metadata
|`7` (`int`)
|`7` (`int`)
|Passes
|===

The metadata *entry* survives every carrier fine (`int`, `bool`, `float`
round-trip through both PyYAML and JSON without loss as ordinary values).
The problem is the *referenced physical column label itself* silently
changes type on three of four maintained carriers, which breaks the
selector's entire purpose (naming a column that must still exist under that
name after reload) independent of whether the metadata copy is admitted into
Trusted Metadata at all. Coercing the metadata `7` to string `"7"` would make
lookup happen to work again on the JSON/XLSX/ODS paths, but would silently
discard the numeric semantic type and collide with a genuine string label
`"7"` on the same frame -- exactly the "no generic coercion" rule already
established for values and keys in census sections 17.1/18.6, which this
FTR does not violate.

=== 8.2 Other non-`str` E1 Scalar categories fare no better

* *Date/DateTime:* native `datetime.date`/`datetime.datetime` round-trip
  through the YAML sidecar, but the XLSX/ODS JSON metadata blob raises
  `TypeError: not JSON serializable` -- not carrier-neutral, independent of
  the physical-header problem in 8.1.
* *NumPy/pandas scalar carrier variants* (`numpy.bool_`, `numpy.int64`,
  `numpy.float64`, `numpy.str_`, `pandas.Timestamp`, `numpy.datetime64`):
  each either raises `PyYAML RepresenterError`, raises JSON `TypeError`, or
  is silently narrowed to a different native type by one carrier and not the
  other (`numpy.float64`/`numpy.str_` narrow under JSON, fail under YAML) --
  none is persistable unchanged across every carrier.
* *`str` itself* is the only category with none of these failure modes: a
  `str` physical column label reloads as the identical `str` value on every
  maintained frame carrier (JSON, YAML, XLSX, ODS), and a `str` metadata
  entry reloads as the identical `str` value on every maintained metadata
  carrier. This is why the durable-reference vocabulary is exact `str`
  specifically, not "the E1 categories that happen to look simple."

=== 8.3 This is a deliberate compatibility revision, not a discovery that the old contract was unsupported

The previously published contract ("physical labels are not required to be
strings; a unique numeric label, for example, is accepted") was real,
intentional, and correctly documented for the DataFrame-column-address
question it was answering (Class A, section 4) -- it was never accidental
tolerance. This FTR does not reclassify that prior contract as unsupported.
It revises, narrowly and for the first time with authority to do so, a
*different, more specific* role that same broad contract was silently also
being asked to serve -- durable selector *reference* identity, a role in
which the label must additionally survive frame-plus-metadata round trips
with type fidelity, which the broad physical-label contract never actually
promised and (section 8.1) cannot deliver for anything but `str`. Retiring
the numeric example specifically for *selector nomination* is therefore a
compatibility break this FTR owns explicitly (section 10), not documentation
drift being corrected after the fact. An ordinary `int`-labeled DataFrame
column remains fully legal wherever it is not nominated as a `row_keys` or
dense-axis selector (Class A, section 7.4).

Census section 20's `str`-only proposal reached the same durable vocabulary
by a different, insufficient route: it inferred `str`-only from an absence
of maintained tests/fixtures for non-`str` selectors (Class C evidence
per its own taxonomy), which the review correctly rejected as unauthorized
because absence of a test does not revoke a published contract. This FTR's
route is the opposite: the contract is real and published, and is being
*deliberately revised* by an artifact with the standing to do so, on
carrier-proof evidence that the revised role cannot be served any other way
-- which is why section 10 requires the atomic documentation update census
section 20 never attempted.
